## Bleeding after sex is worth clear notes

Bleeding after sex happens to some people. A tracker cannot tell you the cause. It can help you describe what happened and decide what to ask.

Pain matters. No pain, entry pain, deep pelvic pain, discharge, pregnancy concern, and repeat bleeding are different notes to bring to care.

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not a substitute for medical advice.

## Cervical Ectropion: The Most Common Cause

Cervical ectropion is one possible cause of contact bleeding. The cervix has two types of surface cells:

- **Squamous cells** on the visible outer portion: tough, flat, and relatively resistant to contact
- **Columnar cells** (glandular epithelium) inside the cervical canal: softer, taller, and much more vascular

In cervical ectropion, columnar cells extend to the outer surface of the cervix. A clinician can see this during an exam. Do not use bleeding pattern alone to diagnose it.

Ectropion is more common:
- In people using combined oral contraceptives (estrogen promotes columnar cell migration)
- During pregnancy (due to hormonal and blood flow changes)
- In younger people, because the squamo-columnar junction (where the two cell types meet) migrates inward with age and hormonal changes

Ectropion is visible to a clinician during a speculum exam.

## Cervical Polyps

A cervical polyp is a small, usually benign growth extending from the inner cervical canal. Polyps are common. Many people have them without knowing, as they cause no pain and are often discovered during a pelvic exam.

Polyps can produce contact bleeding during sex or pelvic exams because they are vascularized and protrude into a position where they receive friction. They can also cause light spotting between periods or slightly heavier discharge than usual.

A clinician can tell you whether a polyp is present and what follow-up is needed.

## Cervicitis From STIs

CDC notes that cervicitis can be asymptomatic. Some people may have abnormal discharge or bleeding between periods, especially after sex.

Symptoms alone cannot tell you if cervicitis or an STI is present.

If bleeding after sex is new, repeated, or paired with discharge, ask what testing makes sense.

## Cervical Dysplasia and Cervical Cancer

Cervical changes are one reason clinicians take repeated bleeding after sex seriously. They are not the most helpful thing to guess about at home.

Screening history can matter. Bring your Pap or HPV test history if you know it.

If bleeding repeats, ask whether you need a pelvic exam, cervical screening, or infection testing.

## Vaginal Causes: When the Bleeding Is Not Cervical

A minority of painless post-coital bleeding originates from the vaginal walls rather than the cervix. Causes include:

- **Vaginal dryness or tissue changes:** these can matter more in perimenopause, after menopause, during breastfeeding, or with some medicines.
- **Vaginal varicosities:** dilated veins in the vaginal wall that can bleed with pressure, more common in pregnancy.
- **Skin conditions:** vulvar lichen sclerosus or similar conditions that affect vaginal tissue integrity.

These causes typically produce bleeding on the vaginal walls rather than spotting from the cervical os, which a pelvic exam can distinguish.

## What to Track

Log each episode of post-coital bleeding:

- **Date** and cycle day
- **Amount:** trace, light, similar to period
- **Color:** pink, red, brownish
- **Duration:** how long bleeding lasted after sex
- **Pain:** none, mild, significant (and where)
- **Discharge:** any accompanying unusual discharge
- **Context you want to share:** contraception, product use, or anything else you choose

## A Note on Privacy for Sexual and Reproductive Health Data

Sex and bleeding notes can be very sensitive. They may include partner context, pregnancy concerns, STI questions, screenshots, exports, and portal messages.

Consumer health apps do not all follow the same privacy rules. HIPAA does not cover every app. Screenshots, backups, exports, support emails, and device access can also create copies.
