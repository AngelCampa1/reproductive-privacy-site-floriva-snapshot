## What Period Discharge Actually Is

Menstrual discharge is not simply blood. It is a combination of blood from uterine blood vessels, shed endometrial tissue (the uterine lining), cervical mucus, and vaginal secretions. The ratio of each component changes across the days of a period, which is part of why the appearance of discharge shifts from day to day.

On heavy flow days, blood predominates and discharge looks and flows like blood. On lighter days, especially at the beginning and end, cervical mucus makes up a larger proportion, producing thinner, pinker, or brownish discharge. Tissue-like material visible in the flow is typically shed endometrial cells.

Understanding what you are seeing across the cycle gives you a baseline. Changes from your personal baseline are the things worth paying attention to.

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not a substitute for medical advice.

## Discharge Day by Day: What to Expect

### Before Your Period (Premenstrual)

In the final days of the luteal phase, progesterone is high and cervical mucus is typically thick, white or off-white, and minimal to moderate in volume. Some people notice a slight increase in discharge in the two or three days before flow begins as the body prepares for menstruation.

### Day 1 of Your Period

The first day of a period often produces lighter, more mixed discharge as menstruation gets started. Some people see brown or dark pink discharge before bright red flow begins. Others jump straight into moderate or heavy red flow. Both are normal.

### Heavy Flow Days (Usually Days 2 to 3)

At peak flow, discharge is primarily blood, typically bright red to dark red, fast-moving, and possibly containing small clots or tissue-like material. This is when pad and tampon changes are most frequent. Odor is mild and metallic.

### Lightening Days (Usually Days 4 to 6, Variable)

As the period tapers, flow slows and discharge begins to mix more with cervical fluid again. Color shifts to darker red, then brown, then sometimes pinkish or rust-colored. Tissue-like material becomes less common as the primary lining has shed.

### Final Days of Period

Brown or dark tan discharge is the norm at the very end of a period. This is highly oxidized blood moving slowly. It can continue for one or two days after you would describe the period as over. Some people refer to these final days as spotting.

### After the Period Ends

Once menstruation is complete, discharge typically becomes minimal and white or clear for a few days. As estrogen rises in the follicular phase, discharge increases in volume and becomes creamier, then more fluid, then progressively clearer and more stretchy as ovulation approaches.

## Discharge Colors and What They Suggest

| Color | Context | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Bright red | Heavy flow days | Normal |
| Dark red | Moderate flow | Normal |
| Brown | Start/end of period | Normal (oxidized blood) |
| Pink | Light flow, start/end | Normal; also ovulation spotting |
| Near-black | Very end of period | Normal (very oxidized) |
| Cream/white | Post-period | Normal (follicular phase) |
| Clear/stretchy | Mid-cycle | Normal (approaching ovulation) |
| Orange | Any time | Investigate, possible infection |
| Gray | Any time | Investigate, possible BV |
| Cottage-cheese texture + itching | Any time | Possible yeast infection |

## Odor: Normal vs. Worth Investigating

Menstrual blood has a mild, metallic odor due to its iron content. This is normal and not a sign of infection. A stronger, fishy, or pungent odor is not a feature of normal menstrual discharge. It can indicate bacterial vaginosis or another infection that deserves medical attention.

Odor perception can be amplified during menstruation because the vaginal environment is temporarily more alkaline than usual, which favors certain bacterial imbalances.

## What to Track

Tracking period discharge is straightforward with a brief daily note:

- **Color**: bright red, dark red, brown, pink, or other
- **Texture**: fluid, thick, tissue-like, or unusual
- **Odor**: normal (mild/metallic) or different
- **Volume**: how much, and does it require a specific level of protection
- **Any itching, burning, or unusual sensation alongside the discharge**
- **Any unusual colors mid-cycle or far from the period**

Even a simple one-word daily log (red, brown, pink, normal) over three cycles creates a useful reference for what is normal for you personally.

## When to Seek Medical Evaluation

- Orange or gray discharge at any point in the cycle
- Strong, fishy, or unpleasant odor
- Thick, white, cottage-cheese-like discharge with itching or vulvar irritation
- Any discharge accompanied by pelvic pain, fever, or significant burning
- Significant tissue-like material passing outside the first days of a heavy period
- Any post-menopausal bleeding or discharge

## A Note on Discharge Logs and Privacy

The daily log of discharge, color, and menstrual patterns you build in a tracking app is detailed health data. It can signal ovulation timing, possible pregnancy, cycle irregularity, and infection. This information is personal, medically relevant, and legally sensitive depending on where you live.

Period tracking data is not protected by HIPAA. In states where abortion is criminalized, this data can be subpoenaed from cloud-based apps.

On-device trackers like Floriva, Drip, and Euki store all your discharge logs and cycle observations solely on your device. No readable central record exists, which means there is nothing that can be compelled, breached, or monetized.
