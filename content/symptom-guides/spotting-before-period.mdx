## When Pre-Period Spotting Is Normal

Spotting that appears one to two days before your period starts and immediately transitions to full menstrual flow is a normal feature of the uterine lining shedding process. As progesterone drops in the late luteal phase, the endometrium begins to break down gradually. For many people, this produces a small amount of brown or pink discharge as the first signal that menstruation is about to begin.

This type of spotting is brief, self-resolving, and consistent with the start of a period. It does not require investigation.

This guide is for educational purposes only. It is not a substitute for medical advice.

## When Pre-Period Spotting Warrants Attention

The pattern changes when spotting starts significantly earlier than the expected period, when it recurs consistently across multiple cycles, or when it is accompanied by other symptoms.

### A Short or Deficient Luteal Phase

The corpus luteum, the structure that forms in the ovary after ovulation, needs to sustain progesterone production for roughly 10 to 16 days to maintain the uterine lining. When the corpus luteum breaks down too early, progesterone drops before the lining is ready to fully shed. This causes spotting in the days before the expected period.

A luteal phase shorter than 10 days, or consistent spotting that begins four or more days before flow, may suggest a luteal phase deficiency. This pattern is more likely to be noticeable for people who are trying to conceive, since an insufficient luteal phase can also affect the ability of a fertilized egg to implant and survive.

### Uterine Polyps and Fibroids

Polyps are soft, benign growths on the uterine lining. Fibroids are benign muscular growths in the uterine wall. Both can cause irregular bleeding and spotting, particularly spotting before periods, because they disrupt the normal architecture of the endometrium or irritate local blood vessels.

Spotting from polyps or fibroids tends to be:
- Consistent across multiple cycles
- Not explained by hormonal factors
- Sometimes accompanied by heavier period flow or mid-cycle bleeding

Diagnosis requires ultrasound and sometimes a saline-infused ultrasound or hysteroscopy for clearer imaging.

### Hormonal Imbalance

Fluctuations in estrogen and progesterone outside the normal range can cause spotting. Thyroid dysfunction in particular affects reproductive hormone regulation and can produce irregular bleeding patterns including pre-period spotting. Elevated prolactin levels from a benign pituitary growth or other causes can also disrupt the luteal phase.

If pre-period spotting is accompanied by other symptoms such as unexpected weight changes, temperature sensitivity, hair changes, or breast discharge, a broader hormonal evaluation may be indicated.

### Implantation Bleeding

When conception has occurred, implantation of the embryo into the uterine lining can cause brief, light spotting. This happens 6 to 12 days after fertilization and can fall in the same window as pre-period spotting. Implantation bleeding is typically lighter, often just traces or a pantyliner amount, pink or brown in color, and lasts one to three days without becoming heavier.

If spotting does not progress to a full period within a few days, a pregnancy test is a reasonable next step.

### Endometriosis and Adenomyosis

Both endometriosis, which is tissue similar to the uterine lining outside the uterus, and adenomyosis, which is uterine lining tissue embedded in the muscular uterine wall, can cause abnormal bleeding including spotting before periods. These conditions are typically accompanied by other symptoms such as painful periods, pelvic pain throughout the cycle, and painful sex. These provide additional diagnostic context.

## What Spotting Looks Like vs. What You Might Confuse It With

Spotting is:
- Small in volume, a few drops to light staining, not requiring a pad or tampon
- Often brown or pink rather than bright red
- Not increasing in flow over hours the way a period does

It can be confused with:
- The very beginning of a period, which you can distinguish by whether it progresses
- Discharge mixed with a small amount of blood
- Irritation from sex, which produces cervical bleeding

## What to Log

For each spotting episode:
- Date and cycle day, count from last period start
- Color, whether brown, pink, or red
- Amount, whether trace, pantyliner, or light
- Whether it progressed to a period, and how quickly
- Any associated symptoms such as cramping, bloating, or discharge changes

Track this across at least two full cycles before drawing conclusions. The pattern, consistent timing, consistent amount, whether it precedes full flow, tells a more useful story than a single event.

## The Privacy Consideration

Spotting logs, cycle tracking data, and notes about possible pregnancy-related bleeding are reproductive health records. In states with abortion restrictions, law enforcement has sought this type of data. Consumer period tracking apps are not covered by HIPAA and have handed data to third parties. Flo Health's 2021 FTC settlement and $59.5 million class action settlement make this concrete, not theoretical.

An on-device tracker stores your data on your device alone. The company has no copy, which means there is no data to subpoena, sell, or expose in a breach. When spotting data intersects with pregnancy-related concerns, the storage location of your tracking data is a meaningful privacy decision.
