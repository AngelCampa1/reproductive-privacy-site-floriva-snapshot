## What Is Happening in Your Cycle a Week Before Your Period?

One week before your period, you are typically in the late luteal phase. Ovulation has already occurred, progesterone levels are at or near their peak, and the body is either sustaining a pregnancy or preparing to shed the uterine lining. It is a hormonally active window, which is why spotting in this phase has several plausible explanations.

Understanding the cause requires knowing your cycle. How long is your typical cycle? Do you know roughly when you ovulate? Was this cycle different from your usual pattern? The answers narrow down the likely source.

This guide is for educational purposes only. It is not a substitute for medical advice.

## Common Causes of Spotting a Week Before a Period

### Progesterone Decline

Progesterone rises after ovulation to maintain the uterine lining. When progesterone drops at the end of the luteal phase, it triggers menstruation. If progesterone drops earlier than expected, or the luteal phase is short, the lining can begin to shed before the full period begins. This causes spotting a few days to a week before the main flow.

This pattern often looks like: a day or two of spotting, a day of near-nothing, then the full period arriving on or slightly before schedule. If this is consistent cycle to cycle, it may be worth discussing with a provider who can evaluate progesterone levels in the luteal phase.

### Implantation Bleeding

If conception occurred this cycle, implantation typically happens 6 to 12 days after ovulation. In a standard cycle with mid-cycle ovulation, this places implantation about a week before the next expected period.

Implantation bleeding is characteristically light, often just trace spotting, brief lasting hours to two days, and pink or brown in color. Not everyone experiences it. It cannot be distinguished from other spotting causes without a pregnancy test.

### Ovulation Spotting That Arrived Late

Ovulation spotting typically occurs mid-cycle, around the LH surge. But if ovulation was delayed this cycle, which stress, illness, or other factors can cause, ovulation spotting might appear later than expected. This places it closer to the premenstrual window.

### Cervical Causes

The cervix produces more mucus and is more vascular in the luteal phase. Minor cervical contact from sex, a pelvic exam, or vigorous activity can cause light spotting that, if it occurs a week before the period, may seem premenstrual in timing.

### Uterine Polyps or Fibroids

Small uterine polyps or submucosal fibroids can cause spotting at various points in the cycle, including before menstruation. This is a less common cause but worth knowing about if premenstrual spotting is a new, consistent, or heavy pattern.

## What to Track

Tracking premenstrual spotting precisely gives you and your healthcare provider useful patterns:

- **Cycle day of spotting onset**, count from your last period start date
- **Color**, pink, brown, or red
- **Volume**, trace only, or does it require protection
- **Duration**, how many days before the main flow begins
- **Whether the main period follows on schedule**, or is the spotting shifting your period timing
- **Associated symptoms**, cramping, breast tenderness, mid-cycle pain, unusual discharge
- **Contraceptive use**, type and how long you have been using it

Three to six months of this data makes patterns visible that would otherwise be missed.

## When to See a Healthcare Provider

Consider a provider visit if:

- Premenstrual spotting is new and persists across multiple cycles
- The spotting is heavier than a trace amount or requires a pad
- It is accompanied by pelvic pain, unusual discharge, or pain during sex
- You are concerned about pregnancy and the spotting has not been followed by a clear period
- You have a history of endometriosis, fibroids, or polycystic ovary syndrome and the pattern has changed

## A Note on Tracking This Data Privately

Premenstrual spotting logs, especially when they occur around potential implantation timing, can carry reproductive health implications beyond a simple physical health record. In states where abortion is criminalized, data suggesting a possible pregnancy, even unconfirmed, could theoretically be relevant if it were accessible to outside parties.

Flo Health was subject to an FTC enforcement action in 2021 for sharing user period data with Facebook and Google. A $59.5 million class action settlement followed in 2025. Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA and can be subpoenaed from cloud-based apps.

On-device period trackers like Floriva, Drip, and Euki store your cycle and spotting data only on your device. There is no readable central record that can be compelled, breached, or sold.
