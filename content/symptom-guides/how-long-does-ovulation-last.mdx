The confusion around "how long ovulation lasts" comes from conflating three different timeframes: the act of ovulation itself, the egg's viability window, and the fertile window. All three are different, and all three matter for understanding fertility and cycle tracking.

## Three Different Timeframes

### The Act of Ovulation: Minutes

The rupture of the dominant follicle and release of the egg is not a sustained process. It happens in minutes. The LH surge triggers a cascade of enzyme activity that breaks down the follicle wall, and the egg is released surrounded by its cumulus cells (called the cumulus-oocyte complex). This whole process takes approximately 15-30 minutes in humans.

This is why "ovulation" is not something you can feel clearly happening. It is too brief. The discomfort some people experience (mittelschmerz) is the aftermath: follicular fluid released into the peritoneal cavity after rupture, not the rupture itself.

### Egg Viability: 12-24 Hours

After release, the egg begins traveling down the fallopian tube toward the uterus. It remains viable for fertilization for approximately 12-24 hours. This is the outer limit most cited in reproductive medicine literature. Some sources cite up to 24 hours; more conservative estimates use 12-18 hours.

After this window, the egg degenerates and fertilization is no longer possible.

This is the critical constraint on the fertile window's post-ovulatory side: you have at most 24 hours after ovulation during which intercourse can result in conception, and realistically less than that.

### The Fertile Window: 5-6 Days

The "fertile window" is longer than egg viability because sperm can survive for several days:

- Sperm deposited in the uterine cavity survive 3-5 days in fertile-quality cervical mucus
- Some research suggests up to 5 days of survival is possible with ideal conditions (peak-quality egg-white mucus)
- This means intercourse 5 days before ovulation can still result in fertilization

The combined window: 5 days before ovulation (sperm can survive to reach the egg) plus ovulation day = 5-6 days of potential fertility per cycle.

Post-ovulation fertility is essentially zero after 24 hours, and realistically negligible after 12-18 hours.

## When Does the Fertile Window Open and Close

This depends entirely on when you ovulate, which varies between people and between cycles for the same person.

**The "Day 14" assumption:** The textbook 28-day cycle with Day 14 ovulation is a population average. Real ovulation timing varies significantly:
- Cycles of 24-35 days can all be physiologically normal
- In a 24-day cycle, ovulation may occur around Day 10
- In a 35-day cycle, ovulation may occur around Day 21
- The luteal phase (post-ovulation) is relatively consistent at 10-16 days; it is the follicular phase length that varies

**Why calendar methods fail:** Calendar prediction uses past cycle lengths to estimate future fertile windows. Ovulation timing is not fixed to a specific day. It varies cycle to cycle based on stress, illness, travel, disrupted sleep, and other factors. A single stressful month can delay ovulation by a week.

**Tracking instead of predicting:** LH test strips confirm when the surge is happening. Cervical mucus shows when fertile-quality conditions are present. BBT confirms when ovulation has occurred. These real-time observations are more reliable than calendar predictions.

## The Most Fertile Days

Research on conception timing has consistently found that the highest conception rates come from intercourse in the 2-3 days ending on ovulation day. The probability of conception is highest on the day before ovulation and ovulation day itself. Intercourse 5 days before ovulation can still lead to conception, but the probability per act is lower.

The practical implication: intercourse every 2 days throughout the fertile window (identified by LH strips and cervical mucus) produces approximately the same conception probability as daily intercourse during the window, with less physical and psychological pressure.

## How to Confirm the Window Has Closed

After ovulation, progesterone from the corpus luteum causes:

1. **BBT rise:** Temperature elevation of 0.2°C or more above follicular baseline. After 3 consecutive elevated temperatures, ovulation is confirmed and the fertile window is closed.

2. **Cervical mucus change:** Abrupt return to sticky or dry mucus, the progesterone "plug" forming at the cervix. This typically happens within 24 hours of ovulation.

In the Billings Ovulation Method, the fertile window is considered closed at the end of the 3rd day of consistently dry or sticky mucus after the peak day.

## What Counts as the "Ovulation Window"

People often search for "how long does the ovulation window last" meaning "how long do I have to try to conceive." The answer:

- **Intercourse before ovulation:** Counts if sperm can survive to reach the egg. Start 5 days before estimated ovulation day.
- **Intercourse at or after ovulation:** Effective only on ovulation day and within approximately 12-18 hours afterward. After that, the egg is gone.

For conception, the days before ovulation matter more than the day of ovulation, because sperm need to be present before the egg is released. Timing intercourse on ovulation day alone misses most of the fertile window.

## What This Means for Floriva Users

Tracking the fertile window accurately requires knowing when ovulation actually occurs in your individual cycle, not when a calendar algorithm estimates it might. Logging LH surge data and BBT shows you the real pattern over 2-3 cycles. Once you know your typical follicular phase length, your fertile window becomes predictable within a few days, much more accurate than "Day 14."
