Right-sided lower abdominal pain at mid-cycle is usually mittelschmerz, ovulation pain from a follicle releasing on the right ovary. It can also be appendicitis, an ovarian cyst, or other pathology. Knowing the differences is important because one of those possibilities is a surgical emergency.

## Mittelschmerz: What Normal Ovulation Pain Looks Like

Mittelschmerz occurs in a portion of people with regular ovulatory cycles. The mechanism: when the dominant follicle ruptures, it releases follicular fluid and sometimes a small amount of blood into the peritoneal cavity. This can irritate the peritoneum (the membrane lining the abdominal cavity), producing pain.

**Characteristic features:**
- **Location:** One side of the lower abdomen, whichever ovary released the egg that month. Typically switches sides from cycle to cycle (occasionally the same ovary releases two cycles in a row).
- **Quality:** Dull ache, cramping, or a sharper twinge. Some people describe it as a pop.
- **Duration:** Minutes to a few hours. Occasionally one to two days, but this is on the long end.
- **Severity:** Mild to moderate. You can go about your day.
- **Associated symptoms:** None beyond the pain itself. No fever, no nausea, no change in bowel habits, no loss of appetite.
- **Cycle timing:** Mid-cycle, typically days 12 to 16 in a 28-day cycle (later in longer cycles).
- **Concurrent signs:** Often occurs with or just before egg-white cervical mucus and LH surge.

The pain from follicle rupture typically peaks briefly then fades as the fluid disperses and the peritoneal irritation resolves.

## Appendicitis: What Requires Immediate Attention

Appendicitis is inflammation of the appendix, a small structure attached to the large intestine in the right lower quadrant. It can become life-threatening if it ruptures.

**Characteristic features:**
- **Location:** Right lower quadrant only. Never left-sided.
- **Progression:** Worsens continuously over hours. Does not improve on its own.
- **Starting location:** Pain often begins around the navel (periumbilical), then migrates to the right lower quadrant over 4 to 12 hours.
- **Severity:** Progressively severe. Becomes difficult to move or walk as it progresses.
- **Associated symptoms:** Nausea and vomiting (in most cases), loss of appetite, low-grade fever early (higher if perforated), sometimes diarrhea.
- **Cycle timing:** Not related to the menstrual cycle. Can occur at any point in the cycle, in any month, at any age.
- **Movement sensitivity:** Appendicitis pain typically worsens with movement, particularly jarring (bumps in a car, jumping).

**Rebound tenderness:** If pressing firmly on the right lower abdomen and then quickly releasing causes a sharp spike in pain when you release (not when you press), this is rebound tenderness. It is a clinical sign of peritoneal inflammation consistent with appendicitis. This requires immediate emergency evaluation.

## When It's Neither

Right or left lower abdominal pain at mid-cycle that does not fit typical mittelschmerz may have other causes:

**Ovarian cyst rupture:** A cyst on the ovary can rupture at ovulation, producing sudden, sharp, severe pain. Usually one-sided. If pain is sudden onset, severe, or accompanied by rapid heart rate or lightheadedness, seek emergency evaluation. A large cyst rupture can cause significant internal bleeding. Use the [ovarian cyst urgent-care notes](/free/ovarian-cyst-tracking-kit) after care is underway, not to decide whether to wait.

**Hemorrhagic corpus luteum:** The corpus luteum (the structure left after ovulation) occasionally bleeds internally, causing sharp one-sided pain in the days after ovulation rather than at ovulation itself.

**Endometriosis:** If ovulation pain is severe, lasting, and gets worse each cycle, this warrants investigation for endometriosis, particularly ovarian endometriomas (cysts on the ovary filled with old blood).

**Ovarian torsion:** An ovary that twists on its ligaments produces sudden, severe, one-sided pain, often with nausea. This is a surgical emergency requiring immediate evaluation.

**Ectopic pregnancy:** One-sided lower abdominal pain in the early weeks after a potential conception, with a positive pregnancy test, requires immediate evaluation to rule out ectopic pregnancy.

## Decision Guide

**Seek emergency care immediately if:**
- Right-sided pain worsening over hours
- Any combination of severe pain plus fever plus vomiting
- Sudden onset severe pain (either side), possible ovarian torsion or ruptured cyst
- Pain with lightheadedness, rapid pulse, or fainting, possible internal bleeding
- Rebound tenderness on examination

**Monitor and follow up if:**
- Mid-cycle, one-sided, brief, mild pain with no systemic symptoms, typical mittelschmerz
- But add to your cycle tracking data and note if it worsens over cycles

**Seek non-emergency evaluation if:**
- Ovulation pain is severe (requiring stronger pain medication)
- Duration exceeds two days
- Pattern is worsening over cycles
- Pain at mid-cycle is accompanied by deep pelvic pressure or pain with sex
