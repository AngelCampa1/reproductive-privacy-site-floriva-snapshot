## Discharge Is a Normal Cervical Function

The vaginal environment maintains itself through a continuous process of fluid production from the cervix and vaginal walls. This discharge removes dead cells, maintains pH balance, and, depending on cycle phase, either helps or hinders sperm passage. Some discharge, of varying appearance, throughout the cycle is normal. It is not a problem to solve.

What changes across the menstrual cycle is the character of the discharge. Shifting levels of estrogen and progesterone drive those changes.

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not a substitute for medical advice.

## How Discharge Changes Across the Cycle

### After Your Period Ends: Dry or Sticky Phase

In the days right after menstruation, many people notice little to no discharge, or a discharge that is dry and sticky. Estrogen is low in the early follicular phase, and the cervix produces minimal mucus.

### Approaching Ovulation: Clear and Stretchy

As ovulation approaches, estrogen rises sharply. The cervix produces more mucus that becomes clearer and more elastic. This is the classic "egg white cervical mucus" described in fertility tracking. It stretches between two fingers without breaking, sometimes several centimeters. Its job is to help sperm travel toward the egg.

Peak egg white mucus typically appears one to two days before ovulation.

### After Ovulation: Thick and White (The Luteal Phase)

After ovulation, progesterone takes over. The cervix closes and produces a thick mucus plug that blocks sperm entry. This is the body's way of preventing further fertilization attempts. Discharge during the luteal phase is typically:

- White or off-white
- Thick or pasty in consistency (sometimes described as lotion-like)
- Produced in moderate amounts
- Without strong odor

This is what most people are noticing when they observe "discharge before period." It is normal luteal phase mucus. It is the expected character of discharge in the 10 to 14 days between ovulation and menstruation.

### The Day or Two Before Your Period

As progesterone and estrogen drop in the late luteal phase, discharge may become slightly more watery again. Some people notice light pinkish or brownish tinting as small amounts of blood mix with mucus before full menstrual flow begins. This spotting-mixed-with-discharge is a normal pre-period transition.

## What Normal Pre-Period Discharge Looks Like

Normal discharge in the days before your period:
- Color: white, off-white, clear, or with a very slight yellowish tint when dry on underwear
- Consistency: thicker than mid-cycle, ranging from paste-like to lotion-like
- Odor: none or very mild; the natural smell of vaginal discharge is not unpleasant when it is healthy
- Sensation: should not cause itching, burning, or irritation

## Discharge Patterns That Warrant a Clinician Visit

### Yellow or Green Discharge

Yellow or green discharge, particularly when combined with odor, unusual texture, or irritation, is associated with sexually transmitted infections including gonorrhea, chlamydia, and trichomoniasis. A small amount of yellowish discharge on underwear that has dried is sometimes normal, but fresh yellow-green discharge with any symptoms is worth evaluating.

### Gray Discharge With Fishy Odor

This pattern strongly suggests bacterial vaginosis (BV), an overgrowth of anaerobic bacteria that disrupts normal vaginal flora. BV is not sexually transmitted but is associated with sexual activity. The fishy odor often becomes more noticeable after sex or at the end of the menstrual period. BV is treated with antibiotics and typically clears quickly with treatment.

### Thick, White, Clumpy Discharge With Itching

The combination of cottage-cheese-like white discharge and significant vulvar itching is characteristic of a vaginal yeast infection (candidiasis). This can worsen in the late luteal phase. Antifungal treatments are available over the counter in many countries, but recurrent yeast infections (four or more per year) warrant evaluation for underlying causes.

### Discharge Accompanied by Pelvic Pain or Fever

If unusual discharge comes with pelvic pain, fever, or pain during sex, pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) is possible and requires prompt medical evaluation.

## What to Track

If you want to use discharge observations to understand your cycle better, log the following each day:

- Texture/consistency (dry, sticky, creamy, watery, egg-white/stretchy)
- Color (clear, white, yellow, brown, pink-tinged)
- Amount (none, trace, moderate, significant)
- Any odor or irritation

Tracking this across two to three full cycles helps identify your personal pattern and makes deviations from it easier to notice.

## The Privacy Angle

Cervical mucus tracking, fertility window data, and discharge observations are reproductive health information. For people navigating fertility decisions, contraception, or pregnancy concerns, this data is particularly sensitive. Apps that store this information on servers can hand it over to third parties through subpoenas, data broker sales, or breaches. Period tracking data has no HIPAA protection.

Flo Health's FTC settlement and the $59.5 million class action that followed were direct consequences of sharing reproductive health data without users' real understanding or consent. On-device tracking apps store your cycle data on your device only, making server-side disclosure impossible. For data this personal, the architecture of the app is part of the privacy decision.
