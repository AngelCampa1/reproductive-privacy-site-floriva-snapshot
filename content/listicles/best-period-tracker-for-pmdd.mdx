PMDD diagnosis under DSM-5 criteria requires more than describing symptoms to a doctor.

It requires prospective documentation: daily symptom ratings across at least two complete menstrual cycles. That documentation must show the characteristic pattern. Symptoms are present in the luteal phase (typically the week before menstruation), absent or minimal in the follicular phase, and resolve within a few days of menstruation onset.

"Prospective" is the key word. Retrospective recall, trying to remember how you felt two weeks ago, is not sufficient for diagnosis. Clinicians familiar with PMDD know this. The app you use must support consistent, dated, daily logging for the documentation to carry diagnostic weight.

Of the four apps evaluated here, Floriva and Bearable are strongest for this purpose. Floriva provides purpose-built menstrual health tracking with detailed daily logging and on-device privacy protections that matter when you are documenting psychological symptoms. Bearable allows fully customized PMDD-specific symptom rating that can align with validated instruments, such as the DRSP (Daily Record of Severity of Problems), that clinicians may use for formal PMDD evaluation.

Clue is a legitimate option with an evidence-based approach and strong GDPR privacy protections. Flo's data-sharing enforcement history makes it a poor choice for this use case. PMDD symptom logs capture sensitive psychological and emotional health data, and Flo has a documented history of sharing health data with advertising platforms.

For anyone actively pursuing a PMDD diagnosis, the most useful combination is a dedicated period tracker (Floriva or Clue) for cycle phase confirmation alongside Bearable for structured daily symptom documentation. Alternatively, Floriva alone works well if you configure detailed daily symptom logging from the start. Bringing two full cycles of consistent, dated logs to a clinician makes the evaluation substantially more efficient.
