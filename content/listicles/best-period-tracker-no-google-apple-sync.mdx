Apple Health and Google Health Connect serve a real purpose. They aggregate health data from multiple apps into a single place, making it easier to share a consolidated health picture with doctors or track changes over time. For many types of health data, this is a straightforward tradeoff.

Menstrual cycle data sits in different legal and political territory. In states where abortion access is restricted or prosecuted, menstrual cycle data has potential use as evidence in legal proceedings. The data consolidated in Apple Health and Google Health Connect is subject to subpoena and law enforcement requests governed by US law, which provides fewer protections for health data than many users assume.

The apps in this list take the position that menstrual cycle data should not flow into platform health aggregators by default. That is either a deliberate design decision (Floriva, Drip, Euki) or a consequence of no integration existing (Read Your Body).

The distinction between "no health platform sync" and "fully offline" matters. Drip and Floriva both make zero network connections. Neither one syncs to anything. Euki is on-device only. Read Your Body offers user-controlled cloud backup that is separate from health data stores.

For most users, Floriva is the best recommendation because it keeps period data away from Apple and Google while still supporting a more complete daily tracker, and its encrypted backup file gives you a real way to move data to a new phone without routing through Apple or Google. Drip and Euki are good options if you use one phone and want the narrowest possible local-only setup.
