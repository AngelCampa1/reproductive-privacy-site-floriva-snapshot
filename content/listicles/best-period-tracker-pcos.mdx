## PCOS Tracking and the Data Accumulation Problem

PCOS users typically track for years, not months. Irregular cycles, hormone symptoms, BBT patterns, and ovulation timing create a detailed long-term health record. That record is medically useful. It is also exactly the kind of sensitive data that advertisers and [data brokers](/resources/guides/data-brokers-reproductive-health/) want.

The FTC enforcement action against [Flo](/compare/alternatives/flo-app-alternative/) in 2021 was specifically about sharing reproductive health data with Facebook and Google. For a PCOS user who has tracked with Flo for three years, that is three years of detailed symptom logs, cycle irregularities, and health patterns stored in a system with a documented data-sharing history. That is the same pattern that produced the [$59.5M settlement](/resources/guides/flo-period-tracker-lawsuit-settlement/).

This does not mean Flo is unusable. It means the feature-versus-privacy tradeoff is more consequential for PCOS users than for someone with regular cycles doing basic tracking. [Natural Cycles](/compare/alternatives/natural-cycles-alternative-privacy/) and [Euki](/compare/alternatives/euki-app-alternative/) address parts of that tradeoff in different ways.

## What PCOS Users Actually Need From a Tracker

The core requirements for useful PCOS tracking are: symptom logging beyond just period dates (skin, hair, weight, energy, pain), support for cycles that do not follow a predictable length, some form of ovulation detection (BBT or symptom-based), and a way to export data for doctor appointments. How each app addresses these requirements, and what it does with the resulting data, determines where it ranks.

## Bottom Line

Floriva is the top pick for PCOS tracking with private long-term records. Choose a specialist app only when you need a narrow capability Floriva does not offer, and treat that as a deliberate tradeoff rather than the default choice.
