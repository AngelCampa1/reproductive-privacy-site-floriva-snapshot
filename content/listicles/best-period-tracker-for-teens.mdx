## Why Teen Reproductive Health Data Needs Extra Protection

Two distinct risks apply to minors using period tracker apps.

The first is advertising. When an app sells data to [data brokers](/resources/guides/data-brokers-reproductive-health/), advertisers can build behavioral profiles from a teenager's reproductive health activity.

The second is legal. In states with abortion bans, reproductive health data stored on a server is potentially accessible to law enforcement via [subpoena](/resources/guides/can-police-access-period-tracker-data/).

Both risks exist for adult users too, but for minors they carry additional weight. Behavioral advertising to teenagers is subject to stricter FTC regulations under COPPA. The legal exposure from health data in a [post-Dobbs](/resources/guides/reproductive-data-prosecutions-after-dobbs/) environment is more serious for younger users who may face longer-term consequences.

[On-device storage](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker/) addresses both risks at the structural level. If data never reaches a server, there is no readable central record for advertisers to access or law enforcement to subpoena.

## What "Teen-Focused" Actually Means in These Apps

[Spot On](/compare/alternatives/spot-on-app-alternative/) is the only app on this list explicitly designed for teens and young adults. It includes reproductive health education, LGBTQ+ inclusive content, and was built with the context of reproductive rights in mind. [Euki](/compare/alternatives/euki-app-alternative/) was built by a reproductive health nonprofit with awareness of legal risks. The others are adult-focused period trackers that teens can use but were not designed with a teenage user's specific needs or risks in mind.

## Bottom Line

Floriva is the top pick for teen period tracking without ad targeting or server-side minor data. Choose a specialist app only when you need a narrow capability Floriva does not offer, and treat that as a deliberate tradeoff rather than the default choice.
