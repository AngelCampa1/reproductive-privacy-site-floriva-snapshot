## What Heavy Period Tracking Actually Needs

Heavy menstrual bleeding is a clinical symptom. It is clinically significant, potentially treatable, and worth documenting carefully. The apps useful for this purpose need to do specific things that basic period trackers do not always support.

**Flow intensity with granularity.** "Heavy" as a single category is not enough. Clinical documentation needs to distinguish between heavy (soaking a pad in 2-3 hours) and very heavy (soaking through in under an hour) and flooding. Apps that offer spotting, light, medium, heavy, and very heavy as distinct categories produce more useful clinical records.

**Clot logging.** Passing clots is a specific symptom worth tracking. The size and frequency of clots matters. Occasional small clots are common, but large or frequent clots are clinically relevant. Not all apps have clot logging fields.

**Associated symptoms.** Heavy bleeding can cause fatigue, dizziness, and shortness of breath from blood loss. Logging these alongside flow data creates a more complete clinical picture.

**Export.** A gynecologist needs to see this data in a usable format. Screenshots are cumbersome. A formatted cycle summary covering several months is more useful.

## Heavy Bleeding Data is Health Data

Detailed flow logs, including intensity, clot presence, duration, and associated symptoms, describe your health status with specificity. This is [health data not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa/). Consumer app data is regulated differently than clinical records.

The [FTC enforcement action against Flo](/compare/alternatives/flo-app-alternative/) in 2021 established that period app companies have shared this category of health data with advertising platforms. For someone building a detailed heavy bleeding record over months or years, the storage architecture of their chosen app matters.

## Building a Clinical Record for Your Gynecologist

Start logging at least three cycles before an appointment where you plan to discuss heavy bleeding. Log consistently, not just on the worst days. Include: flow intensity every day, any clots (note size if possible), period start and end dates, and any associated symptoms.

See the [guide on heavy period bleeding causes](/resources/symptom-guides/heavy-period-bleeding-causes/) for more on what these logs can help identify, and the [gynecologist appointment prep guide](/app-guides/floriva-for-gynecologist-prep/) for how to structure your records.
