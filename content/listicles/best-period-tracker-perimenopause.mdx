## Why Perimenopause Breaks Standard Period Trackers

Most period tracker apps are built around one core assumption: cycles are roughly regular and predictable. You log a period, the app predicts the next one. That model works reasonably well for users with consistent cycles.

Perimenopause breaks that assumption. Cycles may stretch to 45 days, then shorten to 21. Periods may disappear entirely, then return heavily. Flow may lighten or intensify without warning. Symptoms unrelated to menstruation, such as hot flashes, night sweats, and sleep disruption, become just as important to track as cycle dates.

Apps that average your historical cycle length and project forward become less reliable as perimenopause progresses. What you need is an app that records what you log without making assumptions about what comes next.

## The Long-Horizon Problem

Perimenopause also creates a data accumulation problem. The longer the tracking period, the more detailed and sensitive the health record becomes. A four-year perimenopause record logs not just cycle irregularity but symptom severity over time, treatment responses, and the full arc of a significant hormonal transition.

If that record is on a cloud server, particularly one with a documented history of sharing reproductive health data with advertisers, as in the Flo FTC case and the [$59.5M settlement](/resources/guides/flo-period-tracker-lawsuit-settlement/), it is accessible in ways you may not have consented to when you started tracking. [On-device storage](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker/) keeps that record in your control for the full duration of the transition.

## Bottom Line

Choose the features that fit your notes. Check where the app saves them.
