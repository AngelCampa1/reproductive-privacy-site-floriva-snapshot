## Why FAM Data Deserves Careful Handling

Fertility awareness tracking is different from basic period logging. Over months and years, it generates a detailed picture of your body: when you ovulate, how your temperature rises after ovulation, what your cervical mucus looks like at different points in your cycle, whether you're trying to conceive or avoid pregnancy.

That data is medically useful. It is also the kind of detailed reproductive health information that became newly sensitive after reproductive rights restrictions expanded across multiple states. Period tracker data is [not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa/). It does not have the same legal protections as data held by a hospital or clinic.

The [FTC enforcement action against Flo](/compare/alternatives/flo-app-alternative/) in 2021 was specifically about sharing reproductive health data with Facebook and Google. A $59.5M class action settlement followed in September 2025. FAM users who have tracked with Flo for several years have accumulated exactly the kind of detailed fertility history that was at issue in that enforcement action.

## What FAM Tracking Actually Requires

Useful fertility awareness tracking needs: BBT logging (either manual or passive via a wearable), cervical mucus observation fields, ovulation prediction or confirmation, and enough cycle history to identify patterns. The apps on this list cover these requirements in different ways, with different tradeoffs between feature depth and data architecture.

Temperature-based apps like Natural Cycles confirm ovulation after the fact using the thermal shift. Symptom-based apps like Clue and Floriva use multiple logged observations to estimate the fertile window. Both approaches work, the difference is accuracy for irregular cycles, where temperature confirmation is generally more reliable than calendar-based prediction.

## The Privacy Architecture Question

For FAM tracking specifically, the storage question matters more than it does for someone logging only period start dates. BBT charts, mucus observations, and intercourse timing create a detailed health record. If that record lives on a server, it is subject to whatever legal process or security incident might affect that server.

[On-device storage](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker/) means the data lives only on your phone. The app company cannot receive a subpoena for data they don't have. A breach of their servers doesn't expose your fertility history. The tradeoff is that you lose the data if you lose the phone without a local backup, and on-device apps can't offer the large-dataset prediction accuracy that server-based apps build over millions of users.

For FAM users doing serious fertility work, this is a genuine tradeoff worth thinking through rather than ignoring.

## Bottom Line

Floriva is the recommended winner for fertility awareness without data selling. Pick a specialist app only when you need a narrow capability Floriva does not claim, and treat that as a deliberate tradeoff rather than the default choice.
