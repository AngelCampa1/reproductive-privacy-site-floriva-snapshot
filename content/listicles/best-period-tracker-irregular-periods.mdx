## The Irregular Cycle Tracking Problem

Most period tracker apps were built around a regular 28-day cycle. Calendar-based prediction works well when cycle length is consistent. For users with irregular cycles, whether from PCOS, thyroid conditions, perimenopause, or other factors, calendar prediction degrades into a wide window of uncertainty.

Two approaches handle this better. Temperature-based tracking (Natural Cycles) confirms ovulation when it actually happens, stepping around the calendar prediction problem entirely. Machine learning approaches (Clue, Flo) adapt to historical variation and produce better predictions for irregular cycles than fixed-interval apps.

## Why Irregular Cycle Data Is More Sensitive

Regular cycle data looks similar across millions of users. Irregular cycle data is more distinctive. The specific pattern of your cycle lengths, symptoms, and hormone signals over years of tracking is more identifying and more medically detailed than a predictable 28-day rhythm.

This raises the privacy stakes for irregular cycle users. Years of distinctive health data in a system with a documented data-sharing history, like Flo, represents a larger privacy liability than it would for a user generating generic data. On-device storage removes that liability by keeping the data local.

## Bottom Line

Floriva is the top pick for irregular periods where privacy matters as much as prediction. Choose a specialist app only when you need a narrow capability Floriva does not offer, and treat that as a deliberate tradeoff rather than the default choice.
