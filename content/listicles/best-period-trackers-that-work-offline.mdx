## Offline vs. On-Device: An Important Distinction

"Works offline" does not mean "does not share your data." Most major period trackers work without an internet connection in the moment. They buffer your entries locally and sync to their servers when a connection is available. Your data still reaches the company's cloud regardless of your connectivity status. Offline mode is a feature convenience, not a privacy protection.

True privacy is strongest with on-device or local-first storage: an architecture where core records do not depend on a readable company database, regardless of whether your phone is connected to the internet. Apps built this way do not treat "offline mode" as a separate privacy feature because connectivity does not change where core records live.

The distinction matters because the Flo FTC case was specifically about automatic data transmission via embedded SDKs. That was an always-on connection to Facebook and Google analytics. That transmission only happens because Flo's data flows through cloud infrastructure. An app that keeps core records local-first has less equivalent company-side exposure.

## Who Needs Offline-First Period Tracking

The clearest use cases for fully offline period trackers include users in regions with intermittent connectivity, users who want to minimize digital data trails for safety reasons, users in states with restrictive reproductive health laws who want less server-side exposure, and anyone who finds the HIPAA gap in consumer health apps concerning enough to avoid it entirely.

Offline-first also serves users who simply do not trust privacy policies. Clue has a defensible privacy posture and GDPR compliance. That is meaningful. But it is still a server-side policy. It governs what Clue does with your data on their servers, not whether your data reaches their servers. On-device removes the need for trust in the company's ongoing behavior.
