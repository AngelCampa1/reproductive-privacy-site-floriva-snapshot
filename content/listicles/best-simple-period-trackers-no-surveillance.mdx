## How We Evaluated

We prioritized simplicity: how fast can you go from installing the app to having a working tracker? We disqualified any app that requires more than 2 minutes of setup or demands personal information before you can start tracking. Then we checked privacy: on-device storage, no accounts, no third-party analytics code.

The target user here is someone who has been tracking on paper (or not tracking at all) and wants the prediction benefits of digital without the data collection overhead of mainstream apps. Feature richness was scored negatively when extra features added complexity without improving core tracking.

## The Simplicity Problem With Most Period Trackers

Most period trackers are designed to maximize engagement, not minimize friction. Flo greets you with account creation, email verification, health profile questions, notification permissions, and a premium upsell before you can log a single period. Clue asks for your birth date, cycle history, and creates a server-side account.

This design serves the company, not the user who just wants to know when her next period starts. If you have been using paper because digital felt like too much, these apps confirm your instinct.

## What Minimal Actually Means

A genuinely minimal period tracker should work like a calculator. You open it, input data, get a result. No login screen, no onboarding flow, no daily notifications asking you to log your mood. The five apps in this comparison represent the simplest options available, ranked by how close they get to that calculator experience.

Floriva gets closest because we built it specifically for this use case. We asked ourselves: what is the minimum viable period tracker for someone who values simplicity and privacy above all else? The answer was start-date tracking with automatic predictions and nothing else required.
