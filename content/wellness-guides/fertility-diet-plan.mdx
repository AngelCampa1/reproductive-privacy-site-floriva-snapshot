The fertility supplement market generates enormous revenue from people willing to try almost anything during what is often a frightening and uncertain time. Most of it is marketing. The subset of dietary and supplemental interventions with legitimate clinical evidence is smaller than the industry suggests. But it is real and worth knowing.

Here is what the evidence supports, ranked by the strength of evidence behind it.

## Foundation: Mediterranean Diet Pattern

This is the single most evidence-backed dietary recommendation for fertility. Not a specific food, not a supplement. A broad eating pattern.

Research supports it: a cohort study of couples undergoing IVF found that women with the highest Mediterranean diet adherence had 65-68% higher odds of pregnancy and live birth compared to those with the lowest adherence, after adjusting for age and other factors. A separate case-control study found higher Mediterranean diet adherence linked to lower risk of ovulatory infertility. Multiple smaller studies support the association.

**What Mediterranean eating looks like for fertility:**
- **Fatty fish 2-3 times per week:** salmon, sardines, mackerel (EPA+DHA support egg quality and reduce inflammation)
- **Legumes as protein anchors:** lentils, chickpeas, black beans (high in folate, fiber, iron)
- **Olive oil as primary fat:** 2-4 tablespoons daily
- **Non-starchy vegetables as the meal volume base**
- **Whole grains over refined:** oats, quinoa, farro, brown rice
- **Modest dairy** (if tolerated): yogurt, cheese in moderation
- **Limit:** red meat (2-3 times per week maximum), alcohol, processed foods, refined sugar

The Mediterranean pattern is not a rigid rule system. It is a general direction: more whole food plant and fish proteins, more olive oil, more vegetables, less processed food.

## Folate: Non-Negotiable Before Conception

Folate is required for neural tube closure in the embryo. This occurs at 3-4 weeks post-conception, before most people know they are pregnant. The evidence is clear and has been established since the 1990s.

**Standard recommendation:** 400-800mcg daily, beginning at least one month before conception (three months is better for building tissue stores).

**The MTHFR consideration:** About 40% of the population carries MTHFR polymorphisms that reduce the conversion of folic acid to active methylfolate. For these individuals, taking methylfolate (5-MTHF, sold as Metafolin or labeled as "methylfolate") rather than synthetic folic acid ensures adequate bioavailable folate regardless of genetic variation. You do not need genetic testing to take methylfolate. It is safe and equally effective for people without MTHFR variants.

**Dietary folate:** Leafy greens (spinach, romaine, asparagus), legumes, and citrus are high in dietary folate. Dietary folate supports but does not replace supplemental folate in the preconception window.

## CoQ10: Egg Quality Specifically

Coenzyme Q10 has the best evidence specifically for egg quality, rather than general fertility.

**The mechanism:** Egg cells undergo meiosis, a chromosomally demanding division process that requires enormous amounts of mitochondrial ATP. Mitochondrial function declines with age. CoQ10 is essential for the electron transport chain that generates ATP. Supplementing CoQ10 may help restore mitochondrial function in aging ovarian cells.

**The evidence:** A pilot RCT found reduced aneuploidy rates and better embryo quality in women with poor ovarian response taking CoQ10 (600mg daily) before IVF. A separate RCT of poor-responder IVF patients found significantly higher retrieved oocyte counts in the CoQ10 group. Multiple observational studies support a link between CoQ10 supplementation and better fertility outcomes in women over 35.

**Who this is most relevant for:** Women over 35, women with diminished ovarian reserve, and women with prior IVF failures or poor embryo quality. For younger women with normal ovarian reserve, the effect size may be smaller.

**Form and dose:** Ubiquinol (reduced form) is better absorbed than ubiquinone, particularly with food. 400-600mg daily, divided into two doses. The 90-day egg maturation window means starting at least 3 months before the target conception period.

## Omega-3 Fatty Acids

DHA (docosahexaenoic acid) is essential for embryo development, particularly neural development. EPA and DHA reduce prostaglandin-driven inflammation in the uterus, improving the implantation environment. Omega-3s are also important for sperm quality if a male partner is involved.

**Dose:** 2-3g EPA+DHA combined daily. Use fish oil (molecularly distilled for purity) or algae-based DHA for people avoiding fish.

**Mercury in fish:** Fatty fish 2-3 times per week is safe during preconception and pregnancy. Avoid high-mercury species: shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish, and bigeye tuna. Salmon, sardines, herring, and anchovies are low-mercury with high omega-3 content.

## Vitamin D

Vitamin D deficiency is common in reproductive-age women, especially at northern latitudes, and is linked to fertility outcomes. Vitamin D receptors are present in the ovary. Vitamin D appears to regulate AMH signaling and follicle development.

**Get tested:** A 25-OH vitamin D level is a routine blood test. Target: 40-60 ng/mL. Many people need 2000-4000 IU daily to reach this range, especially in winter at northern latitudes.

Correcting deficiency matters more than supplementing beyond sufficiency.

## Iron

Iron deficiency impairs ovulation quality. The Nurses' Health Study found that supplemental iron intake was linked to lower rates of ovulatory infertility in a dose-dependent manner.

**Sources and absorption:** Heme iron from meat absorbs most efficiently. Non-heme iron from plants absorbs at 2-20%. Always pair plant iron with vitamin C to maximize absorption. Avoid calcium at iron-rich meals because calcium inhibits iron absorption.

If you have heavy periods, get serum ferritin tested (not just hemoglobin, since you can be iron-depleted without being anemic). Ferritin below 30 ng/mL warrants treatment even without anemia symptoms.

## What to Avoid

**Trans fats:** Consistently linked to ovulatory infertility. The primary sources are industrial fried foods, processed snacks, non-dairy creamers with hydrogenated oils, and packaged baked goods. Check ingredient labels for "partially hydrogenated oil."

**Alcohol:** Alcohol reduces fertility through multiple mechanisms. It impairs ovulation quality, reduces progesterone in the luteal phase, disrupts early embryo implantation, and raises miscarriage risk. Complete elimination during the active conception period is the standard recommendation.

**High-mercury fish:** Mercury accumulates in eggs and embryos. Avoid shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish, and bigeye tuna.

**Soy in excess:** Soy contains phytoestrogens that may interfere with ovulation at very high intakes. Moderate soy consumption (1-2 servings daily) has not been shown to impair fertility in well-designed studies. Extremely high soy intake from protein powders and supplements is the more relevant concern.

**Processed meats:** Linked to worse fertility outcomes in both sexes in multiple cohort studies. The mechanism is unclear. It may relate to trans fat content, preservatives, or overall diet quality.

## Building a Preconception Supplement Baseline

For a person with no identified deficiencies:
- Methylfolate (400-800mcg daily): start now if you might conceive in the next 3 months
- CoQ10 ubiquinol (400-600mg daily): especially relevant if you are over 35
- Omega-3 fish oil (2-3g EPA+DHA daily): check the label for actual EPA+DHA content, not total oil
- Vitamin D: test first; supplement to correct deficiency
- A prenatal vitamin with iron (if not iron-sufficient through diet)

This baseline does not include the many fertility supplements on the market with no clinical evidence. The evidence-supported list is short. Adding unproven compounds does not improve your odds. It adds cost and complexity.

## What This Means for Floriva Users

Tracking ovulation timing - when your LH surge occurs, whether BBT shows a sustained post-ovulatory rise - is fundamental preconception information. Knowing your actual ovulation day (not a calendar estimate) optimizes the conception timing window. Luteal phase length tells you whether progesterone is adequate for implantation support. This data is clinically useful and it stays on your device, not in a company's database.
