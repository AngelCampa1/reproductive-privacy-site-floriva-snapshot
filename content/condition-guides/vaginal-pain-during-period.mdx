## Why "Vaginal Pain" Is Not One Thing

The vagina is surrounded by the pelvic floor muscles, adjacent to the rectum, below the cervix and uterus, and near a dense network of nerves and blood vessels. Pain in this region during menstruation can originate from any of these structures and be perceived as vaginal, even when the source is elsewhere.

This matters because clear details can make a visit more useful. Where the pain is felt, when in the cycle it occurs, what it feels like, and what happens at the same time can help you explain the pattern without guessing the cause.

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not a substitute for medical advice.

## Referred Pain From the Uterus

Menstrual cramps begin in the uterus, but the pain does not always stay there. The uterus and vagina share overlapping nerve pathways, and the pelvic floor muscles that surround the vagina often contract protectively in response to uterine cramping. People often describe this as a deep, downward-pressing pain that feels vaginal or rectal but is actually driven by uterine contractions and pelvic floor tension.

This type of pain may follow the timing of cramps, show up early in bleeding, and fade as flow changes. Log whether the vaginal or rectal feeling rises and falls with cramps.

## Pelvic Floor Muscle Involvement

The pelvic floor can develop chronic tension patterns, particularly in people who experience painful periods, have had previous pelvic trauma, or deal with anxiety. Tense pelvic floor muscles can produce pain at the vaginal entrance, in the perineum, or deep in the pelvis. During menstruation, the combination of blood flow, uterine activity, and posture changes can aggravate muscles that are already held in tension.

Pelvic floor tension does not always announce itself with obvious symptoms. Some people experience it as a persistent, localized ache that worsens with sitting, tampon use, or sex. A clinician can help decide whether pelvic floor care is worth considering.

## Vulvar Conditions

The vulva, the external genital tissue, can be sensitive to the moisture, blood contact, and friction of menstruation. Conditions including vulvodynia, contact dermatitis, lichen sclerosus, and active yeast or bacterial infections can all produce vulvar pain that is more noticeable around or during menstruation.

### Vulvodynia

Chronic vulvar burning or rawness without an identified cause. It may be localized (often at the vestibule, the area just inside the vaginal opening) or generalized. It does not follow the menstrual cycle precisely, though some people find it worsens around menstruation.

### Yeast and bacterial infections

Both can cause burning, itching, and vaginal discomfort that is exacerbated by menstruation. Unusual discharge (thick and white, or thin and gray or green with a strong odor) alongside vaginal pain is a straightforward indication that evaluation is warranted.

### Contact reactions

Scented products, certain pad materials, and some lubricants can cause vulvar irritation in some people. If you already changed a product before making this note, write down what changed and when.

## Endometriosis and the Rectovaginal Septum

A less discussed location for endometriosis is the rectovaginal septum (the tissue between the posterior vagina and the front of the rectum). Lesions here produce deep vaginal or rectal pain that is typically most intense around menstruation, often alongside pain during sex and pain during bowel movements.

Some people with endometriosis have deep vaginal or rectal pain, especially when period pain overlaps with pain during sex, bowel pain, bladder pain, or painful bowel movements. Standard imaging may not answer every question. If this pattern fits your notes, ask your clinician what evaluation or referral fits.

## What to Log

Document the following every time you experience vaginal pain during your period:

**Location:** At the vaginal entrance, deep inside, one side only, or diffuse. Also note if it extends to the rectum or perineum.

**Character:** Burning, aching, sharp, pressure, cramping, or raw. These words can make your notes easier to understand.

**Timing:** When in the cycle does it start and stop? Is it only during heavy flow, or throughout the period? Does it occur between periods as well?

**Relationship to activities:** Does using a tampon or cup cause or worsen it? Does sex cause it? Is it worse with sitting for long periods?

**Associated symptoms:** Any discharge changes, burning with urination, rectal symptoms, or fever.

## When to Seek Evaluation

Vaginal pain that is new, worsening, accompanied by unusual discharge or fever, persistent outside menstruation, or limiting daily function warrants prompt evaluation. Do not assume pain that occurs with your period is simply "bad cramps" without considering whether it fits that pattern.
