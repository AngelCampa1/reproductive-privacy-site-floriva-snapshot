## Why Cycles Go Irregular: The Core Mechanism

A regular menstrual cycle depends on a precisely timed hormonal conversation between the hypothalamus, the pituitary gland, and the ovaries. Anything that disrupts that conversation can change when or whether ovulation occurs, which in turn changes when or whether a period follows.

Understanding this chain helps explain why so many different things can produce irregular cycles: the disruption can happen at any point along the axis, and the cycle is downstream of all of it.

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not a substitute for medical advice.

## Cause 1: PCOS

Polycystic ovary syndrome is one of the most common hormonal conditions affecting people with ovaries. In PCOS, elevated androgen levels disrupt the normal development of follicles in the ovaries. Instead of one follicle maturing and releasing an egg, multiple follicles begin developing but stall. The result is irregular or absent ovulation, and consequently irregular or absent periods.

The irregularity pattern with PCOS tends to be unpredictable: cycles may range from 35 to 90 days or longer, may come inconsistently, or may be absent for months at a time. Flow when it does occur may be lighter (from an anovulatory cycle) or heavier (if the lining has had longer to build).

**What to track:** Every cycle start date, no matter how infrequent. Daily symptoms including acne, hair changes, energy, and mood. Basal body temperature to identify whether any cycles include ovulation.

## Cause 2: Thyroid Dysfunction

The thyroid gland regulates metabolic rate, and thyroid hormones interact directly with the reproductive hormonal axis. Both too little and too much thyroid hormone can disrupt cycles.

Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) is more commonly associated with heavy, irregular, or infrequent periods. Elevated TSH can trigger elevated prolactin, which in turn suppresses ovulation. Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) more often causes light, infrequent, or missed periods, along with other symptoms like heat intolerance, palpitations, and unintended weight loss.

**What to track:** Cycle dates, flow intensity, and any associated symptoms including fatigue, cold or heat intolerance, hair texture changes, and weight changes. These symptoms alongside cycle changes give a clinician the picture needed to order targeted tests.

## Cause 3: Functional Hypothalamic Amenorrhea

When the body perceives a state of energy deficit or significant stress, the hypothalamus downregulates the hormonal signals that drive ovulation. This is not a malfunction; it is a protective response. The body is prioritizing survival over reproduction.

This can happen with:
- Significant caloric restriction or very low body weight
- High training volume, particularly in endurance or aesthetic sports
- Chronic psychological stress
- Acute illness followed by gradual recovery

The hallmark pattern is cycles that become increasingly infrequent and then stop. The period often returns when energy availability improves, though recovery can take months even after the underlying stressor resolves.

**What to track:** Cycle dates, any changes in exercise volume or intensity, dietary changes, sleep quality, and stress levels. Also log any symptoms of low estrogen including bone pain, vaginal dryness, and mood changes. This context helps distinguish FHA from other causes.

## Cause 4: Elevated Prolactin

Prolactin is a pituitary hormone primarily known for driving milk production after childbirth. However, elevated prolactin outside of pregnancy or breastfeeding can suppress the hormonal axis that drives ovulation, causing irregular or absent periods.

Common causes of elevated prolactin include a benign pituitary growth called a prolactinoma, thyroid dysfunction, and certain medications including antipsychotics, antidepressants, and some medications for nausea and blood pressure. Nipple discharge (galactorrhea) alongside irregular periods is a notable combination that warrants prolactin testing.

**What to track:** Cycle dates, any nipple discharge, medications you are taking, and any vision changes or headaches (which can accompany larger pituitary growths).

## Cause 5: Perimenopause

The years before the final menstrual period are characterized by declining and increasingly erratic ovarian function. Cycles may become shorter, longer, more variable, or more widely spaced. Heavy bleeding is common. This process can begin in the mid-to-late 30s, though the average onset is in the mid-40s.

Perimenopausal irregularity is normal but should not be assumed. Heavy bleeding and irregular cycles can also indicate endometrial hyperplasia or other structural changes that require evaluation.

**What to track:** Cycle dates and length, flow intensity, and any new symptoms including hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, and sleep changes. Also note if any individual period is much heavier than your recent baseline.

## Cause 6: Structural Changes

Uterine polyps and submucosal fibroids can interfere with the normal shedding of the uterine lining, producing irregular bleeding, spotting between periods, and heavier flow. These structural changes do not always produce irregular cycle timing, but they do produce irregular bleeding patterns.

**What to track:** Spotting between periods (with dates), flow intensity per day, any bleeding after sex, and any new pelvic pain or pressure.

## What to Track Across All Causes

Regardless of which cause seems most likely, the most useful tracking data for any evaluation of irregular periods includes:

- Cycle start dates (day 1 of full flow) for as many cycles as possible
- Flow intensity per day (light, moderate, heavy, very heavy)
- Any spotting or bleeding between periods, with dates
- Associated symptoms: pain, fatigue, mood changes, physical symptoms like hair or skin changes
- Lifestyle context: major stress events, changes in exercise, changes in diet or weight
- All medications and supplements

Three to six months of consistent data is the foundation for a productive clinical conversation. Without it, the evaluation starts from a lower base.
