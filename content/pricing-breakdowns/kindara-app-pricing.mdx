## What Kindara Was

Kindara was a fertility awareness method (FAM) app for people who tracked basal body temperature, cervical mucus, and other fertility signs. It was one of the more serious FAM tools available. Popular with users who wanted detailed charting beyond basic period tracking.

The app had a free tier with limited charting and a premium subscription with full FAM tools. Kindara also sold the Wink, a Bluetooth BBT thermometer that synced with the app.

For FAM practitioners, Kindara was a dedicated tool. It is now gone.

## What Happened

Kindara was acquired, then discontinued. The app was removed from app stores. The servers went offline. The community forums disappeared. The Wink thermometer lost its companion software.

Users who had tracked their cycles for years lost access to that data. Daily temperature readings, cervical mucus observations, and symptom logs disappeared when the company stopped maintaining the product.

## The Sunk Cost Problem

Fertility tracking data gets more useful over time. One cycle of BBT data tells you very little. Twelve cycles start showing patterns. Three years give you a reliable baseline.

Kindara users who tracked for three or more years built up hundreds of hours of daily data. When the servers went offline, that work was gone. The data lived on infrastructure controlled by a company that chose to shut down.

This is the core risk of cloud-dependent health tracking. Your data's lifespan is tied to the company's lifespan. Startups fail. Products get cut. Acquisitions lead to shutdowns. When the company stops, your data stops.

## The Hardware Trap

The Wink thermometer made things worse. Users bought dedicated hardware designed to work with Kindara's app. When the app disappeared, the Wink became a Bluetooth thermometer with no charting software.

It still reads temperatures. But the automated syncing, charting, and pattern recognition stopped working. Users paid for hardware locked to software that no longer exists.

## What This Teaches About Pricing

Kindara's pricing looked reasonable: a subscription for a dedicated FAM tool with hardware integration. But the true cost included a risk not on the price tag. Your entire tracking history and hardware investment could become worthless from a business decision you had no say in.

Every cloud-dependent health app carries this risk. Clue, Flo, Natural Cycles, any app that stores your data on its servers ties your health history to whether that company keeps going.

## Floriva's architecture reduces this risk

Floriva keeps core records on your device. If Floriva stopped operating tomorrow, local records would still be on your phone. Your cycle history and symptom notes remain under your control on hardware you own.

Check current Floriva onboarding for exact export options before relying on a specific file or format. The core architecture still reduces shutdown risk because your main record starts on your device.

With Floriva's subscription, you are paying for an app and for data sovereignty. Years of cycle tracking cannot be wiped out by an acquisition, a pivot, or a shutdown.

## The Lesson

Kindara's former users are rebuilding their tracking history from scratch. The years of data they accumulated are gone. The patterns they found are lost. The Wink thermometers sit in drawers.

When you evaluate a period tracker's price, factor in the risk of losing everything you put into it. For cloud-dependent apps, that risk is real. Kindara proved it.
