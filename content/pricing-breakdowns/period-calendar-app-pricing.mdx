## The Price Tag: Free

Period Calendar by Simple Innovation costs nothing to download. It has been one of the most popular period tracking apps on both app stores for years, mostly because of the zero-dollar price. For users who search "period tracker" and sort by price, it appears at the top.

The app covers basic period and ovulation tracking, a fertility calendar, symptom logging, and cycle history. It works. Predictions improve over time as you log more cycles. For basic functionality at no cost, it does what it says.

## How Free Apps Make Money

Period Calendar is ad-supported. Ads appear throughout the app, and that revenue funds development. This is a standard mobile app model. But the implications are different when the app handles reproductive health data.

Advertising in a period tracker is not like advertising in a weather app. The data collected alongside those ads includes menstrual cycle dates, fertility windows, symptom logs, and sexual activity records. Ad SDKs collect device identifiers and behavioral signals, building profiles that follow users across the advertising ecosystem.

The ads you see are the visible cost. The data flowing to ad networks is the invisible one.

## Small Developer, Big Data Questions

Period Calendar is made by a smaller development studio. This matters for privacy.

Smaller companies typically have less detailed privacy policies. They have fewer legal resources to challenge subpoenas or data requests. Their data handling gets less scrutiny from regulators. And their ad SDK implementations may share more data than needed because ad revenue optimization is the main business concern.

This does not mean the developer is acting in bad faith. It means the structural incentives of ad-supported development push toward more data sharing, not less. A small team focused on ad revenue does not have the motivation to build privacy-preserving ad integrations.

## Server Storage and Legal Risk

Period Calendar stores tracking data on the developer's servers. There is no local-only mode. Your complete cycle history, including dates that could indicate a missed period or pregnancy, lives on infrastructure controlled by a third party.

In states with reproductive health restrictions, this creates exposure. Law enforcement can serve legal process on the developer to obtain user records. A small company is less likely to challenge such requests than a large one.

Your data's security depends entirely on a company you have no relationship with beyond installing their free app.

## What Ad Removal Actually Changes

If Period Calendar offers an ad-removal purchase, paying it stops ads from appearing on your screen. This is a user experience improvement, not a privacy improvement.

In many implementations, the ad SDK stays active for analytics purposes even when ad rendering stops. The data pipeline may continue running silently.

Even if ad SDKs fully deactivate, your data stays on the developer's servers. The fundamental architecture does not change with an ad-removal purchase.

## The Floriva Alternative

Floriva is a paid app with no free tier: no ads, no ad SDKs, no readable central cycle database storage, no account required. Your cycle data stays on your device. There is no readable central cycle database to request from Floriva.

The subscription is the business model. Floriva does not need your data to be valuable to advertisers because your payment funds the product directly.

## Making the Choice

Period Calendar is free and functional. Floriva is a paid app with no free tier and is private. The decision comes down to whether your reproductive health data is worth paying to keep away from the advertising networks that currently receive it for free.
