## Stardust's Privacy Positioning

Stardust launched after Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization raised public concern about period tracker privacy. The app markets itself as privacy-conscious, and it has attracted users looking for a Flo alternative on those grounds.

The marketing is not dishonest. Stardust states it does not sell data, and there is no evidence otherwise. But there is a distinction worth understanding before paying $7.99/mo for it.

## Policy Privacy vs Architectural Privacy

Stardust's privacy is policy-based. The company commits to not misusing your data. This is the same commitment every period tracker makes, including Flo before the FTC found otherwise in 2021.

Policy-based privacy depends on three things:
- The company maintaining its current commitment
- No breach exposing server-side data
- No court order compelling data production

Stardust requires an account and stores data on its servers. Policy language cannot change these facts. A subpoena to Stardust's servers would produce your data regardless of what the privacy policy says.

Architectural privacy works differently. If data is stored on your device and never sent to a server, there is no readable central record to subpoena.

## Policy Privacy vs Paying for Architecture

Stardust Premium costs $69.99/yr for policy-based privacy on Stardust's servers. Floriva is a paid app with no free tier that keeps data on-device instead.

If the astrology features are the draw, Stardust is the only option. If the draw is privacy, the architecture matters more than the brand positioning.
