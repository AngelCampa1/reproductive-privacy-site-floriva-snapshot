## Life Period Tracker: The Standard Free Model

Life Period Tracker follows the most common business model in period apps: free to use, supported by ads, with a premium upsell to remove them. It has been downloaded millions of times and has solid ratings on both app stores.

The app covers period tracking, ovulation predictions, fertility windows, symptom logging, and a cycle calendar. For basic tracking, it works. The interface is clean, predictions improve with use, and the free tier does not block core features.

The question is not whether Life works. It does. The question is what "free" actually costs.

## The Ad-Supported Architecture

Life's free tier shows ads throughout the app. Behind those ads sit advertising SDKs: code from ad networks that collects data to target ads.

These SDKs collect device identifiers that link your activity across apps, timestamps showing when you use the tracker and what you log, behavioral patterns that reveal your cycle timing and health concerns, and interaction data used for ad targeting.

The ad network does not care that you are using a period tracker. It cares about the behavioral signals your usage creates. Those signals become part of a profile that follows you across the internet.

## What Premium Changes

Life's premium subscription removes ads from the interface. Check the app store for the current price, as it varies by platform.

What premium changes: you stop seeing ads. What premium does not change: your data stays on Life's servers, your account is still required, and your data stays accessible through legal process.

Premium is a user experience upgrade. The experience improves. The privacy does not.

## The Server Problem

Every piece of data you log in Life goes to the developer's servers. Cycle dates, symptoms, moods, sexual activity, pregnancy indicators. There is no local-only mode.

A complete record of your reproductive health exists on infrastructure you do not control. The developer controls access policies and decides how to respond to law enforcement requests. You have no say in any of this.

## Legal Exposure

Server-stored reproductive health data can be compelled through legal process. In states with abortion restrictions, cycle data has become legally relevant. Missed periods, late cycles, and fertility indicators stored on a third party's servers can be subpoenaed.

Life's developer must respond to valid legal process. A small app developer is unlikely to have the legal resources to challenge broad requests. Your data's protection depends on their ability and willingness to fight for it.

## Floriva: A Different Structure

Floriva is a paid app with no free tier. That subscription replaces advertising revenue entirely. No ads means no ad SDKs. No ad SDKs means no behavioral data flowing to third parties.

Floriva keeps core records on your device. There is no readable central cycle database holding your symptom logs. No account is required for core tracking.

## The Decision

Life Period Tracker is free and functional. It also feeds your reproductive health data into an advertising ecosystem and stores it on servers accessible to anyone with a subpoena.

Floriva is a paid app with no free tier and keeps everything on your device. The question is simple: is your cycle data worth paying to keep private, or are you fine with the current arrangement where it funds advertising for free?
