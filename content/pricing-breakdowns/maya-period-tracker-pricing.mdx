## What Maya Costs in Money

Maya is a free period tracking app. You download it, open it, and start tracking without paying anything. If a premium tier exists, it removes ads and may add analytics features. The free version works for basic cycle tracking.

Zero dollars beats any subscription if you are looking at sticker price alone. But sticker price is not total cost.

## What Maya Costs in Data

Maya makes money through advertising. The app embeds software development kits (SDKs) from ad networks into its code. These SDKs run alongside the app and send data to ad platforms.

Privacy International investigated Maya in 2019 and found it was sharing intimate health data with Facebook's SDK. The data included contraception methods, moods, and sexual activity. This sharing happened before users had agreed to Maya's privacy policy. The app was transmitting data to Facebook the moment it opened.

This is not a bug. This is how ad-funded apps work. Ad SDKs need data to target ads. The more personal the data, the more valuable the targeting. Reproductive health data is among the most personal data a person creates.

## The Ad-SDK Problem

When people hear "ads," they picture banner placements. The real mechanism goes deeper. Ad SDKs are code libraries from companies like Meta, Google, and smaller ad networks that run inside the app. They can collect:

- Device identifiers that link your activity across apps
- Usage patterns showing when and how often you track
- Behavioral signals that inform advertising profiles
- App-level data the developer permits access to

The ad network does not just show you a banner. It builds a profile. That profile follows you across every other app and website using the same ad network. Your period tracking data becomes one input in a cross-platform advertising identity.

## What Happens to That Data

Once data reaches an ad network's servers, the app developer no longer controls it. The ad network's privacy policy governs what happens next. That data can be retained, combined with other sources, and used for purposes the original app never described.

Maya can update its own privacy policy. It cannot update Facebook's or Google's. Once the SDK transmits data, it belongs to the receiving platform.

## Server Storage and Legal Exposure

Maya also stores your tracking data on its own servers. This creates a second exposure risk. Server-stored health data can be reached through subpoenas, court orders, and law enforcement requests.

In states with abortion restrictions, server-stored cycle data is a specific legal risk. Missed periods, late cycles, and fertility indicators stored on a company's servers can be compelled by authorities. The developer may fight a request, or may not. You have no control over that decision.

## Floriva's Model

Floriva is a paid app with no free tier. That subscription is the entire business model. No ads, no ad SDKs, no data flowing to ad networks. All tracking data stays on your device.

There is no server to subpoena because there is no server storing your health data. There are no ad SDKs in the code because the subscription covers all revenue needs.

The subscription is the price. Not a floor price with hidden data costs on top. The full price.

## The Real Comparison

Maya costs $0 and your reproductive health data. Floriva costs a subscription and nothing else. The question is whether your cycle data, symptom logs, and fertility indicators are worth more or less than that to you.

For many people, the answer was not clear until they understood what "free" actually meant.
