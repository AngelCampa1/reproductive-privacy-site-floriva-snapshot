## What School Devices Monitor

School-issued devices (primarily Chromebooks, but also iPads, Windows laptops, and managed Android tablets) run monitoring and management software as a condition of the school's acceptable use policy. This monitoring is not hidden. Schools disclose it in technology use agreements that students and parents sign, though the scope of monitoring is often described in vague terms.

The most common monitoring tools in US schools include:

**GoGuardian**: Logs all web browsing activity, allows teachers to view student screens in real time during class, flags searches matching keyword lists, and can capture browsing history across all sessions on the device.

**Hapara**: Provides teachers with real-time visibility into student browser tabs and activity during class. Hapara Highlights shows open tabs and browsing history.

**Securly**: Filters web content and monitors browsing activity. Includes AI-based flagging of content deemed concerning.

**Bark**: Monitors for signals of self-harm, bullying, and other safety concerns. Scans communications and web activity on managed devices and school-provisioned accounts.

**Lightspeed Systems**: Web filtering and monitoring with reporting dashboards for administrators.

Each of these tools can see and log websites visited, searches performed, and in some cases screen content. The data is stored in administrative dashboards accessible to school IT staff, designated administrators, and in some configurations, teachers.

## What This Means for Period Tracking

If you use a school-issued device to:

- Search for "period tracker" or "menstrual cycle calculator"
- Visit a web-based period tracking service
- Access reproductive health information websites
- Search for information about pregnancy, Plan B, or reproductive health
- Use a browser extension related to health tracking

That activity is captured by the monitoring software and stored in logs that school staff can review.

The monitoring is not targeted at reproductive health specifically. These tools monitor all web activity and flag content matching keyword lists configured by the school. However, the effect is that reproductive health searches are captured alongside everything else.

## The Real-Time Visibility Problem

Some monitoring tools provide real-time screen viewing. During a class session, a teacher using GoGuardian Teacher or Hapara can see exactly what is on every student's screen. If a student opens a period tracking website or searches for menstrual health information during class, the teacher sees it immediately.

This means a student's reproductive health activity can appear not just in a log file reviewed later, but on a teacher's monitoring dashboard in real time, visible to anyone near that screen.

## CIPA, FERPA, and the Legal Framework

**CIPA (Children's Internet Protection Act)**: Requires schools receiving federal E-rate funding to filter internet access. CIPA is about blocking access, not monitoring, but the filtering tools schools deploy often include monitoring features that go well beyond what CIPA requires.

**FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act)**: Protects education records, records directly related to a student and maintained by the school. Whether web browsing logs from monitoring software constitute education records under FERPA is an unsettled legal question. If they do, parents have a right to inspect them, and the school has restrictions on disclosure. If they do not, the logs may have fewer protections. This is not legal advice.

**State student privacy laws**: Some states have enacted student data privacy laws that restrict how schools and educational technology vendors can use student data. Coverage and strength vary by state. California's SOPIPA, for example, restricts the use of student data by educational technology providers.

**The practical reality**: Regardless of the legal classification, the data is collected and stored. It is accessible to school staff. The legal framework may restrict what the school does with it, but it does not prevent the collection itself.

## How to Protect Your Privacy

**Use a personal device for all health-related activity.** This is the single most effective step. A personal phone that is not managed by the school runs no school monitoring software. Use your personal phone for period tracking, reproductive health searches, and any health-related browsing.

**Use cellular data, not school WiFi, for health searches.** Even on a personal device, connecting to school WiFi gives the school visibility into which domains you visit. Cellular data connections do not pass through the school's network monitoring.

**Use an on-device-only app on your personal phone.** An app that stores data only on your device, runs no cloud sync, and contains no third-party tracking creates the smallest possible digital footprint. There is no web browsing history, no cloud account, and no data transmitted to any server.

**Do not use school email accounts for health-related accounts.** If you create an account on a period tracking service using your school email, the school may have access to account confirmation emails and any communications from the service. Use a personal email account for any health service registrations.

**Be aware that school accounts (Google Workspace for Education) may be monitored.** If your school uses Google Workspace for Education, your school Google account is managed by the school. Google Drive files, Gmail messages, and Chrome browsing history on that account may be visible to administrators.

## A Note for Parents

If your child uses a school-issued device at home, be aware that the monitoring continues outside school hours. Review the school's technology acceptable use policy to understand what is monitored. Consider providing a personal device for health-related activities, or at minimum, ensure your child knows that school devices are monitored.

Schools implement monitoring for legitimate safety reasons, including identifying students in crisis. But the same monitoring infrastructure that flags a student searching for self-harm resources also captures a student tracking their period or researching reproductive health. The monitoring tools do not distinguish between safety-relevant and privacy-sensitive health activity.

## The Architecture Principle

For students, [on-device-only period tracking](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker) is especially worth considering. A native app on a personal phone, storing data locally with no cloud sync, leaves no web browsing trail for school monitoring to capture, no cloud account for anyone to access, and no data transmission for any network to intercept. The data exists in one place: the student's personal phone, protected by the device's own lock screen and encryption.
