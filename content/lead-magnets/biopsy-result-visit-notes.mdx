Cervical and breast results can arrive as a portal note, a letter, or a call. The words can be short and hard to hold in your head.

This page keeps the work small. Copy the words. Write your questions. Do not use this page to explain, interpret, or diagnose a result.

Ask your clinician or imaging center what your own result means.

CDC explains that a Pap test checks cervical cells and an HPV test checks cells for HPV. NCI explains that abnormal HPV and Pap results need clinician review, with terms like ASC-US, LSIL, HSIL, unsatisfactory, and HPV-positive. Ask: What are the exact result words? Was an HPV test done too? Do you need my last Pap or HPV date? Is the full lab report in the portal?

| Your words for the change | |
| Pain or tenderness | no / yes |
| Skin or nipple change | no / yes |
| Prior note or visit about this | no / yes / not sure |

Mayo Clinic says cyclic breast pain is related to the menstrual cycle and may come with swelling, fullness, or lumpiness. Mayo Clinic lists breast cyst symptoms that can include a movable lump, nipple discharge, and size or tenderness changes around a period. Cleveland Clinic says a clinical breast exam may be used for breast changes such as pain, skin changes, nipple fluid, or lumps.

Use words you would say out loud. "Left outer side, pea-sized spot" is enough. Do not force a reason into the notes.

## Records and portal access

HHS says people have rights to see and get copies of health records from covered providers and health plans, including asking for corrections.

Ask your clinic or imaging center:

- Can I download the full report?
- Can I get the images too?
- Who can see this portal result?
- Does proxy access affect this result?
- Can I correct a wrong name, date, or phone number?

## Privacy note

Cervical and breast result notes can include sex history, HPV status, images, clinic names, insurance, family history, and portal messages.

Keep the smallest useful note.

| Instead of saving | Try saving |
|---|---|
| Full portal screenshot | Exact result words |
| Full phone transcript | Main question |
| Full image file | Where images are stored |
| Full app export | Short clinic note |

FTC guidance says health information can include more than diagnoses and treatment. Small details, such as browsing or location data, can still imply health facts.
