Perimenopause can feel messy. One month may look normal. The next may bring a short cycle, poor sleep, and hot flashes.

This tracker groups symptoms so a long list feels manageable. It cannot diagnose perimenopause, rule out other causes, or tell you what treatment to use.

## There is no official symptom count

You may see lists of 34, 66, or 100 perimenopause symptoms online. Those counts are not official medical checklists. Major medical sources group symptoms instead, focusing on period changes, hot flashes, sleep, mood, vaginal and urinary symptoms, sex pain, and body changes. Your list does not need to match anyone else's. Track what is new, repeated, or hard on daily life.

Seek urgent care if bleeding is heavy enough that you feel faint, weak, confused, or short of breath.

## Questions for a clinician

- Could these symptoms fit perimenopause? What else should we rule out?
- Which bleeding changes need follow-up?
- Could my medicines or hormones affect this pattern?
- What can I do for hot flashes, night sweats, or sleep?
- What can help vaginal dryness or sex pain?
- What symptoms mean I should call sooner?
- How long should I keep tracking?

## A note on postpartum cycle return

If you are tracking cycle return after childbirth rather than perimenopause, the patterns and timelines are different. Ovulation can return before your first postpartum period, so an absent period does not mean you cannot conceive; exclusive breastfeeding can delay period return for 6 months or longer through lactational amenorrhea, while combination feeding or formula feeding often sees earlier return, typically between 6 weeks and 6 months. Postpartum lochia (bleeding from the healing uterine wound) is not a period and is not hormonally driven; it transitions from red to pink to white or yellow over about 4 to 6 weeks and should have a gap before true period bleeding begins. If heavy red bleeding returns after lochia has already lightened, or you soak a pad in under an hour, contact your provider. This kit's daily and weekly log format above can be adapted for postpartum tracking, but the reference ranges and red flags differ from perimenopause. Ask your OB or midwife which pattern applies to you.

## Privacy note

Perimenopause notes can include sex, mood, medicine, bleeding, and location details.

Bring a short list to care, not every detail: the date your pattern changed, your last few bleeding dates, symptoms that repeat, symptoms that disrupt life, medicine or hormone changes, and your main question.
