Use this kit if you have endometriosis symptoms, a diagnosis, or are preparing for a visit or surgery.

It gives you one place for pain, bowel and bladder symptoms, diet notes, pain relief trials, imaging visits, and laparoscopy recovery.

It is a set of tracking sheets and question lists.

It is not medical advice.

It cannot diagnose endometriosis, rule it out, or replace a clinician visit.

If pain is severe, new, or unsafe to wait on, contact a clinician now.

## What this kit can do

| This kit can help you | This kit cannot |
|---|---|
| Name symptoms in plain words. | Diagnose endometriosis. |
| Show if symptoms come near your period. | Rule out other causes. |
| Help you prepare for a visit or surgery follow-up. | Tell you what treatment to use. |
| Help you ask for next steps. | Replace a clinician visit. |

## Symptom checklist

Check what fits. Leave the rest blank.

### Pain timing

- [ ] Cramps stop me from work, school, sleep, or plans.
- [ ] Pain starts before my period.
- [ ] Pain is worst during my period.
- [ ] Pain happens at ovulation or between periods.
- [ ] Pain lasts after bleeding stops.
- [ ] Pain medicine does not help enough.
- [ ] Pain wakes me up.

### Where the pain is

- [ ] Lower belly, one or both sides.
- [ ] Lower back, hips, or thighs.
- [ ] Rectum or anus.
- [ ] Bladder area.
- [ ] Deep pain inside the pelvis.

### Bowel and bladder symptoms

- [ ] Pain when having a bowel movement.
- [ ] Rectal pain or pressure.
- [ ] Constipation or diarrhea near my period.
- [ ] Bloating or nausea near my period.
- [ ] Pain when peeing or needing to pee more often.
- [ ] Blood in stool or urine near my period.

These symptoms can have many causes. Write them down. Do not use them to diagnose yourself.

### Sex pain, if you want to note it

Skip this section if it does not apply or does not feel safe.

- [ ] Deep pain during sex or pain after sex.
- [ ] Pain is worse near my period.
- [ ] I avoid sex because of pain.
- [ ] I want to ask about pelvic pain in private.

### Follow-up questions

- What did you find during surgery? Did it confirm endometriosis?
- Were bowel, bladder, ovary, or tube areas involved?
- What symptoms should improve first, and what may take more time?
- When can I return to work, school, sex, exercise, or lifting?
- Should I keep tracking pain by cycle day, and my first period after surgery?

## Ultrasound question list

Use this before or after imaging. It does not read your scan or diagnose endometriosis.

- What will this scan look for? Can it look for ovarian endometriomas or deep endometriosis?
- What did the scan show, and what did it not show?
- If my scan is normal, what does that mean? Could symptoms still fit endometriosis?
- When would referral or laparoscopy make sense?
- What symptoms mean I should call sooner?

## Short visit script

> "I am not trying to diagnose myself. I am using this to show a pattern. My pain or symptoms affect ________. They are worst around ________. I also notice ________. What should we check next?"

If your visit includes sex, fertility, pregnancy, bowel, or bladder details, you can ask to speak alone.
