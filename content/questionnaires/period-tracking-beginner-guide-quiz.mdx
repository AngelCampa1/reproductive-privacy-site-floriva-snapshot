The biggest mistake beginners make is trying to track everything on day one. Apps show dozens of logging fields: mood, energy, sleep, cervical mucus, cravings, exercise. New users either fill everything in for a week and stop, or skip everything because it's overwhelming.

This assessment helps you start with the right amount.

## Question 1: How Long Have You Been Menstruating?

**Where are you in your cycle history?**

- **Option A: Less than a year.** Your cycles are likely still establishing a pattern. Irregular cycle lengths are normal at this stage. Focus only on start dates.
- **Option B: One to three years.** Your pattern is forming but may still vary. Start and end dates plus flow intensity give you useful baseline data.
- **Option C: More than three years.** You have an established pattern, even if you haven't tracked it formally. You can add more detail from the start.

If your cycles are new, resist the urge to interpret patterns from three data points. You need six or more recorded cycles before patterns become meaningful.

## Question 2: Have You Tracked Before?

**Is this your first time using any kind of tracking?**

- **Option A: First time ever.** Start with the absolute minimum. Period start dates only. Add complexity after you've built the habit.
- **Option B: I've used a calendar or notes but not an app.** You already have the habit. An app just structures what you're doing. You can start with a few more fields.
- **Option C: I've used another app before.** You know what inputs are useful to you. Focus on moving your preferences to a better app. See [rebuilding your history](/compare/alternatives/rebuild-period-history-after-switching-apps).

The goal is consistency over completeness. Three months of period start dates is more useful than two weeks of detailed logging followed by silence.

## Question 3: What Do You Actually Want to Know?

**What question are you trying to answer by tracking?**

- **Option A: When will my next period start?** Log period start and end dates. That's all the app needs for prediction.
- **Option B: Are my symptoms connected to my cycle?** Log the specific symptoms you're curious about, but only those. Logging everything scatters your attention.
- **Option C: Is something wrong with my cycle?** Log start dates, end dates, flow intensity, and any specific symptom your doctor asks about. Bring the export to your appointment.
- **Option D: I'm not sure yet.** Start with period dates. You'll figure out what else matters after two or three cycles.

Tracking without a question produces data without insight. Even a vague question like "do I always get headaches before my period?" gives your tracking a direction.

## Question 4: How Much Time Will You Spend on This Per Day?

**Be honest about your logging tolerance.**

- **Option A: Under 10 seconds.** Tap "period started" or "period ended." That's your sweet spot. Don't add daily fields.
- **Option B: About 30 seconds.** You can mark period days and one or two daily observations (mood, pain level).
- **Option C: One to two minutes.** You have room for detailed symptom logging, maybe temperature, maybe notes.

Match your input commitment to your actual daily willingness. An app that asks for two minutes when you have ten seconds will go unused by month two.

## Question 5: Do You Want to Share Data with a Doctor?

**Will you use this data in a medical context?**

- **Option A: No, this is just for me.** Track what's personally useful. No need for clinical-level detail.
- **Option B: Maybe eventually.** Make sure your app can export data. Even simple period dates are useful to a provider if they're consistent.
- **Option C: Yes, my doctor asked me to track.** Ask your doctor exactly which data points they want. Log those and nothing else. Bring the [data export](/app-guides/floriva-data-export-guide) to your appointment.

If a doctor is involved, let them set the tracking scope. Their clinical question should define your logging inputs.

## Question 6: How Important Is Privacy to You?

**Do you care who can see your cycle data?**

- **Option A: I haven't thought about it.** Think about it now. Your period data is health data. Read the [privacy audit quiz](/tools/quiz/how-private-is-your-period-tracker) before choosing an app.
- **Option B: Somewhat important.** At minimum, choose an app that doesn't require a social sign-in and read the privacy policy.
- **Option C: Very important.** Choose an app with on-device storage and no account requirement. See [best private trackers](/resources/best/best-private-period-tracker-apps).

Privacy matters more for beginners than they realize, because the data you start logging today accumulates. Switching apps later means either losing history or trusting the old app to actually delete it.

## Your Results

**Your starter kit based on your answers:**

**Mostly A answers (minimal tracker):** Start with period start dates only. Choose a simple, private app. Don't touch fertility features, symptom logs, or daily tracking. Build the habit first. After three consistent months, decide whether to add anything.

**Mix of A and B answers (light tracker):** Log period start and end dates, flow intensity, and one symptom category that matters to you. Set a reminder to log on period days only. Skip daily logging for now.

**Mostly B and C answers (structured tracker):** You're ready for daily tracking with a focused set of inputs. Choose your fields based on your specific question (Question 3), set up your app with only those fields visible, and hide everything else. Review your data after three cycles to see if the pattern answers your question.

**Option C on Question 5 (medical tracker):** Your doctor's instructions override everything else. Track exactly what they ask for. Choose an app with [data export](/app-guides/floriva-data-export-guide) and bring your records to appointments.

The best tracking system is the one you'll actually use for six months. Start small. Add later.
