Switching period trackers is not free. You lose prediction accuracy during the transition. You may lose historical data entirely. You spend time learning a new interface. These costs are real, and they're why most people stay with apps that no longer serve them well.

This assessment helps you weigh those costs against the reasons to move.

## Question 1: Why Are You Considering a Switch?

**What triggered this evaluation?**

- **Option A: A news story about my app's data practices.** A specific, documented concern. Worth investigating whether the issue is real and whether it affects your data.
- **Option B: My app changed its pricing, features, or privacy policy.** Material changes to the deal you signed up for. Read the new terms and compare.
- **Option C: I moved to a state with reproductive health restrictions.** Your location changed your risk level. Data architecture that was fine before may not be fine now.
- **Option D: The app just doesn't work well for me anymore.** Feature mismatch, poor predictions, annoying interface. Functional reasons are valid.
- **Option E: General unease, no specific trigger.** Vague dissatisfaction is worth examining but may not justify the switching cost.

Options A through C are structural reasons that probably justify a switch. Option D is a quality-of-life reason that might justify it. Option E needs more investigation.

## Question 2: What Would You Lose by Switching?

**How much history is at stake?**

- **Option A: Less than six months of data.** Minimal loss. Your new app will establish patterns quickly.
- **Option B: Six months to two years.** Meaningful history, but rebuildable from calendar or memory for the basics. See [rebuilding history](/compare/alternatives/rebuild-period-history-after-switching-apps).
- **Option C: More than two years.** Significant history. Check whether your current app exports data and whether your target app imports it.

The practical impact of lost history is about three to six months of reduced prediction accuracy in your new app. After that, the new app has enough data to match the old one's predictions.

## Question 3: Can Your Current App Export Your Data?

**Check the settings menu of your current app.**

- **Option A: Yes, full export available.** Good. Export before you delete anything. Your history is portable.
- **Option B: Partial export or PDF only.** Better than nothing. A PDF of your cycle history lets you manually enter key dates into a new app.
- **Option C: No export option.** Your data is locked in. You'll need to screenshot or manually record key dates before switching.
- **Option D: I don't need to export because the data never left my phone anyway.** On-device apps: the data goes where your phone goes.

Export your data even if you're not sure you'll switch. Having a backup costs nothing.

## Question 4: What Does Your Ideal App Look Like?

**After using your current tracker, what do you now know you want?**

- **Option A: Fewer features, simpler interface.** You're over-instrumented. Look for minimal trackers.
- **Option B: Better privacy and on-device storage.** You want structural data protection, not just policy promises.
- **Option C: More features for fertility, symptoms, or conditions.** Your tracking needs have grown beyond what your current app offers.
- **Option D: Lower cost or no subscription.** Pricing changed or you want off the subscription model.

Knowing what you want prevents you from jumping from one mediocre app to another. Define your non-negotiables before browsing alternatives.

## Question 5: Are You Willing to Rebuild?

**Switching requires a setup period. Are you ready for that?**

- **Option A: Yes, I'll spend an afternoon setting up and entering historical dates.** You're committed. The transition will be smooth.
- **Option B: I'll set up the new app but I won't re-enter old data.** Fine. Accept three to six months of less accurate predictions.
- **Option C: I want a simple migration with no effort.** This does not exist in period tracking. No major app-to-app migration path is fully automated.

Set expectations before you start. The rebuild is a one-time cost that pays off over the life of using a better app.

## Question 6: Is Your Current App's Data a Liability?

**Beyond switching, should you delete your data from the old app?**

- **Option A: Yes, the old app stores data on servers in a way that concerns me.** Delete your account after exporting. Don't just uninstall the app.
- **Option B: The old app is fine. I just want something better.** No urgency to delete, but consider it anyway. There's no benefit to leaving your data on a former provider's servers.
- **Option C: The old app already uses on-device storage.** Nothing to delete server-side. Uninstalling the app removes the data.

If you're leaving an app over privacy concerns, the last step is making sure your data doesn't stay behind.

## Your Results

**Decision framework based on your answers:**

**Switch now (A/B/C on Question 1, B on Question 4, A on Question 5):** You have a structural reason to leave, you know what you want, and you're willing to do the work. Export your data, set up your new app, delete the old account. Start with the [best private trackers list](/resources/best/best-private-period-tracker-apps).

**Switch soon (D on Question 1, any answer on Question 4, B on Question 5):** You have functional reasons to leave but no urgency. Take time to evaluate options. Use the [recommender quiz](/tools/quiz/which-period-tracker-is-right-for-you) to narrow your choices, then switch when you're ready.

**Investigate first (E on Question 1, C on Question 3):** Your concern is vague and your data isn't portable. Run the [privacy audit](/tools/quiz/how-private-is-your-period-tracker) on your current app first. If the results show real problems, come back to this assessment with a specific reason.

**Stay put (no structural concern, C on Question 5):** If your reason is vague and you're unwilling to rebuild, switching will create more frustration than it solves. Revisit when either the reason clarifies or your willingness increases.

The right time to switch is when the cost of staying exceeds the cost of moving. For privacy and legal concerns, that math has already tipped.
