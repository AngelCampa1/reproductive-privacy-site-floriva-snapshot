There's no single right way to track your cycle. Some people need a daily routine with thermometers and charting. Others need a button that says "period started." Both work. The method should fit the person.

This assessment finds that fit.

## Question 1: What's Your Tracking Goal?

**Why are you tracking at all?**

- **Option A: Period prediction.** You want to know when your period is coming. Calendar methods and app algorithms handle this.
- **Option B: Fertility awareness for conception.** You want to find your fertile window. This requires biomarker tracking beyond calendar dates.
- **Option C: Fertility awareness for birth control.** You want to find and avoid your fertile window. This requires the most accurate method and the most consistent practice.
- **Option D: Health monitoring for a condition.** You're tracking patterns for PCOS, endometriosis, PMDD, or another condition. Your doctor's needs should define your method.

Option C has the highest accuracy requirements. If you're relying on cycle tracking to prevent pregnancy, the calendar method alone is not enough.

## Question 2: What's Your Morning Routine Like?

**BBT requires measurement at the same time each morning before getting out of bed.**

- **Option A: I wake up at inconsistent times and I'm out of bed right away.** BBT will be unreliable for you. Consider mucus-only methods or algorithm-based tracking.
- **Option B: I wake up at a consistent time and can spend 60 seconds with a thermometer before moving.** BBT is doable. You'll get usable data.
- **Option C: I use a wearable that tracks temperature automatically overnight.** Wearable BBT removes the manual measurement step. Your data will be more consistent than with manual BBT.

If BBT doesn't fit your life, don't force it. An inconsistently measured temperature is worse than no temperature. It adds noise that can mislead your charting.

## Question 3: How Comfortable Are You with Body Literacy?

**Some methods require observing cervical mucus or cervix position.**

- **Option A: I'm not comfortable with that yet.** Calendar-based or app-algorithm methods don't require physical self-examination.
- **Option B: I'm willing to learn.** Cervical mucus observation is a skill that takes one to three cycles to develop. Resources and instructors can help.
- **Option C: I already observe cervical mucus or other biomarkers.** You can use symptom-based or sympto-thermal methods right away.

Comfort develops with practice. But if you're not ready for body-literacy methods, that's not a failure. It's information about which method fits right now.

## Question 4: How Regular Are Your Cycles?

**Cycle regularity determines which methods are reliable for you.**

- **Option A: Very regular (variation under 3 days).** Calendar methods are more reliable for you than for most people. Algorithm-based predictions will be reasonably accurate.
- **Option B: Moderately regular (variation of 3-7 days).** Calendar-only methods become less reliable. Adding at least one biomarker (BBT or mucus) improves accuracy.
- **Option C: Irregular (variation over 7 days, or cycles outside 21-35 days).** Calendar methods are unreliable. Real-time biomarker tracking (BBT plus mucus) is the only method that adapts to your actual physiology each cycle.

Irregular cycles don't prevent effective tracking. They just eliminate the shortcut methods and require daily observation.

## Question 5: How Much Time Can You Commit Daily?

**Be realistic.**

- **Option A: Zero daily time. I'll log when my period starts and stops.** Calendar method or app algorithm. Simple and sustainable.
- **Option B: One minute each morning.** Enough for BBT. Add cervical mucus observation for sympto-thermal charting.
- **Option C: Two to five minutes for daily charting, observations, and notes.** Full sympto-thermal method with detailed records. Maximum data, maximum accuracy.

Sustainability beats accuracy. A method you use inconsistently for three months is less useful than a simpler method you maintain for years.

## Question 6: Do You Want the App to Interpret for You?

**How much analysis should the app provide?**

- **Option A: I want the app to tell me when my period is coming and nothing else.** Algorithm-based prediction. Let the app do the math.
- **Option B: I want the app to show me my data, but I'll interpret it.** Charting tools that display your BBT curve and mucus patterns without drawing conclusions.
- **Option C: I want the app to identify my fertile window and ovulation day.** Algorithm interpretation of your biomarker data. Useful but never a substitute for understanding the method yourself.

Algorithm interpretations are estimates. If you're using fertility awareness for birth control, learn to read your own chart rather than relying only on app predictions.

## Your Results

**Your method match:**

**Calendar/Algorithm (mostly A answers):** You want simplicity. Log your period start dates and let the app predict. This is enough for period prediction and general awareness. It is not reliable enough for fertility-based birth control. Set up a [simple tracker](/tools/quiz/which-period-tracker-is-right-for-you) and start logging.

**BBT-focused (B on Questions 2 and 5, A on Question 3):** You can handle daily temperature measurement but aren't ready for mucus observation. Temperature-only tracking confirms ovulation after the fact but doesn't predict it in advance. Start with [BBT tracking setup](/app-guides/floriva-basal-body-temperature-tracking).

**Sympto-thermal (B or C on Questions 2, 3, and 5):** You're ready for the most complete method. Combine BBT with cervical mucus observation for both predictive and confirmatory fertility data. Learn the method from a certified instructor, then use your app as a charting tool. See [fertility awareness method setup](/app-guides/floriva-fertility-awareness-method-setup).

**Condition-specific (D on Question 1):** Your tracking method should be defined by your healthcare provider's diagnostic needs. Ask them what data points they want, then set up your tracker accordingly. Consider [cycle syncing setup](/app-guides/floriva-cycle-syncing-setup) if that fits your plan.

Pick the method you'll stick with for at least six months. You can always add complexity later. You can't recover from tracking fatigue.
