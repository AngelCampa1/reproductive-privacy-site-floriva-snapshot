## What Perimenopause Actually Is

Perimenopause is not a single event but a multi-year hormonal transition. The ovaries progressively produce less estrogen and progesterone, but not in a smooth, linear decline. Estrogen levels fluctuate, sometimes spiking higher than in earlier reproductive years, sometimes dropping sharply, before ultimately settling at the lower post-menopausal baseline.

This erratic hormonal landscape is why perimenopausal symptoms and period changes can feel so unpredictable. The cycle, which may have been fairly regular for decades, starts reflecting an underlying hormonal process that is no longer consistent.

## How Periods Change During Perimenopause

### Cycle Length Variation

One of the earliest and most reliable signs of perimenopause is increasing variability in cycle length. A person whose cycles were consistently 28 to 30 days may start experiencing cycles of 21 days followed by cycles of 45 days, with no predictable pattern.

This happens because ovulation timing becomes less regular. In early perimenopause, some cycles may still ovulate normally, while others are anovulatory. As perimenopause progresses, anovulatory cycles become more frequent.

### Changes in Flow

Flow can go in either direction during perimenopause:

**Heavier and longer:** Anovulatory cycles mean no progesterone surge to limit uterine lining growth. The lining can build up over a longer-than-normal span and then shed in a heavier-than-normal bleed. Clotting becomes more common. Very heavy bleeding, soaking through a pad or tampon in under an hour, or passing large clots, warrants medical evaluation. Not because it is necessarily dangerous, but because causes beyond perimenopause (polyps, fibroids, endometrial hyperplasia) need to be ruled out.

**Lighter and shorter:** Some cycles, particularly those where estrogen is lower, produce a thinner lining and a lighter, shorter period or even spotting.

### Skipped Periods

As anovulatory cycles increase in frequency, periods begin to be missed. In early perimenopause this might be an occasional skipped month. In later perimenopause, gaps of several months become more common before the final cessation.

### Symptom Changes

Beyond the period itself, perimenopause brings a range of symptoms driven by estrogen fluctuation:

- Hot flashes and night sweats, often beginning before significant cycle irregularity
- Sleep disruption, frequently tied to night sweats
- Mood changes, including increased anxiety or low mood
- Vaginal dryness as estrogen declines
- Worsening or changed PMS symptoms in some people

Some people find that PMS symptoms that were manageable earlier in life become more intense during perimenopause. Others find they diminish.

## Why Tracking Matters During Perimenopause

Perimenopausal changes are easier to understand in context. Without a record, it is difficult to know:

- How long your gaps between periods actually are (versus how long they feel)
- Whether your flow is consistently heavier, or only occasionally
- Which symptoms correspond to bleeding and which occur between periods
- How your pattern is progressing over time

This record is also clinically useful. When you see a provider about perimenopausal symptoms, the difference between "my periods have been irregular lately" and "my cycles over the past year have ranged from 18 to 67 days, I've had two periods heavier than normal with clotting, and I've had hot flashes for about 8 months" is significant. The second statement comes from tracking.

### What to Track During Perimenopause

- Period start and end dates (first day of bleeding, last day)
- Flow intensity (light, moderate, heavy, very heavy) each day
- Clotting: present or absent and approximate size
- Any spotting or bleeding between periods
- Hot flash frequency and intensity
- Sleep quality
- Mood and energy
- Any new or changed symptoms

## Changes That Always Warrant Medical Evaluation

Not every change in perimenopause is simply hormonal transition. The following should be discussed with a provider promptly:

- Bleeding heavy enough to soak through protection in under an hour
- Periods lasting more than 7 days
- Bleeding or spotting after sex
- New spotting between periods
- Any bleeding after 12 consecutive months without a period (this is postmenopausal bleeding, which is always evaluated)

These changes can have causes including uterine polyps, fibroids, or endometrial changes that are unrelated to perimenopause and benefit from early detection.

## The Privacy Dimension

Perimenopausal health data is among the more sensitive cycle data someone can generate. It reveals age-related health information, reproductive status, and detailed symptom patterns that span years. For people in workplaces or situations where health status could affect them, or for people who simply value controlling their own medical information, the question of where this data is stored matters.

Period tracker apps that sync data to cloud servers store years of perimenopausal history, including flow changes, symptom patterns, and gap durations. That data is not covered by HIPAA. Flo Health's 2021 FTC settlement demonstrated that even period tracking data can be shared with third-party advertisers without users' meaningful awareness.

An app that stores data only on your device generates no cloud record for third parties to access. For a multi-year health transition like perimenopause, that architectural difference is worth considering when choosing a tracker.
