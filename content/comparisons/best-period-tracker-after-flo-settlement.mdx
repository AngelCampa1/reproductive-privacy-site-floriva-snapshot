Flo shared your period data with Facebook and Google. The FTC confirmed it in 2021. A $59.5M combined settlement resolved the class action in September 2025. If you're reading this, you're probably deciding what to use instead.

Here are four alternatives evaluated on the only question that matters: what happens to your data.

## Why Users Are Leaving Flo

The FTC found that Flo shared sensitive health data (period dates, pregnancy status, symptom information) with Facebook, Google, and third-party analytics firms through embedded SDKs. Users who logged their most intimate health data did so believing it was private. It wasn't.

Flo launched Anonymous Mode in response, but it requires a premium subscription. Privacy as an upsell, after the company was caught violating it for free-tier users. The architecture remains cloud-based. Your data still lives on Flo's servers.

For a full breakdown, see [Flo app alternative](/compare/alternatives/flo-app-alternative) and the [Flo vs Floriva data comparison](/compare/versus/flo-vs-floriva-data-comparison).

## Option 1: Floriva. On-Device With a Real Way to Switch Phones

Floriva keeps core data on your device. No account is needed for core tracking. No analytics SDKs track cycle data. There is no sync. If you want your history on a new phone, you export an encrypted backup file, locked with a passphrase you choose, and import it yourself.

**Why choose Floriva:** iOS and Android with an encrypted backup file solves part of the main problem with on-device trackers: data loss when you change phones. It is a manual step, not automatic sync, but it works. No account required. You can start tracking without an email address.

**The tradeoff:** Paid app with no free tier. Closed source. Newer app with a smaller prediction dataset than Flo's.

Floriva is a [zero-knowledge tracker](/resources/guides/what-is-zero-knowledge-period-tracker). The company cannot read your cycle data because core records stay on your device and the backup file you export is encrypted before it ever leaves your control.

## Option 2: Euki. Free On-Device Tracking

Euki is backed by the International Planned Parenthood Federation. Free. On-device storage. No account. Includes a decoy screen that shows fake content if someone opens your app, useful for physical device privacy that software architecture alone doesn't address.

**Why choose Euki:** Free, on-device, and includes broader sexual health resources beyond cycle tracking. The decoy screen is unique among period trackers.

**The tradeoff:** No sync. Lose your phone, lose your data. Development updates are less frequent than commercial apps.

## Option 3: Drip. Open-Source Verification

Drip is free, open-source, and Android-only. The source code is on GitHub under GPLv3. You don't need to trust anyone's privacy claims, you can read the code and verify that the app doesn't send data anywhere.

**Why choose Drip:** The source code is public. You can verify the privacy claims yourself rather than trusting a policy statement.

**The tradeoff:** Android only. No iOS version. Minimal UI. Small volunteer development team. Functional but not polished.

## Option 4: Clue. Cloud with GDPR

Clue is the strongest privacy option among cloud-based trackers. German-incorporated, GDPR-regulated, with public commitments to resist law enforcement requests for reproductive health data. No FTC enforcement history.

**Why choose Clue:** Feature-rich, well-established, free tier, iOS and Android. GDPR provides meaningful protections that US law doesn't. For users comfortable with cloud storage under strong regulation, Clue is the best of that category.

**The tradeoff:** Cloud-based. Your data exists on servers in Germany. MLAT requests can reach German companies. GDPR slows legal access but doesn't eliminate it. Clue is still a company holding your data, better-regulated than Flo, but architecturally similar.

## How to Decide

**Maximum privacy:** Floriva (if you need iOS or a way to carry your history to a new phone) or Drip (if you're on Android and want open-source verification).

**Maximum privacy + free:** Euki (iOS + Android) or Drip (Android only).

**Most features + reasonable privacy:** Clue. Understand that cloud-based means your data exists on servers, even well-regulated ones.

**Switching from Flo:** Export your Flo data first. See the [switching from Flo guide](/resources/guides/switching-from-flo-complete-guide) for steps. None of these alternatives import directly from Flo, you'll likely re-enter historical data manually.

## The Privacy Bottom Line

Flo's settlement proved that privacy policies are only as good as the company's willingness to follow them. Flo promised privacy and shared data with Facebook. The FTC intervened. A $59.5M settlement was reached. And Flo's servers still hold user data.

The lesson isn't to find a cloud app with a better privacy policy. The lesson is that on-device storage eliminates the risk entirely. When the data isn't on a server, there's nothing to share with Facebook, nothing to subpoena, and nothing to settle a class action over.
