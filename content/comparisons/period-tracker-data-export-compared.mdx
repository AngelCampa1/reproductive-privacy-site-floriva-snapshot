## Why Data Export Is a Feature That Matters

Most people do not think about data export when they choose a period tracker. They think about it when they want to leave. By then, the friction of extracting your data, or the discovery that you cannot get it all out, becomes the reason many people stay with apps they no longer trust.

Data export is not a technical nicety. It is the difference between owning your health data and renting access to it. Here is how four apps handle it.

## Floriva: Your Data Was Never Anywhere Else

Floriva's data export is different from the others in this comparison because core records are device-held. You are not waiting on a cloud account request for those core records.

Export is built for taking your record with you. Check the current app for exact fields and file formats before relying on it for a clinic workflow or app migration.

Because the data lives locally, you can also access the underlying files directly. Your cycle history exists as files on your phone, not as rows in a remote database controlled by someone else.

The trade-off: because there is no cloud backup, export is also your backup strategy. If your device is lost or damaged without a local backup, the data is gone. This is the cost of true data ownership. No one else has a copy, for better and worse.

## Clue: Structured but Server-Dependent

Clue offers data export through its settings menu and supports GDPR data requests for EU users. The export produces CSV files covering cycle dates, symptoms, and tracking data. The process is relatively straightforward for a cloud-based app.

The limitations: export may not capture every custom tracking field. The data comes from Clue's servers, so there is processing time between requesting and receiving your export. For users with years of tracking data, this can take hours.

Clue's GDPR compliance means the company takes data portability seriously as a legal obligation. This is better than apps that technically offer export but make it nearly impossible to find or use.

After exporting, deleting your data from Clue requires a separate account deletion request. Export and deletion are separate processes with separate timelines.

## Flo: Available but Buried

Flo provides data export, but finding it requires navigating deep into settings menus. The option exists because regulatory requirements (GDPR, CCPA) mandate it. The placement suggests it is not a feature Flo wants users to discover easily.

Export produces CSV or PDF files. The PDF option gives you a human-readable document suitable for sharing with a healthcare provider. The CSV is more useful for importing into another app.

Processing time for Flo's export can be significant, ranging from hours to days for large datasets. The company processes these requests server-side, and volume affects speed.

Not all data fields may be included in the standard export. Custom tracking data, notes, and certain symptom categories may require a separate GDPR request to fully extract.

For an app with a documented history of data sharing (FTC enforcement, $59.5M settlement), the difficulty of getting your own data out creates an uncomfortable irony: third parties got your data easily, but you have to work for it.

## Natural Cycles: Export Exists, Translation Is Hard

Natural Cycles supports data export, producing CSV files with temperature readings and cycle data. GDPR compliance applies for EU users.

The complication is proprietary data. Natural Cycles' core value is its FDA-cleared algorithm that classifies days as red (fertile) or green (not fertile). This classification is proprietary. When you export, you get raw temperatures, but the algorithmic interpretation that made those temperatures useful does not translate to another app.

Switching from Natural Cycles means losing the specific algorithm you have been relying on for contraception decisions. Your temperature data travels with you. The intelligence built on top of it does not. This is a form of lock-in through intellectual property rather than data captivity.

The export request process takes time, as with other server-based apps. You submit a request and wait.

## The Switching Cost Reality

No period tracker makes switching to a competitor easy. This is partly technical (different apps use different data schemas) and partly strategic (user retention benefits from friction).

The practical ranking for export ease:

1. **Floriva**: Data is local. Export is instant. Format is standard. You already have everything.
2. **Clue**: Export is findable and GDPR-backed. Processing takes time but works.
3. **Natural Cycles**: Export works but proprietary algorithm data does not transfer. You keep raw data, lose the intelligence.
4. **Flo**: Export exists but is buried. Processing is slow. The company's relationship with your data has historically been more permissive than yours.

## What This Means for Choosing an App

If you value data portability, choose an app that stores data locally or makes export straightforward. The best time to think about leaving an app is before you start using it.

Floriva's local storage means data portability is the default state, not a feature you have to invoke. Your data is files on your device. You do not need to ask anyone for access to your own health records.

Every other app in this comparison requires you to request your data from a company. That request can be slow, incomplete, or made more difficult over time as business priorities change. Your leverage as a user exists only as long as a regulation compels the company to comply.
