Ovia is free because your employer pays for it. The product Ovia sells to employers includes aggregate data about workforce reproductive health. That is the business model. Your period data, pregnancy status, and fertility treatment information contribute to reports purchased by the company that signs your paycheck.

Floriva is a paid app because you are the customer. Core records stay on your device. No employer analytics. No aggregate employer reports. Less readable company-side data.

## The Core Difference

Ovia's customer is your employer, not you. The app is a B2B health benefit. Employers contract with Ovia to offer fertility and pregnancy tracking as a workplace benefit, and in return receive aggregate analytics about their workforce's reproductive health.

Floriva's customer is you. The subscription funds the product. There is no third party receiving data about your cycle, pregnancy, or fertility treatment.

This is not a minor policy distinction. It defines who the product serves and what happens to the data.

## Data Architecture

**Ovia** requires an account and stores data on cloud servers. Your cycle data, pregnancy milestones, fertility treatment logs, and symptom records exist on Ovia's infrastructure. Ovia states that employer reports contain aggregate, de-identified data, not individual records with employee names.

The de-identification claim deserves scrutiny. In a department of 15 people, aggregate data showing "one employee began fertility treatment in March" may not need a name attached to be identifying. Small-group analytics create re-identification risk regardless of technical de-identification.

**Floriva** keeps core records on your device. No account is required for core tracking. There is no readable central cycle database, analytics pipeline, or aggregate employer report. There is no sync, so no cycle data is transmitted at all.

## Features

Ovia has genuine product strength in pregnancy and fertility tracking. Detailed week-by-week pregnancy content, fertility treatment logging, and postpartum tracking reflect its focus on the full reproductive health journey. The content library is extensive.

Floriva focuses on cycle tracking, symptom logging, and pattern analysis. It does not have Ovia's pregnancy-specific content depth or fertility treatment features.

If you are actively pregnant and want a guided pregnancy tracking experience with content and milestone tracking, Ovia delivers more features. If you want your reproductive health data to stay on your device and out of employer analytics, Floriva is the only option between these two.

## Pricing

**Ovia:** Free to employees. Employer-paid. The zero cost to you reflects the fact that you are not the customer. The employer pays because the employer receives value: workforce health analytics that inform benefit design and cost projection.

**Floriva:** Paid subscription, no free tier, paid by you. No employer relationship. No third-party funding. The cost is the price of being the customer rather than the product.

## Who Should Choose Floriva vs Ovia

**Choose Ovia if:** you want a free pregnancy and fertility tracker, you are comfortable with your employer receiving aggregate reproductive health data, and you value the detailed pregnancy content and features. Ovia is a capable product if the business model does not concern you.

**Choose Floriva if:** you do not want your reproductive health data flowing to your employer in any form, aggregate or otherwise. If you are considering fertility treatment, pregnancy, or any situation where your employer knowing about your reproductive health could create professional consequences, Floriva's on-device architecture eliminates that data flow entirely. See [how period tracker apps collect data](/resources/guides/how-period-tracker-apps-collect-data) for broader context.

## The Privacy Bottom Line

The question with Ovia is not whether the app works well. It does. The question is whether your employer should have any window into your reproductive health, even an aggregate one. Ovia's business model requires that window to exist.

Floriva's architecture narrows that window. Core records stay on your device, and Floriva does not build employer reports from your cycle history. That architecture choice is worth paying for to anyone who wants their reproductive health separated from their employment relationship.
