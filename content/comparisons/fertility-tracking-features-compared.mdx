## What Fertility Tracking Demands

Fertility tracking is more data-intensive than basic period tracking. Instead of logging cycle start and end dates, fertility tracking requires daily temperature readings, cervical mucus observations, ovulation test results, and sometimes cervical position checks. The data feeds algorithms that identify fertile and infertile windows.

The stakes are higher too. People use fertility data to conceive or to avoid conception. Accuracy matters more than it does for basic period prediction. The data itself, including ovulation timing, sexual activity logs, and pregnancy attempts, is among the most intimate health data a person generates.

Here is how four apps handle fertility tracking, what each does well, and what the privacy implications are.

## Floriva: Full FAM Toolkit, Local Storage

Floriva supports private daily fertility notes and pattern review. Check current onboarding for exact fields, charts, imports, and exports before relying on a specific BBT, cervical mucus, or OPK workflow.

Floriva is not FDA-cleared for contraceptive use. The trade-off is clear: you get local-first core records and no sync at all, but not regulatory validation for contraceptive effectiveness.

For users practicing FAM with instructor guidance, Floriva provides the charting infrastructure without the cloud dependency. Your fertility data exists only on your phone.

## Natural Cycles: FDA-Cleared, Cloud-Required

Natural Cycles is the only FDA-cleared digital contraceptive. Its algorithm analyzes BBT data to classify days as red (fertile, use protection) or green (not fertile). The clinical evidence backing this clearance is substantial, with peer-reviewed studies on efficacy.

The algorithm runs on Natural Cycles' servers. This is not optional. You cannot use Natural Cycles without an account, without internet connectivity, and without sending your temperature data to their cloud. The FDA clearance is tied to the specific server-side algorithm.

Natural Cycles also sells a proprietary thermometer designed for the app. It works directly with the app but creates hardware dependency on a single company.

The privacy trade-off is explicit: the most clinically validated fertility prediction requires giving a company your complete fertility dataset, stored on their servers, linked to your identity.

## Premom: Best OPK Integration, Worst Data Record

Premom's standout feature is OPK photo reading. You photograph your ovulation test strip, and the app's AI analyzes the line darkness to quantify your LH surge. This is genuinely useful. Reading OPK strips by eye is subjective; automated analysis reduces interpretation error.

Premom integrates with Easy@Home test strips and connected devices from the same parent company, Easy Healthcare. The ecosystem is vertically integrated from test strips to app.

The data handling record is the problem. The FTC investigated Easy Healthcare for sharing user fertility data with third parties. Fertility data, including when you ovulate, when you have sex, and whether you are trying to conceive, allegedly flowed to companies beyond what users consented to.

Premom works well as a fertility tool. Whether you trust Easy Healthcare with your fertility data is a separate question with documented reasons for concern.

## Kindara: Gone, Lessons Remain

Kindara was a dedicated FAM app with strong charting for BBT and cervical mucus. The Wink thermometer provided Bluetooth BBT sync. The community was engaged and knowledgeable about fertility awareness methods.

Kindara was acquired and discontinued. Users lost access to their fertility charts. Wink thermometers lost their companion software. Years of daily temperature readings and cervical mucus observations, data that takes real discipline to collect, disappeared when the servers went offline.

Kindara is included in this comparison not as a current option but as evidence. Cloud-dependent fertility tracking means your data's existence depends on a company's business decisions. Kindara's users learned this the hard way.

## The Privacy Dimension of Fertility Data

Fertility data is not like fitness data. It reveals whether you are trying to conceive, when you ovulate, your sexual activity timing, and potentially whether a pregnancy was interrupted. In states with abortion restrictions, this data has legal significance.

Natural Cycles stores this on their servers with your identity attached. Premom stores it on servers with a documented history of data sharing. Kindara stored it on servers that no longer exist.

Floriva keeps core fertility records local-first. No account is required for core tracking, and Floriva has no readable central fertility database.

## Choosing Based on Priorities

**If FDA-cleared contraception matters most**: Natural Cycles is the only option with that clearance. Accept the cloud storage as part of the deal.

**If OPK photo analysis matters most**: Premom does this best. Weigh the feature against the data handling history.

**If data privacy matters most**: Floriva is the strongest option in this comparison for local-first core fertility records. You lose FDA clearance and automated OPK reading. You reduce readable company-side exposure for your most intimate health data.

**If Kindara was your app**: Your data may be gone unless you exported it. Floriva's local-first storage reduces shutdown risk for core records because those records live on your device, not only on a company's servers.
