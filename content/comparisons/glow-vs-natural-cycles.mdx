## Different tools, competing for the same user

Glow and Natural Cycles both appear in searches for "fertility tracking app," but they are built for different things.

Glow is a cycle and wellness tracker with partner-sharing features and fertility calendars. Its revenue model involves data monetization. The company has documented relationships with insurers and third-party advertisers who receive health data. The free tier exists because the data has commercial value.

Natural Cycles is an FDA De Novo cleared contraceptive device that uses basal body temperature to determine fertile and non-fertile days. The subscription funds the product. There is no advertising model and no documented enforcement actions for data misuse.

The comparison matters if you need general cycle tracking and are deciding whether the data risks of either app are worth it. For contraception, Natural Cycles has regulatory backing. Glow does not.

## The data risk gap

Both apps require accounts. Both store data server-side. Neither offers on-device architecture.

Glow's data risk differs from Natural Cycles' in kind. Glow's business involves monetizing health data commercially. Insurers and advertisers receive information derived from your fertility and cycle logs. That is a form of data exposure separate from law enforcement access, and users have no mechanism to opt out.

Natural Cycles operates under GDPR and has no documented commercial data-sharing problems. Its server-side exposure is primarily legal: a court order can compel production of user data. That risk applies to all server-based period trackers.

In states with abortion restrictions, both apps create records that can be subpoenaed. A prosecutor with a valid court order can compel either company to produce user data. A company holding no data cannot produce any.

## Temperature tracking: what Natural Cycles actually requires

Natural Cycles requires active daily participation. You measure basal body temperature every morning before getting out of bed, using a specific thermometer accurate to two decimal places. The algorithm uses that data to predict fertile windows.

Glow lets you log data when convenient. Natural Cycles does not. Natural Cycles' effectiveness depends on consistent measurement. Missed readings reduce accuracy. The app is not appropriate for users who want hands-off tracking.
