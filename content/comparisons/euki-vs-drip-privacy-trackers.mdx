## Two Free Apps That Took Privacy Seriously

Euki and Drip arrived at [on-device](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker/) privacy from different directions. Euki was built by Ibis Reproductive Health, a nonprofit focused on reproductive health access. The app's design choices, no account, no server, no analytics, reflect the organization's specific concern for users in contexts where reproductive health data [could be used against them in prosecutions](/resources/guides/reproductive-data-prosecutions-after-dobbs/).

Drip was built as an [open source](/resources/guides/open-source-period-tracker-explained/) project. The design choice that matters most is transparency: the source code is publicly available on GitHub, which means the privacy claims can be verified by anyone with the technical ability to read code. Most period apps ask you to trust their privacy policy. Drip shows you the code.

## The Open Source Advantage

Open source is the strongest form of privacy transparency available. When Drip claims it does not transmit any data to external servers, that claim can be verified in the codebase. When [Flo](/compare/alternatives/flo-app-alternative/) claimed it did not share data with Facebook and Google, that was a policy promise that turned out to be contradicted by the behavior of embedded SDKs, the conduct that led to the [$59.5M settlement](/resources/guides/flo-period-tracker-lawsuit-settlement/).

The limitation of Drip's open source approach is platform: it is Android-only. There is no iOS version. For users who need iOS support, open source verification is not currently an option in a free tracker.

## Nonprofit vs Open Source as Trust Models

Euki's trust basis is institutional. Ibis Reproductive Health has an organizational mission that conflicts with data monetization. The nonprofit is accountable to its mission, its funders, and the communities it serves, not to advertising revenue or investor returns. This creates a durable incentive structure against data misuse.

Drip's trust basis is technical. The code can be read. You do not have to trust the developers' intentions because you can verify the implementation. For technically literate users, this is a higher assurance than any policy or mission statement.

## Feature Comparison

Both apps cover the core period tracking functions: cycle logging, prediction, and basic symptom tracking. Neither has the polished interface or feature depth of commercial apps like Flo or Clue. There are no community features, AI health insights, or detailed health reports.

For users who use a period tracker primarily to know when their period is coming and log basic symptoms, both Euki and Drip cover what matters. For users who relied on Flo's health insights, symptom correlation data, or community forums, the transition to either free app will involve meaningful feature loss.

Floriva sits between the free on-device apps and the commercial cloud apps: on-device storage and no account required, but with more feature depth than Euki or Drip and an encrypted backup file for carrying data to a new phone.
