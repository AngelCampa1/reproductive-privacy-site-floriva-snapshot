This is a comparison between two apps that made the same core decision: keep period data away from a readable company database. No account is needed for core tracking. The differences between Floriva and Euki are about everything else.

## The Core Difference

Both Floriva and Euki are [zero-knowledge period trackers](/resources/guides/what-is-zero-knowledge-period-tracker). Neither company can access your data because neither company stores it. That puts them in a different category from cloud-based apps like Flo, Clue, or Natural Cycles.

The distinction between them comes down to funding model, platform maturity, and what happens when you have more than one device.

## Data Architecture

**Floriva** stores core cycle data in your device's local storage. No account is needed for core tracking. There is no sync, so no cycle data is transmitted at all. If you want your history on a new phone, you export an encrypted backup file, protected by a passphrase only you know, and import it yourself.

**Euki** takes the same local-only approach. All data lives on the device. No account. No cloud component. Euki adds a decoy screen: if someone picks up your phone and opens the app, you can configure it to show made-up content instead of your real data.

Both apps reduce what the company can produce. Euki has no readable central records. Floriva has no readable central cycle database. Device access is a separate legal and physical risk.

The meaningful difference is what happens when you switch phones. Floriva gives you an encrypted backup file to carry your history over, a manual step, not automatic sync. Euki has no export at all, so switching means starting over.

## Features

Euki was built as a sexual and reproductive health tool, not just a period tracker. It includes content on contraception, abortion, STIs, and pregnancy alongside cycle tracking. This broader scope reflects its nonprofit mission through Women Help Women.

Floriva is focused on cycle tracking with symptom logging, predictions, and pattern analysis. It aims to be a dedicated tracking tool rather than a health resource platform.

Both apps track periods, symptoms, and fertility indicators. Neither app uses your data for advertising, analytics, or research, because neither app has access to your data.

Euki's decoy screen is a feature Floriva does not match. If physical device privacy matters, a partner, parent, or authority figure might open your phone. The decoy screen provides a layer of protection that software architecture alone does not address.

Floriva's encrypted backup file is a feature Euki does not match. If you switch phones or get a new device, you export the file from your old phone and import it on the new one. It is manual, not automatic, but it means your history does not have to start over. With Euki, a lost or broken phone means lost data, since there is no export at all.

## Pricing

**Euki:** Free. Funded by Women Help Women. No premium tier, no ads, no in-app purchases. The risk with grant-funded apps is that funding can change. If Women Help Women shifts priorities, Euki's development could slow or stop.

**Floriva:** Paid subscription, no free tier. The subscription funds ongoing development without dependency on outside organizations. The trade-off is straightforward: you pay for the app.

## Who Should Choose Floriva vs Euki

**Choose Euki if:** you want a free, on-device tracker with broader sexual health resources, you value the decoy screen for physical privacy, or you are not worried about switching phones down the road. Euki is the right choice for anyone who needs privacy without a subscription.

**Choose Floriva if:** you want a real way to carry your history to a new phone someday, you want active development with regular updates, or you prefer a subscription model over grant-dependent funding. Floriva is the right choice if that encrypted backup file matters and you are willing to pay for it.

Both are strong choices. The [best private period tracker apps](/resources/best/best-private-period-tracker-apps) list includes both. They represent two workable models for a period tracker that cannot leak your data.

## The Privacy Bottom Line

Both apps pass the basic privacy test. Euki has no company server records. Floriva has no readable central cycle database. That limits what can be handed to law enforcement, advertisers, or data brokers.

The decision comes down to secondary factors: cost, backup options, physical device privacy, development pace, and platform support. That is a better set of trade-offs than choosing between private and convenient, which is what cloud-based tracker comparisons usually come down to.
