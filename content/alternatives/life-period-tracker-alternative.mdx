## Why Users Are Leaving Life Period Tracker

Life Period Tracker by Locker Room Labs is a simple cycle tracking app available on iOS and Android. It provides basic period predictions, symptom logging, and calendar views. The app appeals to users who want something uncomplicated.

The concern is not the features. It is the data architecture and the lack of documentation around it.

Life is cloud-based. Your period data is stored on remote servers. The app's public privacy documentation does not provide detailed information about its data architecture, third-party data sharing, data retention policies, or incident response procedures. For an app handling reproductive health data, this absence of detail is itself the issue.

Small development teams face a specific challenge with health data stewardship. Security infrastructure, compliance resources, legal teams, and incident response capabilities require investment that scales with the sensitivity of the data. A period tracker holds some of the most intimate information a person generates. The governance bar should match the data sensitivity, regardless of team size.

## What Life Period Tracker Does With Your Data

Life stores your period and symptom data on cloud servers. Three risks follow from cloud-based storage of reproductive health data, regardless of the developer's intentions.

Legal access: data stored on a company's servers can be obtained through subpoenas, court orders, or law enforcement requests. In states with abortion restrictions, server-stored period data has become a documented category of legally discoverable evidence.

Policy changes: a company can alter its privacy policy at any time, changing what it does with data already collected. Users who agreed to one set of terms may find their data governed by different terms with no practical recourse.

Security exposure: every server-stored dataset is a potential breach target. Small teams may lack the security infrastructure that reproductive health data requires: penetration testing, access controls, encrypted storage, and audit logging.

Life's limited privacy documentation means users cannot evaluate which of these risks the app mitigates and which it does not. Read more about [how period tracker apps collect data](/resources/guides/how-period-tracker-apps-collect-data).

## What Changes When You Switch to On-Device Storage

When core data stays on your device, many cloud risks shrink. There is no readable central cycle database to request, govern, or breach.

Floriva uses local-first storage. Your cycle data, symptoms, and predictions are computed and stored on your phone. No account is needed for core tracking. There is no sync, so no cycle data is transmitted at all. Learn about [what a zero-knowledge period tracker means](/resources/guides/what-is-zero-knowledge-period-tracker).

Life Period Tracker stores reproductive health data on cloud servers without providing detailed documentation of its data practices. When an app cannot clearly answer where your data goes, who can access it, and what happens if the company is acquired or shut down, choose an app with no readable central cycle database.
