## Spot On Has Strong Policy, and Real Constraints

Spot On is Planned Parenthood's period tracking app, and that affiliation matters. Planned Parenthood has sustained organizational and legal commitments to protecting reproductive health privacy. The app has no advertising, charges nothing, and is operated by an organization with strong incentives to not become a vector for user surveillance.

The privacy policy is among the more explicit in the space about not selling or sharing data. These are genuine commitments backed by an organization with reputational stakes in reproductive health privacy.

And yet: Spot On is cloud-based. User data is stored on servers.

## What the Cloud Creates

Server-side storage means data exists somewhere other than your device. That location is subject to legal processes: court orders and subpoenas can compel Spot On to produce user records. Planned Parenthood's commitment to privacy does not exempt it from responding to lawful court orders.

There is a second risk specific to Spot On's affiliation. Planned Parenthood is a politically visible organization, and in states with aggressive abortion enforcement postures, that visibility may make its data infrastructure a higher-profile target for legal process. A strong privacy policy does not change the legal exposure that comes with cloud-based storage.

## How Floriva Compares

Floriva keeps core data on your device. There is no readable central database holding your period dates, symptom logs, or cycle history. Device access is a separate legal path. If you chose Spot On because of its strong policy stance and want privacy built into the product architecture, Floriva provides that. The contraception tracking features Spot On offers are available in Floriva's symptom and tracking system.
