## The gap between privacy marketing and privacy architecture

Stardust launched in 2022 with an explicit appeal to users worried about period data privacy after the Dobbs decision. Women-owned, astrology-inflected branding, and a clear message that it was built in response to data privacy concerns. It grew quickly among users who wanted a values-aligned alternative to Flo.

The problem is that being women-owned and being privacy-protective are different claims. In 2022, Wired analyzed Stardust alongside other period trackers and found its privacy protections weaker than Clue's, a tracker with a clean record on data practices but no special privacy branding.

Stardust is cloud-based. Your cycle data is stored on their servers. A company can have good values and still store data on a server that can be subpoenaed, breached, or subjected to a policy change after an acquisition. The privacy protection comes from the architecture, not the founder's identity.

This guide is for users who chose Stardust because they wanted a more private option, and are now ready to move to an app where the privacy guarantee is structural rather than positional.

For a full comparison with local-storage alternatives, see our [Stardust alternative overview](/compare/alternatives/stardust-alternative-local-storage).

## Step 1: Export your cycle data

Open Stardust and go to your profile settings, then Privacy & Data, then Download My Data. Stardust emails a download link to your registered email address. The export includes cycle dates, symptom logs, and any moon phase or astrological data you tracked.

Download and save the file before anything else. Once the account is deleted, you cannot recover this history from Stardust's servers.

## Step 2: Delete your account

In Settings, go to Account, then Delete Account. Stardust requires email confirmation. Check your inbox and complete the verification. Deleting the app from your phone is not the same as deleting the account. The account deletion is what begins removing your data from Stardust's servers.

## Step 3: Submit a formal data deletion request

Email privacy@stardust.app with the subject "Data Deletion Request." Include your registered Stardust email and state that you are requesting complete deletion of all personal data, including backup copies and any data in derived datasets.

If you are a California resident, invoke CCPA Section 1798.105 explicitly. Stardust has 45 days to respond under CCPA. Keep a copy of the email and any response you receive.

## Step 4: Verify the deletion

Wait 24-48 hours after step 2. Try to log in with your old Stardust credentials. A successful deletion returns an "account not found" or "invalid credentials" error. If login still works, the deletion did not complete, repeat step 2 or contact privacy@stardust.app directly.

For more on what privacy policies actually commit to versus what deletion means in practice, see our [period app privacy audit guide](/compare/alternatives/period-app-privacy-audit).

## Step 5: Choose your replacement

Three on-device trackers cover most Stardust users' needs:

**Floriva** is paid and local-first. The design is calm and considered rather than clinical. Core cycle data stays on your phone.

**Euki** is free and maintained by a reproductive health nonprofit. No account, no sync, strong on-device architecture. Understated interface.

**Drip** is free and open-source on Android. Community-maintained and fully on-device.

The difference between all three and Stardust is architectural: Floriva has no readable central cycle database, while Euki and Drip are local-only. See our [private period tracker roundup](/resources/best/best-private-period-tracker-apps) for the feature comparison.

## Step 6: Transfer your history

From your Stardust export, pull your last 3-6 cycle start dates and enter them into your new tracker during setup. Most on-device trackers include a cycle history step in their onboarding flow.

After 2-3 new cycles are recorded in the app, predictions stabilize. Once that happens, delete the Stardust export file. The data you moved away from a server should not sit in an unprotected local folder indefinitely.
