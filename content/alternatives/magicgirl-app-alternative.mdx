## Why Parents and Teens Are Leaving MagicGirl

MagicGirl markets itself to teenagers getting their first periods. The interface uses pastel colors and cute illustrations. Under that friendly design it is an ad-supported app that collects reproductive health data from minors and stores it on cloud servers.

The combination matters. Ad-supported apps generate revenue by showing ads, which typically involves sharing user data, or at least behavioral signals, with advertising networks. When the users are minors logging their periods, the data being collected is both sensitive and protected under laws like COPPA (the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act).

MagicGirl provides minimal public privacy documentation. It is difficult to determine from the app's disclosures exactly what data is collected, how it is shared with ad networks, or how long it is retained.

## What MagicGirl Does With Your Data

MagicGirl collects cycle dates, period length, and symptoms. This data is stored on cloud servers. It leaves your device as part of normal app operation.

The app is ad-supported, meaning advertising infrastructure is embedded in the app. Ad SDKs (software development kits) typically collect device identifiers, usage patterns, and sometimes location data. In a period tracker used by minors, this means ad networks may receive signals correlated with a teenager's reproductive health activity.

The app's privacy documentation does not clearly explain:

- What specific data is shared with advertising partners
- Whether ad targeting uses reproductive health signals
- How data from users under 13 is handled under COPPA
- What data retention periods apply
- How a parent or teen can request complete data deletion

This lack of transparency is itself a privacy problem. When you cannot determine [how an app collects your data](/resources/guides/how-period-tracker-apps-collect-data), you cannot make an informed decision about using it.

## Why Minors' Data Deserves More Protection

COPPA requires apps to obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting personal data from children under 13. Many period trackers used by young teens do not clearly implement COPPA compliance mechanisms.

Beyond legal requirements, minors' reproductive health data carries particular risks. A teenager's period data, stored on cloud servers and linked to advertising identifiers, creates a record that the teen did not meaningfully consent to and may not understand the implications of.

In states with restrictive reproductive health laws, this data becomes legally sensitive. A [post-Dobbs environment](/resources/guides/reproductive-data-prosecutions-after-dobbs/) makes cloud-stored cycle data from any user, especially a minor, a potential liability. Data on company servers can be [subpoenaed](/resources/guides/can-police-access-period-tracker-data/). An ad-supported app with broad data sharing practices multiplies the access points.

A [zero-knowledge tracker](/resources/guides/what-is-zero-knowledge-period-tracker/) like Floriva eliminates every concern specific to MagicGirl:

- **No ads.** No advertising SDK, no ad network data sharing, no behavioral tracking for ad targeting.
- **No readable cloud database.** Core cycle data stays on the device. There is no sync, so no cycle data is transmitted at all.
- **No account required.** No email, no age verification loophole, no identity tied to health data.
- **No ad profile.** Floriva is [suitable for teens](/app-guides/floriva-for-teens) because core tracking does not need an ad-funded account or identity profile.

## How to Switch From MagicGirl

If you or your teen currently use MagicGirl, note down the last several cycle start dates from the app. This is enough historical data to establish prediction baselines in a new tracker.

Check whether MagicGirl offers account deletion in its settings. If so, delete the account, do not just uninstall. Uninstalling removes the app from the phone but may leave data on the company's servers.

If the app does not offer a clear deletion path, consider sending a data deletion request to the developer, citing applicable privacy laws (COPPA for users under 13, or state privacy laws for other users).

## The Privacy Bottom Line

A period tracker marketed to teenagers should have the strongest privacy protections in the category, not the weakest. MagicGirl combines cloud storage, ad support, and minimal privacy documentation, the opposite of what minors' health data requires. Among the [best private period trackers](/resources/best/best-private-period-tracker-apps), those designed with [on-device storage](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker/) protect every user by default, without requiring parents to audit an app's ad SDK integrations or parse an inadequate privacy policy.
