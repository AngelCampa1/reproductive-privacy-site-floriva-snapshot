## Why Users Are Leaving My Calendar

My Calendar by Abishkking is a simple period tracker on Google Play. It offers basic cycle tracking without the feature bloat of larger apps. For users who want something straightforward, that simplicity is appealing.

The problem is not the features. It is the lack of documentation about what happens to your data.

My Calendar provides limited privacy documentation. For a general utility app, that might be acceptable. For an app that records your menstrual cycle, fertility windows, and sexual activity, limited documentation is a serious gap. Users cannot evaluate what they do not know, and what is undocumented is effectively unaccountable.

The app is ad-supported, which means third-party ad SDKs are embedded in the application. These SDKs collect device identifiers and behavioral data to serve targeted advertising. When the behavioral data includes reproductive health patterns, the stakes of that data collection change substantially.

## What My Calendar Does With Your Data

Three data concerns apply to My Calendar.

Ad SDK data collection is inherent to the ad-supported model. Third-party advertising SDKs collect device advertising identifiers, IP addresses, and app usage patterns. This data flows to ad networks where it is used for targeting and may be combined with data from other sources to build user profiles.

Optional cloud sync introduces server-side storage. If enabled, your period data is transmitted to and stored on remote servers. Server-stored data is subject to the developer's data retention policies (which are not clearly documented), legal process including subpoenas, and potential security breaches.

Small development teams present a specific governance concern. Larger companies, despite their own privacy failures, typically have dedicated security teams, incident response procedures, and legal compliance resources. Smaller teams may not have the infrastructure to protect sensitive health data at the same standard. This is not a criticism of small developers. It is a structural reality of data stewardship. ## What Changes When You Switch to On-Device Storage

Local-first storage reduces the documentation problem. When core records stay on your phone, you need less trust in a privacy policy, a development team's security practices, or a company's data governance.

## The Privacy Bottom Line

My Calendar's limited privacy documentation means users are trusting an ad-supported app with reproductive health data without clear information about where that data goes. If you want a period tracker where the answer to "what happens to my data" is unambiguous (it stays on your device), you need an app with a different architecture.
