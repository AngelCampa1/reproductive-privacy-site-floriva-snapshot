## Why Users Are Looking for a Kindara Alternative

Kindara built a loyal following among fertility awareness method (FAM) practitioners who used it to chart basal body temperature and cervical mucus patterns. Then Prima-Temp acquired the company, and development stopped.

The app has not received meaningful updates in years. For users who relied on Kindara daily, the situation is straightforward: the product they trusted no longer exists as an active, maintained application. Bug reports go unanswered. Security patches do not ship.

## What Happened to Your Kindara Data

Kindara was a cloud-based app. Your cycle charts, temperature readings, and symptom logs were stored on company servers. After the Prima-Temp acquisition, Kindara never published a clear statement about what happened to that stored data, whether it was migrated, archived, sold, or deleted.

This is the fundamental risk of cloud-based period tracking: your data outlives your relationship with the app. When a company is acquired, shuts down, or changes direction, your [health data](/resources/guides/how-period-tracker-apps-collect-data) goes wherever the new owner decides. You have no say in the matter.

Former Kindara users cannot reliably request data exports or confirm deletion. The data may still exist on servers controlled by Prima-Temp or its successors.

## The Problem With Cloud-Dependent Trackers

Kindara's situation illustrates a structural issue beyond any single company's decisions. Cloud-based period trackers create a dependency: your data lives on someone else's infrastructure, subject to that company's business decisions, legal obligations, and continued existence.

When a cloud tracker is acquired, goes bankrupt, or pivots, your reproductive health data follows the company, not you. In a [post-Dobbs environment](/resources/guides/reproductive-data-prosecutions-after-dobbs/), this creates additional risk. Data sitting on servers you don't control can be [subpoenaed](/resources/guides/can-police-access-period-tracker-data/) regardless of the original app's privacy promises.

[On-device storage](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker/) reduces this dependency. When core records stay on your phone, an acquisition or bankruptcy cannot turn them into a readable company database.

## What Changes When You Switch to On-Device Storage

With an on-device tracker like Floriva:

- **No account required for core tracking.** You start tracking immediately. No email or password is needed for core use.
- **Core data stays on your phone.** There is no readable central cycle database that can be orphaned or acquired.
- **No company dependency.** If the app company disappears tomorrow, your data remains on your device, accessible to you.
- **No security patch anxiety.** Your sensitive data is not sitting on an unmaintained server waiting for a breach.

## How to Switch From Kindara

If you still have access to the Kindara app and can view your historical charts, record your last several cycle start dates and any BBT data you want to preserve. Screenshot your charts if the app still allows it.

Enter your recent cycle start dates into your new tracker to rebuild your prediction baseline. For a detailed walkthrough, see [how to rebuild your period history after switching apps](/compare/alternatives/rebuild-period-history-after-switching-apps).

If you can no longer access Kindara at all, start fresh. Most cycle prediction algorithms need only two to three cycles of data to establish a useful baseline.

## The Privacy Bottom Line

Kindara's abandonment is a case study in why cloud-based period tracking carries structural risk. Your data should not be hostage to a company's business strategy. [On-device trackers](/resources/guides/what-is-zero-knowledge-period-tracker/) keep core records on your phone. Among the [best private period trackers](/resources/best/best-private-period-tracker-apps), the safer apps are the ones without a readable central cycle database.
