## Why Users Are Leaving Flo

The FTC took enforcement action against Flo Health in January 2021. The agency found the company shared user health data, including period dates, pregnancy status, and detailed symptom information, with Facebook, Google, and third-party analytics firms. The data was shared through embedded software development kits (SDKs) without user consent, despite Flo's stated privacy commitments. This is the same pattern documented across [data brokers trafficking in reproductive health data](/resources/guides/data-brokers-reproductive-health/).

In September 2025, a combined [$59.5M class action settlement](/resources/guides/flo-period-tracker-lawsuit-settlement/) resolved claims by millions of Flo users. On August 1, 2025, a jury separately found Meta liable for accessing user data from the Flo app.

This is not a policy dispute. It is a documented, FTC-sanctioned, jury-confirmed pattern of sharing intimate health data with advertising infrastructure.

## The Architecture Problem

Flo's Anonymous Mode, launched after the FTC enforcement action, puts privacy behind a paywall. But the core problem is architectural, not a policy matter. Flo stores your data on its servers. Any policy Flo adopts can be changed, pressured by law enforcement, or overridden by a court order. That is a real risk in [post-Dobbs](/resources/guides/reproductive-data-prosecutions-after-dobbs/) states.

The only reliable way to prevent data from being [subpoenaed](/resources/guides/can-police-access-period-tracker-data/) is to ensure it never reaches a centralized server. That is the [on-device storage](/resources/guides/on-device-storage-period-tracker/) approach.

## How Floriva Compares

Floriva is built around the architecture choice that Flo's Anonymous Mode simulates: core records stay on your device. We cannot sell cycle data we do not have. The app has no sync, so we receive nothing.

## How to Switch Without Losing Your History

If you are leaving Flo, export your data before you delete your account. In Flo, go to Settings, then Privacy, then Request My Data. Save the export first, enter your last few cycle starts into your new tracker, and only then delete the Flo account itself instead of just uninstalling the app.
