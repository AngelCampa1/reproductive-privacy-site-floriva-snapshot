## Why Users Are Leaving Period Calendar

Period Calendar by SimpleInnovation is one of the most downloaded period trackers on Google Play. Its appeal is straightforward: a clean interface, basic cycle predictions, and free access. The cost is not monetary. It is data.

The app runs on an ad-supported model. That means third-party ad SDKs are embedded in the app, and those SDKs collect device identifiers, behavioral signals, and usage data. Every time you open the app to log a period or a symptom, ad networks receive data about your device and activity. This is standard for ad-supported apps, but "standard" does not mean "safe" when the data involves reproductive health.

The app also requires cloud-based account creation. Your cycle data is stored on remote servers, not exclusively on your device. That combination, ad-SDK data collection plus cloud storage, creates two separate exposure vectors for sensitive health information.

## What Period Calendar Does With Your Data

Ad-supported period trackers operate on a specific exchange: you get free features, and advertisers get access to behavioral data collected through SDKs embedded in the app. These SDKs typically collect device advertising identifiers, IP addresses, app usage patterns, and sometimes location data.

Period Calendar's privacy documentation does not provide detailed technical disclosure about which ad partners receive data or what specific data points are transmitted. This lack of transparency is itself a concern. When an app handles reproductive health data, users deserve clear documentation of every third party that receives any data.

Cloud storage adds a second layer of risk. Data stored on a company's servers can be accessed through legal process, subpoenas, court orders, or law enforcement requests. The company can also change its privacy policy at any time, altering what it does with data already collected. For users in states with abortion restrictions, server-stored period data represents a specific legal exposure. Read more about [how period tracker apps collect data](/resources/guides/how-period-tracker-apps-collect-data).

## What Changes When You Switch to On-Device Storage

Local-first storage means core period data stays on your phone. Floriva has no readable central cycle database for legal requests or central breaches, and no ad SDK transmits behavioral data to third parties.

Floriva uses this architecture. Core cycle data, symptom logs, and predictions are computed and stored locally. No account is required for core tracking. No ads are displayed. No third-party SDKs are embedded. The app functions as a tool on your device, not as an ad data pipeline.

This is not a tradeoff between features and privacy. On-device apps can provide the same cycle predictions and symptom tracking as cloud-based apps. The difference is architectural: where the data lives and who can access it. Learn more about [what a zero-knowledge period tracker means](/resources/guides/what-is-zero-knowledge-period-tracker).

## The Privacy Bottom Line

Period Calendar provides functional cycle tracking, but its ad-supported, cloud-based model means your reproductive health data is transmitted to ad networks and stored on remote servers. If privacy is a requirement rather than a preference, an on-device tracker eliminates both exposure vectors entirely.
