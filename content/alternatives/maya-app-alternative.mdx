## Why Users Are Leaving Maya

Maya by Plackal Tech is one of the most popular period trackers in India and Southeast Asia. It offers cycle tracking, ovulation predictions, and lifestyle content. In September 2019, Privacy International published an investigation that changed how many users viewed the app.

Privacy International found that Maya was sharing intimate health data with Facebook through the Facebook SDK embedded in the app. The data transmission was happening before users had a chance to consent to Maya's privacy policy. Users were opening an app to track their periods and, without any meaningful disclosure, their health data was being sent to Facebook's advertising infrastructure.

This was not a bug. It was the business model. Ad-supported period trackers embed third-party SDKs that collect and transmit data as a condition of the free service. Maya's case was documented by a credible independent organization, but the underlying pattern, health data flowing to ad networks, is common across ad-supported trackers.

## What Maya Does With Your Data

Maya's data practices involve three distinct concerns.

First, the Facebook SDK integration. Privacy International's research documented that Maya transmitted health data to Facebook, including data entered about reproductive status. Facebook's SDK collects device identifiers, app events, and behavioral data that feeds its advertising targeting systems.

Second, location data. Maya's privacy policy lists location data among the information it collects. A period tracker has no functional reason to know your location. Cycle predictions are based on dates and symptoms, not geography. Location data combined with reproductive health information creates a data profile with serious implications, especially for users in jurisdictions with abortion restrictions.

Third, cloud storage. Maya stores data on remote servers, which means the data is accessible to the company, its partners, and law enforcement through legal process. Read more about [how period tracker apps collect data](/resources/guides/how-period-tracker-apps-collect-data).

## What Changes When You Switch to On-Device Storage

Local-first storage reduces the vectors described above. No SDK transmits your health information to Facebook or any other ad network. Floriva has no readable central cycle database to subpoena or breach.

Floriva uses local-first architecture. Cycle data, symptoms, and predictions are processed and stored locally for core tracking. No account is needed. No ads are shown. No location data is collected. No third-party SDKs are present.

Maya was documented by Privacy International sharing intimate health data with Facebook before users could consent. The app collects location data that serves no cycle-tracking function.
