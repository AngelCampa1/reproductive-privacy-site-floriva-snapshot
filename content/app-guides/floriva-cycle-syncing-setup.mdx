## What Cycle Syncing Actually Requires

Cycle syncing is built on one idea: your hormonal environment changes substantially across the four phases of your cycle, and those changes affect energy, recovery capacity, cognitive style, and physical output. Structuring your activity around those changes rather than against them is the practical application.

That requires looking at your own cycle data, not only a 28-day calendar. Floriva helps you review your period dates, symptoms, and fertility signs on your device.

## Step 1: Log Your Period Dates Accurately

Open Floriva and log your period start date on the first day of flow (not spotting). Log your period end date when flow stops. These entries give you a cycle history to review.

If you have past period dates, from memory, a previous app, or a paper record, enter them retroactively. Floriva accepts backdated entries. Three cycles of historical period data is enough to calculate a useful average cycle length for phase estimation.

Accurate period start dates are the foundation. Every phase calculation downstream depends on them.

## Step 2: Understand the four cycle phases

Floriva's home view shows your current phase. The four phases are:

- **Menstrual** (days 1-5 typical): Period is active. Estrogen and progesterone are both low. Energy is often lower; many people find this a good phase for reduced intensity and recovery-focused activity.
- **Follicular** (days 6-13 typical): Estrogen rises as follicles develop. Energy, motivation, and physical performance often increase through this phase.
- **Ovulatory** (days 13-16 typical): Estrogen peaks, LH surges, ovulation occurs. This phase is brief, 2-4 days, and often correlates with peak physical and social energy for many people.
- **Luteal** (days 17-28 typical): Progesterone rises after ovulation. The early luteal phase often feels productive; the late luteal phase is where PMS symptoms appear if they occur.

The day ranges above are estimates based on a 28-day cycle. Your phases may be shorter or longer. Use your Floriva logs to compare those estimates with your own pattern.

## Step 3: Add Ovulation Data to Anchor the Luteal Phase

The luteal phase, the period between ovulation and your next period, is where most cycle syncing adjustments happen. Its start depends entirely on when you ovulated, which varies even in regular cycles.

Check current Floriva onboarding for the exact ovulation fields in your app version. If available, use a small set of signs you can log every day:

- **Test notes**: If you use ovulation tests, note the date and result in the field available to you.
- **Temperature notes**: If you track morning temperature, keep the timing consistent so your notes are easier to review.
- **Body signs**: If you track other fertility signs, use plain labels you can repeat from cycle to cycle.

Adding any of these gives you a better anchor for luteal-phase review.

## Step 4: Set Up Symptom Fields for Cycle Syncing

Cycle syncing without symptom data is navigation without landmarks. Go to Floriva's symptom settings and add the specific variables you want to track across phases. Useful fields for cycle syncing include:

- **Energy level**: Log daily as low, medium, or high. This is the primary signal for scheduling high-demand activities.
- **Sleep quality**: Changes in sleep across cycle phases, especially late-luteal sleep disruption, are common and clinically documented.
- **Exercise performance**: Log how hard your workout felt, or whether you hit personal bests. Follicular phase often produces stronger performance; late luteal often doesn't.
- **Focus and cognitive clarity**: Useful for scheduling creative work, complex analysis, or high-stakes meetings.
- **Mood and emotional state**: Log consistently using a simple scale. This is the most variable field and benefits most from consistent multi-cycle data.
- **Cravings or appetite changes**: Late-luteal appetite increases are a real hormonal effect; logging them helps distinguish hunger from habit.

You don't need all of these. Pick three to five fields you can log every day without missing entries. Consistent logging of a few fields beats sporadic logging of many.

## Step 5: Log Daily During Each Phase

The practice of cycle syncing depends on granular data, not just phase labels. Log your selected symptom fields every day during your cycle. The best time to log is at the same time each day, morning or evening works, as long as it's consistent.

During the **menstrual phase**, note energy and pain or cramping levels. During the **follicular phase**, watch for energy upticks and whether motivation increases as estrogen rises. During the **ovulatory phase**, note any social energy, confidence, or performance peaks. During the **luteal phase**, pay close attention to when energy starts declining, when sleep changes, and when any PMS-type symptoms appear.

After three cycles, these daily entries form the pattern you'll use to plan activities.

## Step 6: Review Phase Patterns After Three Cycles

After you've logged through three complete cycles, open Floriva's history view and review your symptom data by phase. Look for:

- **Consistency**: Do your energy levels follow the same pattern across each phase in all three cycles? If follicular energy was high in all three cycles, that is a reliable signal for scheduling demanding work during that phase.
- **Variability**: If luteal mood was very negative in one cycle and neutral in another, the pattern is inconsistent. External stressors, sleep quality, or other factors are likely overriding the hormonal signal.
- **Phase length**: How long is your actual follicular phase? How long is your actual luteal phase? Knowing that your luteal phase runs 12 days rather than the textbook 14 changes how you plan.
- **Outlier cycles**: Was one cycle notably different Check whether it coincided with travel, illness, significant stress, or major lifestyle change. Outlier cycles with a known cause are less predictive than average cycles.

Use this review to set phase-specific intentions, not rigid schedules, but general patterns. If your follicular phase consistently produces high focus, plan your most complex work for that window. If your late luteal phase consistently brings lower energy, schedule recovery or lighter tasks.

## What This Means for You

Cycle syncing works best as a probabilistic guide, not a fixed plan. Your data will show you which phases tend to produce which conditions for your body specifically, not for a textbook average. After three to six cycles of consistent Floriva logging, you'll have a personal pattern that's far more reliable than any generic "do yoga during your luteal phase" advice.

All of that data stays on your device. The symptom patterns you log for personal health planning remain in your control.
