## The Diagnosis Gap

Endometriosis affects an estimated 1 in 10 people with a uterus. On average, years pass between when symptoms begin and when a diagnosis is made. Symptoms are often dismissed as "bad periods," and without a documented pattern, each appointment starts from scratch.

Symptom tracking doesn't speed up the diagnostic timeline by magic. But it does two things that verbal symptom description cannot: it shows a pattern over time rather than a single data point, and it quantifies severity in terms of functional impact rather than subjective pain description.

The challenge is that useful endometriosis tracking requires more than period start and end dates. You need to log pain on non-period days, track multiple symptom types, and build a record that spans multiple cycles, which means months of consistent logging before you have something clinically useful to show.

## What to Prioritize

If you're just starting to track for endometriosis, prioritize these over everything else:

**Cycle phase with each entry.** Always note where in your cycle a symptom occurs, day 1 of period, day 8 (between periods), day 14 (near ovulation). Phase correlation is what makes the pattern meaningful.

**Non-menstrual pain days.** Chronic pelvic pain between periods is one of the features that distinguishes endometriosis from primary dysmenorrhea. Log it every time, not just when it's severe.

**Functional impact.** Pain medication taken, activities missed, days spent in bed. This translates subjective pain into objective data that's harder to dismiss.

## Privacy and Endometriosis Data

Detailed endometriosis symptom records (pain severity, bowel symptoms, sexual symptoms, functional impact) are sensitive health information. [Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa/). Storing this data on a server puts it outside your control.

Floriva's on-device architecture means the health record you build over months of endometriosis logging stays on your device. It has less readable company-held exposure to server breaches and legal requests directed at the app company. Given how personal and detailed this data is, that's a meaningful distinction.
