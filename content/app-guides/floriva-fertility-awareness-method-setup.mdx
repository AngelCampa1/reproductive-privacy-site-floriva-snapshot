## FAM Charting Requires a Dedicated Tool

Fertility Awareness Method is built on a daily chart of temperature, mucus, and other signs that, read together, identifies the fertile window and confirms ovulation. Keeping this chart in Floriva gives you a persistent record of every cycle without relying on memory or paper charts that can be lost.

What Floriva adds over paper charting is a private cycle history on your device and a place to review patterns across cycles. Check current app setup for the exact chart options available. What it does not add is interpretation guidance. FAM requires a learning period and ideally instruction from a certified practitioner. This guide covers the charting mechanics, not the method's contraceptive or conception application.

**Note:** Floriva is not FDA-cleared as a contraceptive device. If you're using FAM for family planning purposes, that decision requires your own informed judgment and, ideally, formal training.

## Step 1: Thermometer Selection and Setup

Buy a basal body thermometer that reads to two decimal places. Look for:

- **Two decimal display**: Shows 97.42°F or 36.34°C, not 97.4°F. The second decimal is essential for detecting the small thermal shift.
- **Memory function**: Stores the last reading so you can check it after you've moved or gotten up.
- **Consistent startup time**: Some thermometers take 30 seconds; others take 60. Use the same one every day, switching thermometers mid-cycle introduces variation.

Oral thermometers are standard. Vaginal BBT readings are more consistent but a different method than most practitioners teach. Pick one method and stick to it throughout a cycle.

Set your thermometer on your bedside table with the log open or a paper to write the reading on. You'll take the temperature before getting up, speaking, or eating.

## Step 2: Establish Consistent Measurement Timing

BBT must be taken after at least three consecutive hours of sleep, before getting out of bed, and at approximately the same time each morning (within 30 minutes is the standard guideline). Time variation introduces temperature variation, your BBT rises slightly with each hour of additional sleep.

Pick a time that works even on weekends. If you typically wake at 7am on weekdays and 9am on weekends, your weekend temperatures will be slightly elevated compared to weekday readings, which can obscure the thermal shift pattern.

In Floriva, set the daily BBT reminder to your consistent waking time. When you take your temperature, log it immediately in Floriva's BBT field. Enter the exact reading to two decimal places.

## Step 3: Cervical Mucus Notation

Cervical mucus changes throughout the cycle in response to hormonal shifts. The standard notation system used in most FAM methods uses four categories:

- **D (Dry)**: No mucus sensation. Usually occurs in the early follicular phase after menstruation ends.
- **S (Sticky or Cloudy)**: Thick, opaque, white or yellow discharge. Crumbly or tacky texture. Low fertility indicator.
- **C (Creamy)**: Smooth, lotion-like consistency. White or off-white. Wetter than sticky but no stretching. Indicates approaching fertility.
- **EW (Egg-White)**: Clear, stretchy, slippery, resembles raw egg white. Stretches 1+ inch without breaking. Peak fertility indicator. Typically appears 1-2 days before and on the day of ovulation.

In Floriva, log your cervical mucus observation each evening. Log the most fertile observation of the day (if you noticed EW mucus at any point, log EW, even if earlier in the day it appeared creamy). This conservative practice is standard in FAM instruction.

Log "D" (dry) on genuinely dry days rather than leaving the field blank. Blank entries and dry entries mean different things when you're reviewing the chart.

## Step 4: LH Strip Logging

Ovulation predictor kit (OPK) strips detect the LH surge that precedes ovulation by 24-36 hours. To log in Floriva:

- Test once or twice daily starting a few days before your estimated ovulation window (cycle day 9-10 for a typical 28-day cycle; earlier for longer cycles)
- Log the result as positive (peak or near-peak) or negative
- Note the day of first positive and the day of peak darkness if using standard test strips

A positive LH result means ovulation is likely within 24-36 hours. It doesn't confirm that ovulation occurred, some people have LH surges without releasing an egg. BBT confirmation is the way to verify actual ovulation after an LH surge.

## Step 5: Reading the Biphasic Pattern

A confirmed ovulatory cycle shows a biphasic temperature pattern on Floriva's BBT chart:

- **Pre-ovulatory phase**: Lower temperature cluster, typically 97.0-97.5°F (36.1-36.4°C) for most people
- **Thermal shift**: A rise of approximately 0.2°F (0.1°C) or more that is sustained
- **Post-ovulatory phase**: Higher temperature cluster, typically 97.6-98.0°F (36.4-36.7°C)

The thermal shift confirms ovulation retrospectively, you can confirm ovulation occurred after seeing three consecutive temperatures above the pre-ovulatory range. Floriva's chart makes this pattern visually apparent once you have enough readings.

The luteal phase runs from the thermal shift through your next period. A consistently short luteal phase (fewer than 10 days) is worth discussing with a provider.

## Step 6: Anovulatory Cycles

An anovulatory cycle does not produce a thermal shift. The BBT chart shows a flat or randomly fluctuating pattern with no sustained rise. If you reach cycle day 21+ without a thermal shift and your temperatures remain in the pre-ovulatory range, you may be in an anovulatory cycle.

Signs that distinguish an anovulatory cycle in Floriva:
- No BBT thermal shift by cycle day 20+
- No EW cervical mucus observed, or EW mucus with no subsequent temperature rise
- No positive LH strip, or a positive LH strip with no subsequent temperature rise
- Eventual bleed with no preceding temperature drop (or a drop with no earlier sustained rise)

Occasional anovulatory cycles are common, particularly during high stress, illness, or significant weight change. They're also normal in perimenopause. A pattern of consistently anovulatory cycles warrants investigation.

## Step 7: Handling Disrupted Readings

Not every temperature reading will be clean. Common disruptions include:

- **Illness with fever**: Temperature will be elevated and unreliable. Log the reading with a "fever" note in Floriva. Most FAM guidelines suggest ignoring the reading when charting your cover line.
- **Alcohol the night before**: Alcohol often elevates BBT. Log the reading and note the alcohol.
- **Travel across time zones**: Disrupts wake time and sleep patterns. Log readings with a "travel/disrupted" note.
- **Less than three hours of sleep**: Log with a sleep note. The reading may not reflect your true basal temperature.

In Floriva, use the notes field on the BBT entry to flag these disruptions. A flagged reading is still data, it tells you why the chart shows an outlier at that point.

## What This Means for You

FAM charting in Floriva gives you a complete visual record of your fertility signs cycle over cycle. After three to four charted cycles, patterns become legible: your typical ovulation day, your usual luteal phase length, what your EW mucus days look like relative to your temperature shift.

That record stays on your device. Your fertility sign observations, the kind of detailed health data that can be sensitive in a post-Dobbs legal environment, are not on any server.

Use FAM for any purpose you choose: conception, cycle awareness, or general health monitoring. If you're using it for family planning, do so with proper training and a clear understanding of the method's requirements.
