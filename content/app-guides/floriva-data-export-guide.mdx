## Why data export matters for an on-device app

When a period tracker stores data on a server, the company has a copy regardless of what you do. Your data exists in their infrastructure, under their retention policies, subject to their legal obligations. If you want a copy, you submit a request. If you want it deleted, you submit a different request and wait.

Floriva's on-device storage flips this. Your data exists only on your phone. The export function is not a way to get your data from a company. It is a way to package your own data into a portable file that you can move, store, or delete on your own terms.

This matters for two practical reasons: data portability (moving to another app without losing history) and data security (protecting against device loss or damage).

## What the export contains

Floriva's export includes the data you have logged: cycle start and end dates, flow intensity, symptom entries, and any notes. Because Floriva only stores what you enter on your device, the export reflects exactly what you put in. There is no behavioral metadata, no inferred data from usage patterns, and no third-party data appended.

This is different from data exports from cloud-based apps, which may include account metadata, device identifiers, inferred health insights, and records of which features you used. Floriva has none of that to export.

## How to export and where to save the file

The export walkthrough is in the steps above. The key decision is where you save the file. Options from most to least private:

**Device local storage (most private):** Saving to your phone's local storage keeps the data off any cloud service. Your device's lock screen protects it. The risk is device loss, if your phone is damaged or stolen and you have no device backup, the export file goes with it.

**Encrypted folder on a computer:** Transfer the file to a computer and store it in an encrypted folder (FileVault on Mac, BitLocker on Windows, or an encrypted ZIP file). This is the right choice for long-term archiving.

**USB drive:** A completely offline storage option. No network, no cloud, no third party. Effective but requires you to keep track of physical media.

**Avoid:** General cloud storage services (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud) unless you are specifically comfortable with that provider holding health data. Most cloud storage terms permit the provider to access files for service operation, and cloud accounts can be compromised.

## Using the export to move to a new app

If you are switching from Floriva to another period tracker, the export file is your migration tool. Open the file and locate your cycle start dates. These are the most important historical data for seeding a new tracker's algorithm.

Enter your last 6 cycle start dates manually in the new app when it asks for history. That is typically enough for a prediction algorithm to calculate your average cycle length and generate a reasonable first forecast. After 2-3 cycles with the new app, predictions stabilize.

If the new app accepts CSV import, check whether Floriva's export format matches what it expects. Most apps that accept import are looking for a column of cycle start dates; the exact format varies.

## Using the export for medical purposes

If you need to share cycle data with a clinician, the export file is one way to do it. A CSV file opened in a spreadsheet and printed or emailed gives a clinician a structured record without requiring them to have access to your phone or your app.

For medical sharing, include a date range in your description of the data so the clinician understands the time period covered. A month-by-month table of cycle lengths and symptom frequency, derived from your export, is typically more readable in an appointment than a raw data file.

## What happens if you lose your phone without an export

If your device is lost or damaged and you have not exported your Floriva data, and you do not have a device-level backup that includes app data, your Floriva history is gone. This is the tradeoff of on-device storage: the data is maximally private because no one else holds it, but that also means there is no server to restore from.

Regular exports, every 3-6 months, protect against this scenario. Treat Floriva exports the same way you treat other irreplaceable files.
