## PCOS and the Data Accumulation Problem

PCOS management is a long-term project, not a one-time fix. Cycles may be irregular for years. Treatment responses need to be evaluated across multiple cycles. Hormonal symptoms wax and wane. The data you accumulate over that time is a detailed health record with both clinical value and privacy implications.

Apps that store PCOS data on servers accumulate years of that health information outside your control. The FTC found in 2021 that Flo shared reproductive health data with Facebook and Google. For a PCOS user who has tracked symptoms for three years, that's three years of detailed health patterns in a system with that documented history.

Floriva's on-device architecture means the long data history PCOS tracking requires never becomes a server-side liability. The tradeoff is smaller prediction datasets. Floriva's cycle predictions don't have the same scale as server-based apps. For PCOS users whose cycles are irregular anyway, prediction accuracy is less important than accurate logging and privacy.

## What Makes PCOS Tracking Different

The fundamental challenge with PCOS tracking is that the condition undermines the assumptions most cycle apps are built on. Fixed cycle length, predictable ovulation windows, and regular period timing are often absent. An app that generates confident predictions from these assumptions will produce wrong data for PCOS users.

Useful PCOS tracking is less about prediction and more about documentation: what actually happened, when, and how severe. Over time, that documentation reveals patterns (treatment responses, seasonal variation, relationship between symptoms and lifestyle factors) that are genuinely useful for managing the condition.

## Building a Useful Baseline

Before starting any new treatment or making significant lifestyle changes, log at least three cycles of baseline data in Floriva. This gives you something to compare against. If you start inositol or hormonal birth control or make dietary changes, the pre-change baseline is what makes the post-change data meaningful.
