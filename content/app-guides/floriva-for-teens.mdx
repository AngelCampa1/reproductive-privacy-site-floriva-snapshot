## Why period tracking privacy matters for teens

Wanting privacy about your period is not suspicious or unusual. It is normal. A teenager may not want a parent to know about irregularities they are trying to understand on their own, health questions they are researching before deciding whether to bring them up, or simply the details of their cycle. That is a reasonable thing to want.

The problem is that most period trackers are not built with device-only privacy in mind. Apps that require an account, sync to the cloud, or connect to a family health profile leave traces in places a determined parent can find: shared email accounts, iCloud backup contents, Google account data, family link dashboards.

Floriva's architecture is different in a way that is directly relevant here. Core tracking does not require an account. Core records stay on your device, and there is no sync to transmit anything. This makes the main privacy question who can access your phone, not whether Floriva has a readable cycle database.

## Setup that does not create a trail

### No account means no email receipt

Many apps send a confirmation email when you create an account. A shared family email, or an email account a parent has access to, would show that receipt. Floriva skips this entirely. There is no account creation step and no email sent.

Use the current Floriva download option for your device. Open the app. You will not be asked to enter a name, date of birth, or email address for core tracking.

### Check shared device backups

If your family uses a shared Apple ID for purchases, or a family Google account, your first concern might be whether app data shows up somewhere shared. For Floriva core tracking, readable cycle records stay on your device. There is no sync, so no cycle data is transmitted at all.

The one area to check is device backup. iOS iCloud backups and Android Google Drive backups can include some local app data, and if a parent manages the backup or has access to the same Apple or Google account, they could theoretically access a backup file. To eliminate this:

- On iOS: Settings, your name, iCloud, scroll to Floriva, and turn off backup for that app specifically. Or go to Settings, your name, iCloud, iCloud Backup, and turn off backup entirely for the device.
- On Android: Settings, Google, Backup, you can review what is backed up per app.

If you have your own separate Apple ID or Google account (which is the most private setup), this is less of a concern.

### Protecting the app on your device

Floriva's data is as private as your device's lock screen. If someone can unlock your phone, they can open the app. The practical protections are:

- Use a passcode or biometric that only you know
- Use your device's built-in app lock to add a second layer of authentication for Floriva specifically (available in iOS Screen Time settings and many Android devices natively)

These are device-level protections, not something Floriva controls. But they are effective when used.

## What gets shared with a family plan and what does not

A family Apple or Google plan typically shares:

- Payment method and purchase history (the app purchase or subscription may appear)
- App Store/Play Store download history if you share an Apple ID for purchases

A family plan does not share:

- App data or in-app content (each device has its own data)
- Floriva's readable cycle logs, symptom entries, or notes

If your parent sees Floriva in a shared purchase history, they would see that you downloaded a period tracking app, not what you logged in it. If that visibility itself is a concern, some devices support separate Apple IDs for purchases.

## Using Floriva day-to-day

Once setup is done, use the app normally. Log cycle start dates when your period begins. Add symptoms, flow intensity, or notes as useful. Core records stay on your device. There is no sync, so nothing is transmitted.

If you ever want to delete local records, uninstall the app. There is no account and no sync. If you made an encrypted backup file, delete that file too.
