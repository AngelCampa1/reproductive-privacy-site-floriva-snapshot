## Why longitudinal logging matters for endometriosis

Endometriosis is difficult to diagnose. The average time from first symptoms to diagnosis is still measured in years in most healthcare systems. A significant part of that delay comes from how symptoms are reported: in the exam room, from memory, under time pressure. Pain that has been occurring monthly for years gets compressed into a few sentences that do not convey the actual pattern.

A consistent symptom log changes that. When a patient brings 6 months of daily pain data, location, intensity, timing relative to cycle phase, secondary symptoms, functional impact, the clinical picture is materially different from a verbal summary. Diagnosis still requires imaging or laparoscopy in most cases, but a detailed log accelerates the pathway to investigation.

The data privacy question is real here too. Endometriosis logs contain detailed health information about chronic pain, reproductive function, and daily physical capacity. This is among the most sensitive health data an app can store. [Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa), so apps that sync this information to servers have fewer legal protections on it than most users assume. Floriva stores symptom logs on your device and never transmits them.

## What to log and how to do it consistently

### Daily pain logging

The single most valuable habit is logging pain every day, including days with no pain. A log entry of zero pain is data. It tells a specialist that your pain is episodic, not constant, and shows the pattern of when it appears relative to your cycle.

Log pain with a location (pelvic, lower back, right side, etc.) and an intensity on a consistent scale. Floriva lets you add custom notes, use them to describe character (cramping, stabbing, dull, pressure) and context (during exercise, after eating, during a bowel movement). Over months, these details reveal whether pain has a consistent pattern or is more diffuse.

### Cycle phase context

Endometriosis pain often worsens at ovulation and in the days just before a period, as well as during menstruation. Floriva's cycle tracking provides the phase context for your symptom entries. You can see whether your pain entries cluster in specific phases or occur throughout the month.

Pain that occurs throughout the month, not only during the period, is a relevant clinical finding. A log that shows this is much more useful than a description.

### Secondary symptoms

Bloating, nausea, fatigue, painful intercourse, and bowel symptoms (diarrhea or constipation at specific cycle phases, pain during bowel movements) are all documented symptoms associated with endometriosis. They are frequently underreported because they feel like separate complaints.

Log each of these in Floriva's symptom log as their own entries. A record showing that bloating and fatigue consistently appear in the week before your period, alongside pelvic pain, forms a pattern. Logged separately from pain, these entries add a dimension that verbal recall rarely captures.

### Functional impact

Note when symptoms affect what you can do: work missed, activities avoided, pain medication taken. This is distinct from pain intensity and is often the most compelling data point for insurance authorization or specialist referrals. A pain level of 7 is subjective; missing 2 days of work per month for 6 consecutive months is documented.

## Privacy considerations specific to this data

Endometriosis tracking creates a detailed record of chronic reproductive health conditions, pain history, and medical status. This data has potential insurance implications. Apps that upload data to servers, even encrypted, expose it to company employees, breaches, and legal requests.

The FTC's 2021 enforcement action against Flo documented that period tracking data was shared with Facebook and Google through embedded SDKs. A class action settled for $59.5M in 2025. The same structural problem, server storage of sensitive health data, creates similar exposure for any cloud-based period tracker. Floriva reduces that exposure with local-first core records and no readable central cycle database.

## Building the longitudinal record that changes clinical conversations

The gap between when endometriosis symptoms begin and when a person receives a diagnosis is long in part because the evidence available at any given appointment is thin. Verbal reports from memory are imprecise. A clinician ordering an ultrasound or laparoscopy based on "I've had bad periods for a few years" is working with less information than one reviewing a 6-month daily log showing consistent pain across multiple cycle phases, documented secondary symptoms, and quantified functional impact.

Floriva does not diagnose endometriosis. It gives you the tool to build the record that makes clinical conversations more productive. The symptom log, cycle context, and export function are the pieces that matter, not a specialized "endo mode," but a general symptom logging architecture used consistently.

Start logging now. The value of the record is proportional to its length and consistency. A log you start today will be more useful at an appointment six months from now than anything you can reconstruct from memory at that point.
