## The spreadsheet is a principled choice

Tracking your cycle in a spreadsheet is not a workaround or an inferior substitute. It is a deliberate data sovereignty choice. You own the file. It lives where you put it. If it never touches a cloud service, no third party has access to it. No company can change their privacy policy next year and retroactively apply it to your data. No breach at a health tech company exposes your cycle history.

That logic is sound. The people who have landed on spreadsheet tracking after reading about [how period tracker apps collect data](/resources/guides/how-period-tracker-apps-collect-data), or after Flo's 2021 FTC enforcement action and 2025 $59.5M settlement, often have good reasons for their choice.

The question is not whether the spreadsheet is wrong. It is whether a purpose-built app that matches the privacy architecture of a local file can do things the spreadsheet cannot.

Floriva can, in specific ways that matter for daily tracking.

## What a spreadsheet does well

A spreadsheet lets you track exactly what you want, in exactly the format you choose, with no mediation by an app's opinion about what fields matter. For people with complex conditions or tracking needs that do not fit standard app templates (PCOS, endometriosis, perimenopause, post-surgical recovery), the flexibility is genuinely useful.

Spreadsheets are also free, portable across devices when you control the file location, and indefinitely exportable in standard formats.

## Where friction becomes a problem

The weakest point in spreadsheet tracking is daily logging. Logging a symptom or flow entry in a spreadsheet requires:

1. Finding the app (often not on your home screen)
2. Opening the file
3. Finding the right row and column
4. Entering the value
5. Saving

That is three to five steps in a moment when you might be in a bathroom, at work, or simply in a hurry. The result is retrospective logging, adding data later from memory, which is less accurate than in-the-moment entry.

Floriva reduces daily logging to a single tap from a home screen widget. For symptom data in particular, which is most accurate when logged at the time of occurrence, this friction difference produces a meaningfully better dataset over months of tracking.

## What Floriva does that a spreadsheet cannot

### Cycle prediction

A spreadsheet requires you to build the formula or do the math. Floriva calculates your predicted next period, fertile window, and cycle phase from your logged history automatically. The prediction adapts as your cycle length changes. For people with irregular cycles, this adaptation is not trivial to replicate in a spreadsheet without meaningful formula work.

### Phase-aware symptom display

Floriva associates symptoms with cycle phases, follicular, ovulatory, luteal, menstrual. Over months, patterns emerge: symptoms that consistently appear in the luteal phase, pain that clusters around ovulation. In a spreadsheet, identifying this pattern requires comparing symptom entries to calculated cycle phase for each row. Floriva surfaces it visually.

### Notification-based logging

Floriva can remind you to log at a consistent time, which improves the completeness of the dataset. A spreadsheet cannot send you a notification. For people who track because they are trying to build a record for medical purposes, consistent prompts matter.

## The privacy comparison

A local spreadsheet that never syncs to cloud storage matches Floriva's privacy model closely: records stay on your device. If your spreadsheet syncs to Google Sheets, Microsoft OneDrive, or Apple iCloud, you have introduced cloud storage, and with it the same exposure risks as any cloud-connected app.

Floriva keeps records on-device and makes no network calls. If you are using a cloud-synced spreadsheet because "at least it is not a period tracking app," the privacy difference from a dedicated app with no readable central cycle database may be smaller than you think, and the spreadsheet's cloud provider may be less careful with health data than a purpose-built privacy-first app.

If you want the best of both: use Floriva for daily logging and prediction, and use its export function to maintain a local file backup on your own terms.

## When to stick with the spreadsheet

The spreadsheet wins in two situations. First, if you have tracking needs that go significantly beyond what Floriva's fields cover, medication doses, fertility treatment protocols, lab result timestamps, the flexibility of a custom spreadsheet is hard to replicate in an app. Second, if you are tracking irregularly and only want to record a few data points a year, the overhead of an app is probably not worth it.

For the majority of people who are logging daily symptoms, want cycle prediction, and found their way to a spreadsheet specifically because they did not trust a cloud-connected app with their data, Floriva's on-device architecture answers the privacy concern without requiring you to maintain formula logic or accept the friction of file-based mobile logging.

The question is not which approach is more principled (both are). It is which one you will actually use consistently over months, because the value of cycle tracking is entirely in the longitudinal record.
