## What postpartum period tracking actually involves

The postpartum period is not a single event. It is a months-long recovery process with several distinct phases that are worth tracking for different reasons.

First comes lochia, postpartum bleeding that begins immediately after birth and typically continues for 4-6 weeks. Lochia is not a period; it is the shedding of uterine lining after delivery. It progresses from heavier, redder bleeding to lighter, more brownish discharge. A record of this progression is useful if bleeding resumes heavily or if you are assessing recovery timelines.

Then comes the gap before your period returns. For people who are not breastfeeding, this is typically 6-8 weeks. For people who are breastfeeding, hormonal suppression from prolactin can delay the return of your period for months or longer. How long the gap lasts varies considerably.

Finally comes the adjustment period of the first several postpartum cycles, which are often different from your pre-pregnancy cycles: heavier flow, more intense cramping, irregular timing. This adjustment phase normalizes over several cycles for most people.

Logging all three phases in Floriva gives you a complete postpartum reproductive record.

## Lochia: logging the immediate postpartum phase

Floriva's cycle log is designed for menstrual cycles, so the most practical approach for lochia is to use the symptom or notes fields. Log lochia as a symptom entry with intensity (heavy, moderate, light) starting on the birth date. Note the date when lochia stops.

This creates a clear reference point: everything before that date is lochia, everything after is the gap before your period returns. When your period eventually comes back, you can log it as a cycle start with the postpartum gap clearly documented in your history.

If you notice lochia resuming heavily after it had lightened, a potential sign of retained placenta or other complications, the log gives you a timestamped record to share with your provider.

## Breastfeeding and cycle return

Breastfeeding suppresses ovulation through elevated prolactin levels. While exclusive breastfeeding is not a reliable contraception method, it does delay the return of your period in most cases. The delay is longer with more frequent nursing and shorter as frequency drops.

Log your breastfeeding status in Floriva's notes field as context for your cycle log. When you begin introducing formula or solid foods, or when nursing frequency drops, you may see your period return shortly after. Having that timeline in your log (breastfeeding frequency decreasing, period returning) is useful context for understanding your cycle's return.

## The first postpartum cycles

When your period comes back, treat it as a new baseline. The first several postpartum periods are frequently:

- Heavier than your pre-pregnancy norm
- More painful than before
- Irregular in timing and duration

Log each cycle with flow intensity and pain levels. After 3-4 cycles, comparison against your logs will show whether patterns are normalizing or whether something warrants clinical attention. A documented trend of increasingly heavy periods is more useful to a gynecologist than a recollection that periods "seemed heavier after the baby."

Floriva's prediction accuracy will improve as your cycle regularizes. In the first few postpartum cycles, expect predictions to be off, your body is reestablishing a hormonal rhythm that may have been interrupted for a year or more.

## Privacy considerations for postpartum data

Postpartum reproductive health data is among the most sensitive you can log. It connects birth date information, ongoing reproductive status, breastfeeding data, and menstrual health in a single record. Combined with location data that most phones generate automatically, this profile is highly specific.

[Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa). Apps that store this data on servers expose it to the same risks as any cloud-stored health data: breaches, legal requests, and data sales. The FTC's 2021 action against Flo, which settled in a 2025 class action for $59.5M, documented the transmission of reproductive health data to advertising platforms. The same architecture that made that possible, server-side data storage, is what Floriva avoids.

Your postpartum log in Floriva stays on your device. The app has no sync, so Floriva has no postpartum-log database at all.
