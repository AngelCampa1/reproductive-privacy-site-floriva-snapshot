## What changes during perimenopause and why it matters to track

Perimenopause is the phase before menopause when estrogen and progesterone levels begin to shift. For most people it lasts several years. The defining characteristic is irregularity: cycles that were predictable for decades become unpredictable, sometimes arriving weeks early, sometimes months late. Alongside the timing shifts come new physical symptoms (hot flashes, night sweats, sleep disruption, mood changes) that do not map cleanly onto a simple cycle calendar.

This is precisely the kind of health data that benefits from systematic logging. A clinician trying to assess where you are in the transition, or whether hormone therapy is appropriate, needs a longitudinal record. Memory is not reliable for this. A consistent log is.

The question of where that log lives matters now more than it did when you were tracking a predictable 28-day cycle. Perimenopause logs contain detailed health and hormonal information. Period tracker apps that upload data to servers expose that record to the same risks as any other sensitive health data: breaches, data sales, and legal requests. [Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa), so cloud-stored cycle logs have fewer legal protections than you might expect.

Floriva keeps records on your device. Exports and encrypted backup files are separate choices you control.

## What to log and when

### Cycle timing, even when it is irregular

Log every cycle start date. Even if your cycles are arriving unpredictably, the raw dates form the dataset a clinician uses to identify perimenopause patterns. A cycle that arrives three weeks early is important information. So is a cycle that skips two months. Record all of it.

During perimenopause, Floriva's predictions will be less accurate than they were during your regular years. That is expected. The log of actual dates is what you are building.

### Spotting versus true periods

Perimenopausal spotting is common and medically distinct from a full period. Log spotting separately from cycle starts. Over time, you will have a record that distinguishes breakthrough bleeding from genuine menstruation, which is directly relevant to clinical assessment and, if needed, to ruling out other causes.

### Daily symptoms, not just period-week symptoms

Hot flashes and night sweats often cluster in the week before a period but can occur at any phase. Log them daily, not just when you have your period. The same applies to mood shifts and sleep quality. A log that only covers the days around bleeding misses the full picture of hormonal transition.

Floriva's symptom logging is not limited to period-adjacent days. Use it throughout the cycle.

### Sleep as a distinct data point

Night sweats disrupt sleep, and poor sleep compounds the other symptoms of perimenopause. Logging sleep quality as a separate field from night sweat events lets you track whether the two are correlated and how severe the disruption has been over weeks or months. This is the kind of detail that turns a vague complaint into a documented pattern.

## Sharing your data with a clinician

Before a gynecology appointment or consultation with a menopause specialist, export a summary of your Floriva data. A 3-6 month log of cycle lengths, symptom frequency, and severity gives your clinician a usable record rather than asking you to recall when symptoms started or how often they occur.

Floriva's export function puts your data in a format you control. You decide what to share and with whom. Nothing is automatically transmitted to a healthcare provider or insurer.

## Why data privacy matters during perimenopause

The data you log during perimenopause is not just cycle dates. Over months it becomes a detailed hormonal health record: frequency of hot flashes, severity of sleep disruption, pace of cycle irregularity, onset and pattern of mood changes. That record has potential implications for insurance underwriting, employer wellness programs, and legal requests. None of those are governed by HIPAA, because [period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa).

Apps that store data on servers hold that record in a database that can be breached, subpoenaed, or monetized. Flo's 2021 FTC enforcement action, followed by a 2025 class action that settled for $59.5M, showed concretely what that risk looks like: detailed reproductive health data transmitted to advertising platforms through embedded SDKs.

Floriva's local-first architecture means your perimenopause log starts on your phone, with less readable company-side data than cloud-first trackers. A clinician sees it because you export it and choose to share it. Device backups and exports still need care.
