## How IUDs change what you need to track

IUDs, both hormonal and copper, change bleeding patterns in ways that make a simple cycle calendar less useful on its own. The adjustment period after insertion typically runs 3-6 months, and what happens during that window is genuinely worth logging in detail.

With a hormonal IUD, irregular spotting is common in the first months, followed by progressively lighter periods and sometimes no period at all. With a copper IUD, periods often become heavier and more crampy, at least initially. Both types can produce an adjustment window where the patterns you tracked before insertion no longer apply.

This is health data worth keeping accurate records of. The question of where those records live is not trivial. Contraceptive choices and reproductive health data are among the most sensitive categories of personal information. [Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa), and apps that store data on servers are subject to the same subpoenas, breaches, and data sales as any other commercial platform. Flo's 2021 FTC enforcement action documented that period tracking data was shared with Facebook and Google via embedded SDKs. Floriva's on-device architecture means that readable cycle data does not sit in a central server database to be accessed.

## Setting up Floriva after insertion

### The insertion date as your baseline

The most useful thing to do on insertion day is create a reference entry in Floriva. Log it as a symptom or note. Every bleeding episode, cramp entry, and cycle marker logged after that date can be measured against the insertion baseline. This matters when you are trying to assess whether you are still in the adjustment window.

### What to log differently with a hormonal IUD

Hormonal IUDs suppress the hormonal cycle that drives predictable menstruation. You may experience:

- Irregular spotting that does not correspond to a cycle start
- Lighter periods that are hard to distinguish from spotting
- Progressively less bleeding over 3-6 months
- No period at all after the adjustment period

Log each bleeding episode with a type (spotting or period) and intensity (light, medium, heavy). Do not conflate spotting with a cycle start unless the bleeding is clearly a period. Over time, the logged pattern will show whether spotting is decreasing, which is the expected trajectory for hormonal IUDs.

### What to log differently with a copper IUD

Copper IUDs do not suppress hormones, so your cycle will continue on its natural rhythm. What often changes is flow intensity and cramping:

- Heavier flow, particularly in the first 3-6 months
- More intense cramping around the period
- Longer period duration

Log flow intensity and cramping severity for every period. After a few cycles, the log shows whether the heavier patterns are stabilizing or continuing to worsen. If they worsen over time, that log is what a provider will want to see.

## Symptoms worth logging beyond bleeding

Cramping after insertion is normal and typically peaks in the first 24-48 hours. Log it with a date and severity. If cramping returns or increases significantly in the weeks after insertion, that is worth documenting.

Unusual symptoms (fever, significant pelvic pain, unusual discharge) are potential signs of complications. Log them with a timestamp. A dated log entry is far more useful in a clinical conversation than trying to recall when something started.

## Using your log at follow-up appointments

Most providers schedule a follow-up 4-6 weeks after IUD insertion to verify placement. After that, the standard check-in is annual unless something concerns you. Export your Floriva data before these appointments. A logged record of bleeding changes, cramping patterns, and any unusual symptoms gives your provider a complete picture of your adjustment without requiring you to reconstruct it from memory.

## Why the privacy architecture matters for contraceptive data

IUD tracking produces a specific kind of record: contraceptive method, insertion date, bleeding response, and ongoing reproductive status. This information is sensitive in ways that extend beyond personal preference. Since the Dobbs decision, data about contraceptive use and reproductive choices has drawn attention from legal observers who track how period app data could be used in enforcement contexts.

[Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa). That means contraceptive tracking data stored on an app's server is subject to commercial data practices and legal compulsion with fewer protections than medical records. Flo's 2021 FTC enforcement action and the 2025 class action settlement documented concretely that app-stored reproductive health data can reach parties the user never anticipated.

Floriva's on-device storage removes that server layer. Your IUD tracking log exists on your phone and is removed by uninstalling the app. No server backup, no retention window, no deletion request required.
