## Fertility Tracking Basics: What the Methods Do

Fertility awareness tracking uses biological signs to identify the fertile window in a menstrual cycle. Three primary signs are used:

**Basal body temperature (BBT):** Your resting temperature rises slightly after ovulation due to progesterone. Charting this rise confirms that ovulation has occurred. It cannot predict ovulation in advance, only confirm it after the fact. Over several cycles, the pattern of when your temperature shifts tells you when to expect ovulation.

**Cervical mucus:** Estrogen rising before ovulation changes cervical mucus from absent or sticky to clear and stretchy. This mucus is the biological medium sperm need to survive and reach the egg. The presence of egg-white mucus indicates the fertile window is open.

**Ovulation predictor kits:** OPKs detect the LH hormone surge that triggers ovulation, typically 12-36 hours before the egg is released. A positive OPK means ovulation is imminent. Combined with BBT confirmation, it gives you both a leading indicator (LH surge) and a trailing confirmation (temperature shift).

Used together, these three signs give a much more complete picture than calendar-based fertile window estimates, which assume ovulation occurs on a fixed day that often doesn't match reality.

## Why Fertility Data Needs Privacy Protections

Ovulation and fertility records describe your reproductive health and timing in detail. For people in states with reproductive rights restrictions, this data is particularly sensitive. [Period tracker data is not covered by HIPAA](/resources/guides/period-tracker-hipaa/), so it does not have the same protections as clinical records.

The FTC took action against [Flo](/compare/alternatives/flo-app-alternative/) in 2021 and found that the company shared reproductive health data with Facebook and Google. Fertility tracking data (ovulation timing, intercourse, temperature charts) is among the most sensitive data a period app can hold.

Floriva keeps core fertility data on your device. Floriva has no readable central fertility database. Device access is a separate legal path.

## Getting Accurate Results

The most common BBT tracking error is inconsistent timing. Temperature must be taken at the same time each morning, before getting up, after at least three hours of uninterrupted sleep. Illness, alcohol, and poor sleep all affect BBT and can obscure the thermal shift. Log these factors in Floriva's notes when they occur so you can discount those data points when reading your chart.

Cervical mucus observation takes practice. The most important distinction is between dry/sticky/creamy (non-fertile) and clear, stretchy, slippery mucus (fertile). Check at the same point each day and log immediately. Consistency matters more than precision in the early cycles.
