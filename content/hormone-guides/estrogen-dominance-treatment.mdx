Treating estrogen dominance without knowing which type you have is like treating a fever without knowing the cause. The approach for high-estrogen dominance (improving clearance) is different from the approach for low-progesterone relative dominance (supporting ovulation). Using the wrong approach can delay improvement by months.

The first step, before choosing a supplement or protocol, is getting enough data to understand which problem is primary.

## Establishing the Cause

A basic hormonal panel gives you the information you need to make targeted choices:

- **Day 3 FSH and estradiol:** baseline follicular estrogen levels and ovarian reserve indicator
- **Day 21 progesterone** (or 7 days post-confirmed ovulation): luteal phase adequacy
- **DHEA-S and free testosterone:** to assess androgen contribution
- **TSH and free T4:** thyroid function, which affects estrogen metabolism
- **Sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG):** how much estrogen is bioavailable vs. bound

If estradiol on Day 3 is elevated, the focus is on reducing the estrogen load. If estradiol is normal but Day 21 progesterone is low, the focus is on supporting progesterone production. If both are off, both require attention.

## If the Driver Is High Estrogen

### Support liver estrogen metabolism

The liver processes estrogens through three hydroxylation pathways. The 2-OH pathway produces inactive metabolites that are excreted. The 16-OH and 4-OH pathways produce more potent metabolites. Supporting 2-hydroxylation reduces the net estrogenic effect even if total estrogen production does not change.

**DIM (diindolylmethane):** 100 to 200 mg of stabilized DIM daily shifts estrogen metabolism toward 2-hydroxylation. Clinical evidence is limited to small studies, but the mechanism is well-established. At higher doses above 300 mg, DIM can have unpredictable effects. The typical supplement dose range is more targeted.

**Indole-3-carbinol** is the dietary precursor to DIM, found in cruciferous vegetables. Broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, and kale provide this compound. Cooking degrades some of it, so raw or lightly cooked cruciferous vegetables provide more. The supplement form converts to DIM in the stomach.

**Reduce alcohol.** Alcohol impairs hepatic estrogen metabolism through multiple mechanisms. Even moderate drinking measurably raises estrogen levels. For people with estrogen dominance, this is one of the highest-impact dietary changes.

### Improve estrogen excretion through the gut

Estrogens conjugated by the liver should be excreted through the gut. The enzyme beta-glucuronidase, produced by certain gut bacteria, deconjugates these estrogens in the intestine and allows them to be reabsorbed, increasing total estrogen load.

**Fiber,** particularly insoluble fiber from vegetables, legumes, and whole grains, feeds bacteria that do not produce beta-glucuronidase and supports bowel regularity. Adequate transit time reduces the window for estrogen reabsorption.

**Calcium D-glucarate** is a supplement that inhibits beta-glucuronidase activity. Some practitioners recommend it alongside DIM for high-estrogen presentations. The evidence base is limited but mechanistically sound.

### Reduce xenoestrogen exposure

Synthetic compounds that bind to estrogen receptors contribute to estrogenic load. The highest-impact reductions:

- Replace plastic food containers with glass or stainless steel
- Use fragrance-free or verified-clean personal care products
- Choose organic for the Dirty Dozen produce list

These changes are cumulative rather than individually dramatic. They matter most in the context of an overall high-estrogen picture.

## If the Driver Is Low Progesterone

See the companion piece on [how to increase progesterone naturally](/resources/hormone-guides/how-to-increase-progesterone-naturally) for the full breakdown. The summary: support ovulation quality through adequate caloric intake, reduced cortisol load, zinc and B6, and Vitex if appropriate. Medical progesterone supplementation (bioidentical) in the luteal phase is the intervention for clinically confirmed luteal phase defect.

## Combination Approaches

Most estrogen dominance presentations involve both elevated estrogen and insufficient progesterone. Running both protocols simultaneously is reasonable. The mechanisms do not interfere with each other.

The sequence that makes sense for most people:
1. Get a hormonal panel to establish baseline
2. Start with the highest-impact diet changes: reduce alcohol, increase cruciferous vegetables and fiber
3. Add targeted supplements based on which arm of the problem is more prominent (DIM for high estrogen, B6/zinc/Vitex for low progesterone)
4. Retest after 3 months

## When Natural Approaches Are Not Enough

If you have confirmed fibroids, endometriosis, or adenomyosis, natural approaches alone are unlikely to resolve the structural condition. They may slow progression and improve symptoms, but they do not eliminate estrogen-sensitive growths. Medical management may include bioidentical progesterone, GnRH agonists, or surgery.

If after 3 months of consistent implementation your symptoms are unchanged and hormonal testing still shows significant imbalance, see a gynecologist or reproductive endocrinologist. Natural approaches work for many people but not all. The goal is targeted management, not exhausting every supplement before getting appropriate medical care.
