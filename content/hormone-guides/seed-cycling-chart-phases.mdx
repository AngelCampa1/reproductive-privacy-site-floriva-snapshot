## The Seed Cycling Chart

| Phase | Days (typical 28-day cycle) | Seeds | Amount per day | Preparation |
|-------|----------------------------|-------|----------------|-------------|
| **Follicular** | Day 1 to ovulation (~Day 14) | Flaxseed | 1 tablespoon | Freshly ground |
| **Follicular** | Day 1 to ovulation (~Day 14) | Pumpkin seed | 1 tablespoon | Raw, unsalted |
| **Luteal** | Ovulation to end of cycle | Sesame seed | 1 tablespoon | Freshly ground or tahini |
| **Luteal** | Ovulation to end of cycle | Sunflower seed | 1 tablespoon | Raw, unsalted |

**Daily total:** 2 tablespoons per day (mixed or separate), per phase.

---

## Phase 1: Follicular Phase Seeds

### Flaxseed (1 tbsp/day, ground)

**What it contains:** Omega-3 ALA, fiber, lignans (secoisolariciresinol diglucoside/SDG)

**Why this phase:** Flaxseed lignans are converted in the gut to enterolignans, which can modulate estrogen receptor activity. In the follicular phase when estrogen is rising, the proposed effect is supporting favorable estrogen metabolism. Flaxseed is also high in fiber, which supports estrogen excretion through the gut.

**Preparation critical point:** Whole flaxseeds pass through the digestive tract largely intact. You must grind them. The hull prevents absorption of the active compounds. Grind fresh daily in a coffee grinder. Do not buy pre-ground flaxseed meal; it oxidizes quickly.

**Ideas:** Stir into oatmeal, blend into smoothies, mix into Greek yogurt, add to overnight oats.

### Pumpkin seed (1 tbsp/day, raw)

**What it contains:** Zinc (high), magnesium, iron, omega-6 fatty acids, protein

**Why this phase:** Zinc supports follicle development and is critical for the corpus luteum that forms at ovulation. The follicular phase is when the follicle is developing. Zinc availability during this period may support better corpus luteum function and thus better progesterone production after ovulation.

**Preparation note:** Buy raw, unsalted pumpkin seeds. Roasting degrades the fatty acids and some heat-sensitive compounds. Pepitas (hulled pumpkin seeds) are easier to eat than whole seeds.

**Ideas:** Add to salads, eat as a snack, blend into pesto, sprinkle on grain bowls.

---

## Phase 2: Luteal Phase Seeds

### Sesame seed (1 tbsp/day, ground or as tahini)

**What it contains:** Lignans (sesamin, sesamolin), calcium, iron, zinc

**Why this phase:** Sesame seed lignans have a slightly different receptor profile than flaxseed lignans and are proposed to support progesterone-phase hormonal balance. Sesame is also the highest calcium-containing plant seed, and adequate calcium intake is associated with reduced PMS severity.

**Preparation note:** Like flaxseed, sesame seeds are better absorbed when ground. Tahini (ground sesame paste) works well as an alternative. Use 1 tablespoon. Black sesame seeds have higher antioxidant content than white; either works.

**Ideas:** Tahini in smoothies, dressings, or eaten off a spoon. Ground sesame on rice bowls, mixed into yogurt.

### Sunflower seed (1 tbsp/day, raw)

**What it contains:** Vitamin E (high), selenium, magnesium, linoleic acid (omega-6)

**Why this phase:** Vitamin E is an antioxidant that may support corpus luteum function and has modest trial evidence for PMS symptom reduction. Selenium supports thyroid function, which affects progesterone metabolism.

**Preparation note:** Raw and unsalted. Roasted sunflower seeds are fine for snacking but high-heat roasting degrades vitamin E.

**Ideas:** Trail mix, added to granola, sprinkled on salads or soups.

## For Irregular Cycles

If your cycles are irregular, you cannot rely on Day 1 and Day 14 as phase markers. Options:

1. **Use LH strips:** Test daily starting around Day 8 to 10. The surge day is your switch point from follicular to luteal seeds.

2. **Use BBT charting:** A sustained temperature rise of 0.2 degrees C or more confirms ovulation occurred. Switch seeds the day the temperature rises.

3. **Use moon cycling as a proxy:** Conventionally, start follicular seeds at the new moon and switch at the full moon. There is no scientific basis for this. It is a rhythm placeholder for people who want a cycle structure without tracking.

4. **Use a fixed 14-day split:** Eat follicular seeds for the first half of your expected cycle length, then switch. Less accurate but still provides phase differentiation.

---

## How to Know If It's Working

Track these markers across 3 or more cycles:
- PMS symptom severity (especially mood, breast tenderness, bloating), looking for reduction
- Period heaviness and clotting, looking for reduction
- Cycle length, looking for regularization if cycles are irregular
- Luteal phase length, looking for lengthening if it was short

Most practitioners report that 3 months is the minimum evaluation period. Changes in cycle characteristics happen gradually as nutritional status improves and hormonal patterns shift.
